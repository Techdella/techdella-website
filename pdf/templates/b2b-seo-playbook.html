Who this is for

B2B companies selling services or software with a considered purchase: several people involved, a sales conversation, and a cycle of weeks or months. If most of your buyers search for what you sell before they talk to anyone, this plan applies.

What 90 days can and cannot do

In 90 days you can fix measurement and technical problems, publish or rebuild the pages closest to revenue, and start a content and authority engine. Rankings for competitive terms usually take longer to settle. The plan is built so the earliest work is the work most likely to pay back first.

How to read the plan

Phases overlap on purpose. Technical fixes continue while you write; links start once there are pages worth linking to. Each section ends with a checklist you can tick in a team meeting.

The principle

Why B2B SEO should be judged on pipeline

Traffic is easy to grow and easy to misread. A B2B site can double its visitors with top-of-funnel articles and see no change in sales. Judge SEO on the qualified leads and opportunities it produces.

That changes the order of work. Instead of starting with the highest-volume keywords, start with the searches people make when they are close to buying: the service name, comparisons, "alternatives to", pricing, and problems your product solves. Those pages have lower volume, but each visit is worth far more.

Before week 1

  • ☐ Name an owner for the plan and a weekly 30-minute review slot.
  • ☐ Agree the target: qualified leads or opportunities from organic search by day 90 and day 180.
  • ☐ Get access: CMS, Search Console, GA4, CRM, DNS and the hosting or developer contact.
  • ☐ Book subject-expert time: one hour a week with someone who does the work you sell. Their input is what makes content worth ranking.
  • ☐ Collect sales input: the ten questions prospects ask most, the competitors they mention and why deals are lost.

Resourcing guide

The plan assumes roughly one marketer at half time, a writer producing one to two substantial pieces a week, a designer for page layouts and a few days of developer time in the first month. Fewer resources means stretching the timeline, not skipping phases.

Days 1–14
Foundations

Measure before you change anything

If you cannot connect an organic visit to a lead and a deal, you will not know which pages to invest in. Fix this first, and record a baseline so you can show progress later.

  • ☐ Search Console is verified as a Domain property, plus URL-prefix properties for each subdomain you report on.
  • ☐ GA4 key events exist for every lead action (contact form, booking, demo, resource download) and have been tested.
  • ☐ Forms pass source data to the CRM: landing page, referrer, first-touch and last-touch UTM values.
  • ☐ The CRM has a lead source field with a fixed value for organic search, plus a free-text "How did you hear about us?".
  • ☐ Bing Webmaster Tools is verified; Bing also powers some AI assistants' web results.
  • ☐ A landing-page report shows organic sessions, leads and qualified leads per page.

Days 1–14
Technical

Technical health check

Crawl the site with a crawler of your choice and read Search Console's Pages report. Fix what blocks important pages first; leave cosmetic warnings for later.

Crawling and indexing

  • ☐ robots.txt does not block pages, CSS or JavaScript that important pages need.
  • ☐ Every important page is indexable: no stray noindex, returns 200, and is in the XML sitemap.
  • ☐ Sitemaps list only canonical, indexable URLs and are submitted in Search Console.
  • ☐ Each page has one self-referencing canonical; parameter variants (UTM, filters) point to the clean URL.
  • ☐ One version of the site resolves: HTTPS, one hostname (www or not), consistent trailing slashes, all others 301 in one hop.
  • ☐ No redirect chains or loops on internal links; internal links point at final URLs.
  • ☐ Broken internal links and 404s with traffic or backlinks are fixed or redirected to the closest relevant page.
  • ☐ "Crawled – currently not indexed" and "Duplicate without user-selected canonical" in Search Console are reviewed; thin or duplicate pages are merged or improved.
  • ☐ Staging and development sites are password-protected and not indexed.

Rendering and content access

  • ☐ Main content is in the initial HTML, not loaded only after user interaction. Check with URL Inspection's rendered HTML.
  • ☐ Navigation uses real links (<a href>) that crawlers can follow.
  • ☐ Important text is not inside images; images have descriptive alt text.
  • ☐ Pagination and filters do not create thousands of near-duplicate indexable URLs.
Days 1–14
Technical, continued

Speed, structure and markup

Page experience

  • ☐ Core Web Vitals pass for your main templates in Search Console (based on real-user data). Google's "good" thresholds: LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less.
  • ☐ Hero images are sized and compressed, served in modern formats, and not lazy-loaded.
  • ☐ Third-party scripts (chat, heatmaps, tag managers) are audited; anything unused is removed.
  • ☐ Mobile layout works: no horizontal scroll, readable text, tap targets large enough.

Site structure

  • ☐ Money pages are within two or three clicks of the homepage.
  • ☐ URLs are short and descriptive. Do not change URLs that already rank unless you have a reason and a redirect.
  • ☐ No orphan pages: every indexable page has at least one internal link.
  • ☐ Breadcrumbs on deeper pages, marked up with BreadcrumbList.

Structured data

  • ☐ Organization on the homepage with logo and official profiles (sameAs).
  • ☐ Article or BlogPosting on articles, with author and dates.
  • ☐ Markup matches what is visible on the page and validates in the Rich Results Test.
  • ☐ No markup for things Google no longer shows or does not allow, such as self-serving review stars for your own services.

Days 15–30
Research

Keyword research that starts at the bottom of the funnel

List the searches people make when they are choosing a provider or a product. Validate volume in your keyword tool and Search Console, but do not drop a buying keyword just because its volume is small.

Page types, in priority order

Page typeExample searchesIntent
Service / product"B2B SEO agency", "email marketing services", "[category] software"Choosing a provider
Industry / use case"SEO for fintech", "[category] for healthcare"Choosing, with context
Comparison and alternatives"[competitor] alternatives", "[you] vs [competitor]"Shortlisting
Pricing"[category] pricing", "how much does [service] cost"Budgeting
Problem-aware"why is our [metric] dropping", "how to fix [problem]"Researching a fix
Definitions and guides"what is [term]", "[topic] checklist"Learning

Where to find the keywords

  • ☐ Search Console: queries where you already get impressions in positions 4–20. These are the fastest wins.
  • ☐ Sales calls and CRM notes: the words prospects use to describe their problem.
  • ☐ Competitors' top pages in your keyword tool, filtered to commercial intent.
  • ☐ Google's results page itself: "People also ask", related searches, and the types of pages that rank (if every result is a list article, a service page will struggle).
  • ☐ AI assistants: ask them your buyers' questions and note which sources they cite.

Check intent before you write

Search the keyword and look at the top ten. If they are all guides, the searcher wants to learn, not to buy: answer with a guide that links to your service. If they are service pages from providers, a service page is the right format.

Template

The keyword map

One primary keyword per page and one page per primary keyword. If two pages target the same search, merge them or change one target.

Primary keywordSecondary keywordsIntentVolumeCurrent rankURL (existing or new)Action

Actions: keep (performing), refresh (improve existing page), create (new page), merge (combine competing pages, 301 the weaker one), prune (remove pages with no traffic, links or purpose).

Prioritize the map

Start with rows that combine buying intent, an existing page ranking in positions 4–15, and a clear link to something you sell. Then new money pages. Then supporting content.

Days 30–60
Money pages

On-page checklist

  • ☐ Title tag uses the primary keyword naturally, roughly 60 characters or fewer so it is less likely to be cut, brand at the end.
  • ☐ Meta description written for the click: what the page offers and why it is different. Not copied from the first sentence.
  • ☐ One H1 that matches the search and the title's promise.
  • ☐ The intro answers the query in two or three sentences.
  • ☐ H2s cover what the buyer needs to decide: problem, approach, proof, process, pricing or how to start, FAQ.
  • ☐ Proof is specific and sourced: named clients, numbers with timeframes, quotes with permission.
  • ☐ A clear primary CTA appears after the intro and again at the end.
  • ☐ At least three internal links to related services, case studies or guides, with descriptive anchor text.
  • ☐ Images have descriptive file names and alt text; diagrams are real HTML or SVG where possible.
  • ☐ Tables and lists are real HTML, not images.
  • ☐ FAQ answers real objections from sales calls, written as visible content.
  • ☐ Author and reviewer are named, with a "last reviewed" date.
  • ☐ Structured data fits the page type and matches visible content.
  • ☐ The page loads quickly on mobile and works without JavaScript for its main content.

Location and industry pages

Pages for each city or industry help only when each one carries real, specific substance: clients in that market, local channels and costs, industry rules. Pages that swap one word in the same template are what Google's spam policies call doorway pages.

Days 30–75
Content

Supporting content and the quality bar

Supporting articles answer the questions buyers ask before they are ready to talk, and pass relevance and links to your money pages. Fewer, better pieces beat a high-volume calendar.

Build clusters, not one-off posts

For each money page, plan four to eight supporting pieces that answer the questions around it: definitions, how-tos, comparisons, mistakes, checklists. Each piece links to the money page, and the money page links back to the most useful ones.

Money pageSupporting pieceKeywordFormat

The quality bar

Google's guidance asks whether content is written for people, shows first-hand experience and expertise, and would leave a reader satisfied. Its spam policies treat mass-produced pages made mainly to rank (by people or AI) as scaled content abuse. In practice, before anything is published:

  • ☐ It includes something original: our data, an example from our work, a screenshot, or a clear opinion.
  • ☐ A subject expert has reviewed it and is named.
  • ☐ Every fact and number has a source.
  • ☐ It answers the question better than the current top result, not just at more length.
  • ☐ If AI helped draft it, a person has rewritten, checked and added to it.
Days 30–75
Content

Production workflow and calendar

A simple, repeatable pipeline gets more good pieces published than a heroic effort. Every piece passes the same stages.

  1. Brief. Keyword, intent, angle, proof and internal links agreed before writing (use the page brief).
  2. Expert input. A 20-minute interview with the person who does the work. Record it.
  3. Draft. Answer first, then the detail. Mark anything unverified for checking.
  4. Expert review. Accuracy, missing nuance, examples.
  5. Edit and SEO check. Title, meta description, headings, links, images, structured data.
  6. Publish and link. Add links from at least two existing relevant pages.
  7. Refresh. Review money pages quarterly and guides every six months, or sooner when clicks drop.

Days 45–90
Internal links

Internal links

Internal links are the part of authority you fully control. They tell search engines which pages matter most and how topics relate.

Rules to apply sitewide

  • ☐ Every service page links to two or three related services, its industry pages and its case studies.
  • ☐ Every guide links to one service page and to the definitions it uses.
  • ☐ Every definition or glossary page links to one service and to related terms.
  • ☐ Case studies link to every service they used.
  • ☐ Anchor text describes the destination ("technical SEO audit"), not "click here".
  • ☐ Your strongest pages (most backlinks and traffic) link to the money pages you are pushing.
  • ☐ No orphans, and no important page more than three clicks deep.

Find quick wins

Search your own site for mentions of a money page's topic (site:yourdomain.com "topic") and add a link from each relevant mention. Then check your top-linked pages in Search Console's Links report and add links from them.

Days 45–90
Authority

Authority without buying links

Links from relevant, reputable sites still help pages rank. Buying links, or trading them at scale, breaks Google's spam policies and can lead to manual actions. Earn them with things worth citing.

TacticHow it worksEffort
Original dataPublish numbers from your own work or a survey of your customers (anonymized, with permission). Journalists and bloggers cite data.High, lasting
Expert commentaryAnswer journalist requests in your niche; offer a named expert for quotes on industry news.Medium, ongoing
Partners and integrationsGet listed on partner, integration and agency directory pages you genuinely belong on.Low
Customer storiesClients often link to case studies and launch announcements about work you did together.Low
Free tools and templatesUseful calculators, checkers and templates attract links over time.Medium
Podcasts and eventsGuest appearances usually come with a show-notes link and a relevant audience.Medium
Unlinked mentionsFind mentions of your brand without a link and ask politely for one.Low

Avoid

  • ☐ Paid links without rel="sponsored" (or nofollow).
  • ☐ Guest posts written mainly to place keyword-rich links.
  • ☐ Link exchanges and private blog networks.
  • ☐ Hosting third-party content on your domain that you do not edit (site reputation abuse).
Days 60–90
Conversion

Turn visits into conversations

Organic visitors arrive at different stages. Give each stage an obvious next step, and make the high-intent one as easy as possible.

Visitor stageTypical landing pageOffer
Ready to talkService, pricing, comparisonBook a call, request an audit or demo
EvaluatingCase studies, industry pagesTalk to someone who worked on a similar project; see more examples
LearningGuides, definitionsA checklist, template or tool; newsletter

Conversion checklist for your top 10 organic landing pages

  • ☐ A CTA is visible on the first screen on mobile.
  • ☐ The offer matches the page's intent (no "book a demo" as the only option on a beginner's guide).
  • ☐ Forms ask only what sales needs; optional fields are marked.
  • ☐ Proof sits near the CTA: a result, a logo used with permission, or a short quote.
  • ☐ The thank-you page sets expectations and offers the next step.
  • ☐ Pop-ups do not cover content for mobile visitors arriving from search.
  • ☐ Every form is tested monthly: submission reaches the CRM with source data.

Reading the scorecard

Impressions usually move before clicks, and clicks before leads. If impressions rise but clicks do not, work on titles and descriptions. If clicks rise but leads do not, work on intent match and conversion. If nothing moves after eight weeks of steady publishing, revisit the keyword map and the technical audit.

Search Console data has a delay of a few days; compare full weeks and keep the same date ranges.

Timing launches

Google announces core updates on its Search Status Dashboard. If you can, avoid launching a redesign or a large migration while one is rolling out, so you can tell your changes apart from the update's effects.
