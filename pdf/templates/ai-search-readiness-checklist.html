How to use this checklist

AI answer engines can only cite pages they are allowed to fetch, can read without running scripts, and can tie to a clear entity. Most of AI search readiness is good technical SEO plus content that answers questions directly.

Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond being indexed and eligible to show a snippet, and that normal SEO best practices apply. Other engines (ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Claude) run their own crawlers or rely on a search partner's index. So the work splits into two parts: let the right bots in, and make your pages the clearest answer on the topic.

Section 1

Crawler access

Check robots.txt, then check that your CDN or firewall is not blocking the same bots silently. A bot blocked at the firewall never sees your robots.txt.

  • ☐ Key pages return 200, not 403 or a challenge page, to non-browser clients.
  • ☐ Your XML sitemap is listed in robots.txt and submitted in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

Section 2

Indexing and rendering

Many AI crawlers do not run JavaScript the way Googlebot does. If your answer only appears after scripts run, assume some engines never see it.

  • ☐ The main text of each key page is in the initial HTML. Test: view source (not the inspector) or fetch the page with curl, and search for a sentence from the body.
  • ☐ Headings, lists and tables are real HTML (h2, ul, table), not styled divs or images of text.
  • ☐ FAQs and tabs are in the HTML even when collapsed. <details> works without scripts.
  • ☐ Each page has one self-referencing canonical URL, and parameter variants (UTM, ?ref=) point to it.
  • ☐ Key pages are indexed in Google and Bing. Check the Pages report in Search Console and Bing's Site Explorer.
  • ☐ nosnippet, max-snippet and data-nosnippet are used only on purpose. Google applies these controls to AI Overviews and AI Mode too; a nosnippet page cannot be quoted.
  • ☐ Important pages are not behind a login, a cookie wall or a form.
  • ☐ Staging and preview sites are blocked (password plus noindex), so engines do not cite the wrong copy.
  • ☐ No important pages are noindex by mistake (common after a theme or plugin change).
  • ☐ Pages load fast on mobile: Core Web Vitals pass in Search Console for your key templates.
  • ☐ Bing Webmaster Tools is verified, and IndexNow (or Bing's URL submission) is set up so updates reach Bing quickly.
  • ☐ PDFs and resources you want cited have an HTML page that summarizes them. Answer engines quote HTML pages more reliably than files.

Quick test

Run curl -sA "OAI-SearchBot" https://yoursite.com/your-key-page/ | grep -c "a sentence from your page". A result of 0 means the bot either got blocked or got a page without your content. Repeat with the other user agents you allow.

Section 3

Entity clarity

Answer engines describe companies by stitching together facts from many sources. Make those facts identical everywhere, and make it easy to connect your site, your people and your profiles.

  • ☐ One sentence says what you do, and it is the same on your homepage, About page, LinkedIn, and directory profiles. Example format: "[Company] is a [category] for [audience] that [outcome]."
  • ☐ Your company name is written the same way everywhere (capitalization, spacing, legal suffix).
  • ☐ Organization structured data on the homepage includes name, URL, logo and sameAs links to your official profiles.
  • ☐ Structured data matches the visible page and validates in the Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator.
  • ☐ An About page names the founders and team, where you work, who you serve and how to contact you.
  • ☐ Articles have named authors with an author page (role, experience, LinkedIn) and Person markup.
  • ☐ Service pages name the service plainly in the H1 and title, using the words buyers use.
  • ☐ Case studies name the client, the timeframe and the numbers, with permission. Specific, attributable facts are what engines can quote.
  • ☐ Third-party profiles are complete and current: LinkedIn company page, Google Business Profile if you have an office, and the review or directory sites your buyers use.
  • ☐ Contact details (name, address if public, phone, email) are consistent across the site and profiles.

Section 4

Answer-first content

Engines pull short passages that answer the question. Put the answer in the first two or three sentences, then support it with evidence that only you have.

  • ☐ Each of your 20 buyer questions maps to one page that answers it (from the table on page 2).
  • ☐ The answer comes first: 40 to 60 words directly under the H1 or the matching H2, before any background.
  • ☐ Question-shaped subheadings where they fit the reader ("How long does B2B SEO take?").
  • ☐ Definitions are self-contained: a sentence that still makes sense when quoted on its own.
  • ☐ Comparisons are in tables with clear row and column labels.
  • ☐ Steps are in numbered lists.
  • ☐ Every page has something first-hand: your numbers, a screenshot, what you did and what happened, or a clear opinion.
  • ☐ Claims link to their source, and statistics carry a date.
  • ☐ Pages show "last updated" or "last reviewed" dates that change only when the content does.
  • ☐ Thin and duplicate pages are merged or removed, so one strong page answers each question.
  • ☐ Comparison and "alternatives" pages are fair: they name where a competitor is the better fit.
  • ☐ Internal links connect each answer to the related service page and two or three related definitions.

About llms.txt

llms.txt is a proposed convention (llmstxt.org) for a plain-Markdown file at your site root that summarizes your site and links to key pages. It is cheap to add. Google says you don't need new machine-readable files or "AI text files" to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. The crawler documentation from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity does not say their bots use the file. Treat it as optional housekeeping, not a strategy.

  • ☐ Optional: /llms.txt exists, lists your services and key guides with URLs, and is generated from your CMS so it stays current.
Section 5

Measurement

There is no single "AI rankings" report. Combine what your analytics already sees with a fixed set of prompts you check by hand every month.

  • ☐ GA4 has a channel or segment for AI referrals: sessions with referrers such as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com, gemini.google.com and claude.ai.
  • ☐ Your CRM records "How did you hear about us?" with an option for "ChatGPT / AI assistant". Many AI-influenced buyers arrive by typing your URL, so referrers undercount.
  • ☐ Search Console is reviewed monthly for impressions and clicks on your buyer-question pages. Google includes AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic in the Performance report under the "Web" search type. It does not report it separately.
  • ☐ A fixed prompt set (your 20 questions) is run monthly in Google AI Mode, ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Copilot, logged in the table below.
  • ☐ You record which competitors and which of their pages are cited, not just whether you are.
  • ☐ Answers about your company are checked for accuracy ("What does [Company] do?", "Who founded [Company]?").
  • ☐ Bot visits are tracked from server logs monthly, by user agent.
  • ☐ Results are reviewed on a set date with an owner and next actions.
