How to use this worksheet

A growth audit answers one question: where is the biggest gap between what your pipeline needs and what your marketing produces today? Work through the six parts in order. Write in numbers wherever you can, and leave a cell blank rather than guess.

  1. Goals and constraints. What the business needs from marketing in the next 12 months.
  2. Funnel numbers. Where leads and deals come from today, by channel.
  3. Channel scorecard. Six channels rated against the same questions: search, AI search, paid, lead generation, email, automation.
  4. Website and conversion. Whether visitors who are ready to talk can do so easily.
  5. Tracking and data. Whether you can trust the numbers in parts 2 and 3.
  6. Prioritize. Score your ideas and commit to three for the next 90 days.

Two rules that make the audit useful

Use pipeline, not traffic, as the scoreboard. A channel that sends fewer visitors but more qualified opportunities wins.

Bring sales. Your sales team knows which leads closed and why. Marketing data alone hides that.

Reality check

Work backward from your target: target revenue ÷ average deal size = deals needed. Deals needed ÷ your opportunity win rate = opportunities needed. Keep going up the funnel. Compare the leads you need with the leads you get. That gap is the size of the problem this audit has to solve.

Part 4

Website and conversion

Open your homepage and your three highest-traffic service pages on a phone. Work through the checks as a buyer would.

  • ☐ The homepage headline says what you do and for whom in plain words, above the fold on mobile.
  • ☐ There is one primary call to action per page, and it is the same action sales wants (book a call, request an audit, start a trial).
  • ☐ Each service has its own page with the problem, the approach, proof, process, FAQ and a call to action.
  • ☐ Proof is specific: named clients, numbers with timeframes, verbatim quotes with permission.
  • ☐ Contact and booking forms ask only what sales needs to qualify the lead.
  • ☐ After submitting, people see a clear next step (a booking link, what happens next, when).
  • ☐ Pricing or "ways to work with us" is visible, even as a range or a starting point.
  • ☐ Pages load quickly on mobile: Core Web Vitals pass in Search Console.
  • ☐ There is an offer for people not ready to talk: a guide, a tool, a newsletter.
  • ☐ You know your top exit pages and have looked at why people leave them.

Part 5

Tracking and data

If you cannot trust the numbers in Part 2, fixing tracking comes before any channel work. These checks take about 30 minutes.

  • ☐ GA4 records key events for every form submission and booking, and you have tested each one this quarter.
  • ☐ Search Console is verified for every domain and subdomain you use.
  • ☐ Every form passes source data to the CRM: UTM parameters, landing page and referrer, first and last touch.
  • ☐ The CRM has a required "lead source" field with a fixed list of values, plus "How did you hear about us?" in the person's own words.
  • ☐ Lead stages are defined in writing (what makes a lead qualified, what makes an opportunity) and sales and marketing agree.
  • ☐ Ad platforms receive conversion data for qualified leads or opportunities, not only form fills.
  • ☐ Consent is handled: a cookie banner where the law requires one, and Google Consent Mode set up if you advertise in the EEA or UK.
  • ☐ All campaign links use one UTM naming convention, written down where the team can find it.
  • ☐ One dashboard shows leads, qualified leads and opportunities by source each month, without manual spreadsheet work.
