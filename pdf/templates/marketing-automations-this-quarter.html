Before you automate anything

Automation repeats whatever process you give it, at speed. If lead stages are vague or form data is messy, automation spreads the mess. Get these four things right first.

  • ☐ One CRM is the source of truth for contacts, companies and deals. Other tools read from it and write back to it, not to their own lists.
  • ☐ Lifecycle stages are written down: what makes someone a subscriber, a lead, a marketing-qualified lead (MQL), a sales-qualified lead (SQL), an opportunity and a customer.
  • ☐ Every form sends source data: page URL, referrer, UTM parameters (first and last touch) and consent.
  • ☐ Someone owns automation: they keep a list of every live workflow, what it does and who to ask about it.

Where AI fits

AI is useful inside several of these automations: summarizing a form message for the sales alert, classifying inquiries by intent, drafting (not sending) a first reply, or turning call notes into CRM fields. Keep a person in the loop for anything a customer sees, log what the model produced, and do not send personal data to a model provider without a data-processing agreement in place.

Automation 01

Form to CRM with source capture

Every form submission creates or updates a contact in the CRM, with where the person came from attached to the record.

Trigger
Any form submitted on your website, landing pages or tools.
Steps
  1. Store first-touch and last-touch UTM values in a first-party cookie when the visitor lands.
  2. Pass them, plus page URL and referrer, as hidden fields with the form.
  3. Create or update the contact, matched on email address.
  4. Write the source fields only if they are empty (first touch), and always update last touch.
  5. Record the consent checkbox value and timestamp.
Data you need
A UTM naming convention; CRM properties for first and last source, medium, campaign, landing page.
Measure
Share of new contacts with a known source. Aim for nearly all of them.
Watch out for
Ad blockers and consent choices that strip cookies; forms embedded from third-party tools that drop hidden fields; overwriting first touch on every visit.

Automation 02

Instant lead routing and alert

High-intent inquiries (contact, demo, audit requests) reach the right person within minutes, with the context they need to reply well.

Trigger
A high-intent form is submitted.
Steps
  1. Assign an owner using clear rules (territory, company size, service line, or round-robin).
  2. Post an alert to the owner in Slack or Teams: name, company, message, source, pages viewed.
  3. Create a "reply" task due within your target response time.
  4. If the task is still open when the target passes, alert the owner's manager.
Measure
Median time from submission to first human reply, reviewed weekly.
Watch out for
Routing rules nobody updates when the team changes; alerts in a busy channel nobody watches.

Automation 03

Deduplication and enrichment

Each person and company exists once in the CRM, with the firmographic fields your routing and scoring rely on.

Trigger
A contact or company is created; plus a weekly batch for existing records.
Steps
  1. Match on email for people and on website domain for companies.
  2. Associate the contact with its company by email domain (skip free-mail domains such as gmail.com).
  3. Fill missing fields from an enrichment source: industry, employee range, country.
  4. Flag likely duplicates for a person to merge rather than merging automatically.
Measure
Duplicate rate; share of qualified leads with industry and company size filled.
Watch out for
Enrichment providers' terms and data-protection rules in your markets; overwriting data a salesperson entered by hand.

Automation 04

Lead-magnet delivery and nurture

Someone downloads a guide; they get it immediately, then a short sequence that helps them use it and offers a next step.

Trigger
Resource form submitted.
Steps
  1. Show the download link on the thank-you page and send it by email.
  2. Tag the contact with the resource (for example lm-ai-search-checklist).
  3. If they gave marketing consent: send three emails over about two weeks. One practical tip from the guide, one example or case study, one invitation to talk.
  4. Stop the sequence when they book a meeting or reply.
Measure
Download-to-meeting rate per resource; replies; unsubscribes.
Watch out for
Sending nurture without consent where the law requires it; generic emails that ignore which guide they downloaded.

Automation 05

Meeting booked: reminders and no-show follow-up

Fewer missed calls, and a clear path back when someone does miss one.

Trigger
A meeting is booked through your scheduling tool.
Steps
  1. Move the contact or deal to the "Meeting booked" stage.
  2. Send a confirmation with the agenda and one question to think about.
  3. Send reminders a day before and an hour before.
  4. If the meeting is marked no-show, send a friendly rebooking email the same day, and create a call task for the owner.
  5. After a held meeting, create a follow-up task with a due date.
Measure
Show rate; share of no-shows that rebook.
Watch out for
Reminders that keep sending after a meeting is cancelled or rescheduled.

Automation 06

Lead scoring and MQL handoff

Sales sees the leads most likely to buy first, and marketing keeps nurturing the rest.

Trigger
Changes to contact properties or new activity (page views, email clicks, form fills).
Steps
  1. Score fit (industry, size, role, market) and intent (pricing page visits, repeat visits, high-intent forms) separately.
  2. When both pass your thresholds, set the stage to MQL and route as in automation 02.
  3. Sales accepts (SQL) or rejects with a reason from a fixed list.
  4. Review rejected reasons monthly and adjust the scoring.
Measure
MQL-to-SQL acceptance rate; opportunities created from MQLs.
Watch out for
Scores built on guesses: base the first version on what your last 20 to 50 won deals had in common, and keep it simple.

Automation 07

Stalled-deal nudges

Deals that have gone quiet get attention before they go cold.

Trigger
An open deal has had no logged activity for a set number of days for its stage (for example 14 days).
Steps
  1. Create a task for the owner with the last activity and next step from the deal.
  2. Optional: enroll the contact in a short, personal-sounding check-in sequence sent from the owner's mailbox, which stops on any reply.
  3. If still quiet after a second period, flag the deal in the weekly pipeline report.
Measure
Deals reactivated; average days in stage.
Watch out for
Automated emails to contacts in active negotiation. Let owners opt a deal out.

Automation 08

New-customer onboarding

Everything sales learned reaches the delivery team, and the customer knows what happens next.

Trigger
Deal moved to "Won".
Steps
  1. Create the project or onboarding record from a template, with tasks and due dates.
  2. Copy key deal fields (scope, goals, contacts, start date) to the project.
  3. Send the welcome email: who their contacts are, the first three steps, and what you need from them.
  4. Post the win to the team channel with the deal summary.
  5. Schedule check-ins at set points, such as day 7 and day 30.
Measure
Time from signed to kickoff; onboarding tasks completed on time.
Watch out for
Welcome emails that go out before the contract or invoice is actually signed.

Automation 09

Re-engagement and sunset

Inactive subscribers either come back or leave your list, which protects your sender reputation.

Trigger
No email clicks or replies, and no site visits, for a set period (for example 6 months).
Steps
  1. Send one or two emails asking whether they still want to hear from you, with a clear "keep me subscribed" button.
  2. If they click, reset their engagement date.
  3. If not, suppress them from marketing email. Keep the record for sales and customer history.
Measure
Share of the list that is engaged; spam-complaint rate in Google Postmaster Tools.
Watch out for
Using opens alone as the activity signal: privacy features in some mail apps report opens that did not happen.

Automation 10

Weekly pipeline report to chat

The team sees the numbers that matter every Monday without anyone building a slide.

Trigger
Schedule: every Monday morning.
Steps
  1. Pull last week's leads, MQLs, SQLs, opportunities created and deals won, by source.
  2. Compare with the previous four-week average.
  3. Post a short summary to the team channel with a link to the full dashboard.
  4. Optional: use AI to write a two-sentence summary of what changed, clearly labelled as generated.
Measure
Whether people read it: ask in the first month, and cut what nobody uses.
Watch out for
Reports nobody acts on. End each report with one question for the team.
