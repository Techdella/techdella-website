How to use this template

A good brief makes the creative team's first draft close to right. It names one audience, one problem, one promise and the proof behind it, then sets how you will judge the result.

  1. Fill in the one-page brief (page 3) before anyone opens a design tool.
  2. Write the message (page 4): the problem, the promise, the proof and the objection, in your buyer's words.
  3. Pick hooks to test (page 5). Creative is now the main lever in most ad platforms, because targeting is increasingly automated.
  4. Set the test plan and naming (page 6), so results are readable later.
  5. Check the specs for each platform (pages 7 and 8) and run the pre-launch checklist (page 9).

Labelling example creative

If you show ads made for illustration, such as samples in a pitch, label them "Example". Only show a client's real ads with their permission.

Step 1

The one-page brief

Keep each answer to one or two sentences. If you cannot, the campaign is trying to do too much.

Who exactly is this for?
Role, company type, size, market
What are they trying to get done?
The job, in their words
What is getting in their way?
The problem this ad names
What do we offer?
The specific offer in this ad: a call, an audit, a guide, a trial
Why believe us?
Named proof only: case study, number with timeframe, verbatim quote with permission
What will they doubt?
The main objection, and how the ad or landing page answers it
What should they do next?
One call to action
Mandatories
Logo use, legal lines, words to avoid
How we will judge it
The metric that decides the winner, and after how much spend or how many days

A worked example

This uses TechDella's own published Paycape case study as the proof line.

Problem"Your pipeline depends on outbound, and every month starts from zero."
Promise"Get found by buyers who are already searching for what you sell."
Proof"Paycape reached about 15,000 monthly organic visitors, with 50% of conversions from inbound, in a 5-month engagement." (Source: published case study. Re-confirm with the client before use in ads.)
Call to action"Request a growth audit."

Proof rules

  • ☐ Every number has a source and a timeframe, and the client has approved its use in ads.
  • ☐ Quotes are verbatim and attributed, with permission.
  • ☐ No implied guarantees ("double your leads") unless you can substantiate them; platforms and regulators treat these as misleading claims.
  • ☐ Logos of clients appear only with written permission.

Video checklist

  • ☐ The point lands in the first three seconds, with sound off.
  • ☐ Burned-in captions or on-screen text, placed inside each platform's safe zone.
  • ☐ Vertical 9:16 master; square and 4:5 cut-downs where the platform uses them.
  • ☐ The logo or brand name appears early, without a long intro.
Step 4

Test plan and naming

Change one thing per test, decide the winner on a metric close to revenue, and name every ad so the result is readable in six months.

  1. Hooks first. Same body and visual, two to four different openings.
  2. Then the concept. Take the winning hook and test a different visual approach (screen recording vs. person to camera vs. static).
  3. Then the offer. Call vs. audit vs. guide. This often moves cost per qualified lead the most.
  4. Then the landing page. Only once the ad is stable.

Pick the deciding metric before launch

Click-through rate tells you about the hook. Cost per lead tells you about the offer. For B2B, judge winners on cost per qualified lead or per booked meeting, which means sending CRM outcomes back to the ad platform (offline conversions or a conversions API).

[platform]_[objective]_[audience]_[concept]_[hook]_[format]_[version]

Before launch

Pre-launch checklist

  • ☐ Brief approved by the owner named on page 2.
  • ☐ Every claim is sourced and every client asset is used with permission.
  • ☐ Each ad matches its landing page: same offer, same words, same visual.
  • ☐ Landing page loads fast on mobile and the form works (test submit reaches the CRM).
  • ☐ Tracking is live: platform pixel or tag and the conversions API or offline conversion import, firing only after consent where required.
  • ☐ UTM parameters on every destination URL, following your naming convention.
  • ☐ Ad names follow the convention on page 6.
  • ☐ Specs checked for every placement; text inside safe zones; captions on.
  • ☐ Special categories declared where required (Meta special ad categories, Google financial services verification).
  • ☐ Exclusions set: current customers, employees, and open opportunities where appropriate.
  • ☐ Budget and end dates set; a person checks spend on day 1 and day 3.
  • ☐ Example creative is labelled "Example" wherever it is shown outside the ad account.
