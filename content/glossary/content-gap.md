A content gap is a question your buyers ask that your website doesn't answer, or answers worse than the competition. A content gap analysis is the structured way to find those questions and decide which ones are worth a page. It's one of the most practical ways to plan content, because it starts from demand that already exists.

:::takeaways
- Gaps come from four places: competitors, your own Search Console data, your sales conversations and the buying journey.
- Not every gap deserves a page. Score each one on business value and effort.
- Sometimes the fix is improving an existing page, not writing a new one.
- The best gaps are often missing from tools entirely and show up only in sales calls.
:::

## Four types of content gap

| Gap type | What it looks like | How to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword gap | Competitors rank for queries you don't | Keyword gap tools in Ahrefs or Semrush |
| Quality gap | You have a page, but it's thinner or older than the top results | Compare your page with the top three results |
| Funnel gap | Plenty of awareness content, nothing for comparison or pricing questions | Map pages to buying stages |
| Format gap | Results favor tools, templates or videos, and you only have text | Look at what ranks and what people share |

Funnel gaps are common on B2B sites. Often the blog explains the basics well, but nothing helps a buyer compare options or justify the purchase. Mapping content to your [sales funnel](/glossary/sales-funnel/) exposes these quickly.

## Why content gap analysis matters

It replaces guesswork with evidence. Instead of a brainstorm, you get a list of questions real people ask, ranked by how much each answer would help your business. It also helps you avoid writing yet another version of a topic you already cover, which leads to [keyword cannibalization](/glossary/keyword-cannibalization/).

:::myth-fact
Myth: If a competitor has a page on it, you need one too.
Fact: Copying a competitor's topic list produces the same content everyone else has. Add a gap only if it fits your buyers and you can say something more useful.
Myth: Gap analysis is just a keyword tool export.
Fact: Tools miss new topics, low-volume B2B questions and the objections buyers raise in calls. Those often convert best.
:::

Some of the best gaps never show up in a keyword tool. I ask clients to forward the questions prospects raise on sales calls and in emails, then check whether the website answers them. Usually it doesn't. Those pages may have tiny search volume, but they're the ones a buyer reads right before deciding, so I'd prioritize them over a broad topic everyone already covers.

## How to run a content gap analysis

:::steps "A gap analysis for a small team"
1. Pick three real competitors | Choose the sites that rank for your target keywords, not only your business competitors.
2. Run a keyword gap report | Enter your domain and theirs in a keyword gap tool and filter for keywords where at least two of them rank and you don't.
3. Mine your own data | In Search Console, find queries where you get impressions but rank below position 10. These are partial gaps.
4. Listen to sales and support | List the ten questions prospects ask most often before buying. Check whether each has a clear page.
5. Map to the funnel | Tag each gap as awareness, consideration or decision. Look for stages with nothing.
6. Score and schedule | Rate business value and effort, then plan the top items into your calendar.
:::

### A simple scoring grid

Rate each gap from 1 to 3 on business value and on effort (where 3 is easiest), then multiply. Illustrative example:

| Gap | Value (1–3) | Ease (1–3) | Score | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "[Your product] vs [competitor]" page | 3 | 3 | 9 | Write this month |
| Pricing explainer | 3 | 2 | 6 | Write next month |
| Industry glossary term | 1 | 3 | 3 | Only if it supports a cluster |
| Annual industry survey | 2 | 1 | 2 | Plan for later |

Group the winners into [topic clusters](/glossary/topic-clusters/) so each new page strengthens an existing pillar rather than standing alone.

## Closing gaps without creating thin content

Google's guidance on [helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether content is made mainly to attract search visits rather than to help people. A gap list can tempt teams to publish fast and thin. Close fewer gaps, better: each new page should include something specific, such as an example, a number from your experience, or a clear recommendation.

:::faq
### What is the difference between a content gap and a keyword gap?
A keyword gap is one type of content gap: specific queries competitors rank for and you don't. A content gap is broader. It also includes thin pages, missing funnel stages and missing formats, such as a calculator or template that searchers clearly prefer.
### How often should I do a content gap analysis?
A full analysis once or twice a year is enough for most B2B sites, with a lighter quarterly check of Search Console for new queries. Run one any time you launch a new service, enter a new market or see a competitor pull ahead.
### Can I do a content gap analysis for free?
Yes, partly. Search Console shows your own partial gaps, Google's results and "People also ask" show related questions, and your sales team knows the rest. Paid tools make competitor keyword comparisons much faster.
### Should I fill every content gap I find?
No. Fill gaps that match what you sell and who you sell to, and where you can offer a better answer. A shorter list of strong pages beats a long list of thin ones.
:::
