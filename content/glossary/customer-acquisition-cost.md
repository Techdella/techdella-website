Customer acquisition cost (CAC) tells you how much it costs to win one new customer. Add up what you spent on sales and marketing in a period, divide by the number of new customers you won in that period, and you have your CAC. On its own it's just a number. Compared with what a customer is worth, it tells you whether your growth is profitable.

:::takeaways
- CAC = total sales and marketing cost ÷ new customers won, for the same period.
- Include people and tools, not just ad spend, or CAC will look better than it is.
- Track blended CAC (all channels) and channel CAC (for example, paid search only) separately.
- Judge CAC against customer lifetime value and payback period, not in isolation.
:::

## The CAC formula

**CAC = (sales costs + marketing costs) ÷ new customers acquired**

### What to include

| Include | Often forgotten |
|---|---|
| Ad spend on every platform | Salaries and commissions for sales and marketing staff (or the share of time spent) |
| Agency and freelancer fees | Sales and marketing software: CRM, email, analytics |
| Content, design and video production | Events, travel and sponsorships |
| | Free trials or onboarding costs that exist only to win the sale |

Leaving out salaries and tools is the most common way teams flatter their CAC. If you want a "media-only" number for optimizing ads, call it that and keep it separate.

## Worked examples

**Blended CAC.** An illustrative quarter: $18,000 ad spend, $12,000 agency fees, $24,000 share of salaries, $3,000 tools. Total: $57,000. New customers: 19. CAC = $57,000 ÷ 19 = $3,000.

**Channel CAC.** In the same quarter, paid search cost $9,000 in spend and fees and produced 6 customers. Paid search CAC = $1,500. Channel CAC needs a consistent attribution rule; see [marketing attribution](/glossary/marketing-attribution/).

**Payback period.** If each customer pays $500 a month with a 70% gross margin, they contribute $350 a month. Payback = $3,000 ÷ $350 ≈ 8.6 months.

## Why CAC matters

CAC is the cost side of growth. Combined with [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/) (CLV), it tells you how much you can afford to spend to win a customer, and which channels deserve more budget.

:::stats "Widely used SaaS guidelines" highlight=0
3x+ | lifetime value to CAC ratio seen in the best SaaS businesses | David Skok, For Entrepreneurs, SaaS Metrics 2.0 | https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/
5–7 months | time in which many of the best SaaS businesses recover CAC | David Skok, For Entrepreneurs, SaaS Metrics 2.0 | https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/
:::

Skok presents these as guidelines, not laws, and they come from SaaS. Service businesses with large upfront projects can work with different ratios. Use them as a starting point.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A lower CAC is always better.
Fact: A very low CAC can mean you're under-investing and missing growth. What matters is CAC relative to customer value and how fast you recover it.
Myth: CAC is a marketing metric.
Fact: It covers sales and marketing together. Long sales cycles, discounting and slow follow-up raise CAC as much as expensive ads do.
:::

## How to lower customer acquisition cost

:::steps "Five levers, in the order we'd check them"
1. Fix conversion first | Improving landing pages and forms lowers cost per lead on every channel at once. See [conversion rate optimization](/glossary/conversion-rate-optimization/).
2. Tighten targeting | Cut keywords, audiences and placements that bring leads who never buy. Measure to customers, not leads.
3. Shorten the sales cycle | Respond to inbound leads quickly and give sales the proof and pricing buyers ask for.
4. Invest in compounding channels | SEO, referrals and email to existing contacts cost less per customer over time than paid channels alone.
5. Retain and expand | Customers who stay and buy more raise CLV, which lets you afford a higher CAC where it's worth it.
:::

:::faq
### What is a good customer acquisition cost?
There's no universal number. A good CAC is one you recover quickly and that's well below customer lifetime value. Skok's SaaS guideline is an LTV to CAC ratio above 3 and payback within about a year at most. Compare your CAC with your own margins, not other industries.
### What is the difference between CAC and cost per lead?
Cost per lead divides spend by leads. CAC divides total sales and marketing cost by paying customers. A channel can have a cheap cost per lead and an expensive CAC if those leads rarely buy, which is why CAC is the better decision metric.
### How often should I calculate CAC?
Monthly for tracking and quarterly for decisions, because B2B sales cycles make monthly numbers noisy. Match the period of costs with the period customers closed, or use a lag equal to your typical sales cycle.
### Should CAC include existing customer upsells?
No. CAC measures the cost of winning new customers. Track expansion revenue separately, and include it in customer lifetime value instead.
:::
