A landing page is a page built to do one job. Someone clicks an ad, an email or a social post, lands on the page, and is asked to take one action: book a call, download a guide, start a trial. Unlike a homepage, which serves every visitor and links everywhere, a landing page keeps the visitor focused on that single next step.

:::takeaways
- One page, one audience, one goal, one call to action.
- Message match matters: the headline should repeat the promise from the ad or email that brought the visitor.
- Unbounce's 2024 data puts the median landing page conversion rate at 6.6% across industries.
- Build a separate landing page for each distinct offer or audience, not one page for every campaign.
:::

## Landing page vs homepage

| | Homepage | Landing page |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Everyone | One segment from one source |
| Goal | Orient and route visitors | One specific action |
| Navigation | Full menu | Minimal or none |
| Copy | Broad brand story | One offer, one promise |
| Measured by | Engagement, routing to key pages | Conversion rate on the goal |

Sending paid traffic to a homepage usually wastes money. The visitor has to work out where to go, and many leave instead.

## Types of landing page

- **Lead capture:** a form in exchange for something useful, such as a guide, checklist or audit.
- **Booking page:** for a sales call or demo, often with a calendar embed.
- **Click-through page:** explains an offer, then sends the visitor to a sign-up or checkout.
- **Webinar or event page:** registration for a specific date.
- **Product or service page built for ads:** a tighter version of a service page that matches one ad group's keywords.

## Why landing pages matter

They're where your ad and email spend either pays off or doesn't. A small improvement on a landing page multiplies across every channel that sends traffic to it. In paid search, Google's Quality Score also assesses landing page experience, so a relevant, useful page supports your ads too.

:::stats "Landing page benchmarks" highlight=0
6.6% | median landing page conversion rate across all industries | Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report, 2024 | https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/
3.8% to 12.3% | range of median conversion rates across the nine industries Unbounce analyzed | Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report, 2024 | https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/
:::

Medians hide a lot. A demo-request page for a $20,000 service will convert lower than a free checklist download, and that's fine. Benchmark against your own previous versions first.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Shorter landing pages always convert better.
Fact: Length should match the decision. A free download needs little explanation. A high-value service needs proof, detail and answers to objections before someone books a call.
Myth: One landing page can serve all your campaigns.
Fact: Different audiences and offers need different messages. A page that matches the ad's promise usually beats a general one.
:::

## How to build a landing page that converts

:::steps "Section by section"
1. Headline that matches the source | Repeat the promise from the ad or email in plain words. The visitor should know they're in the right place within seconds.
2. A clear subheading | Say who it's for and what they get.
3. One call to action | A specific button such as "Get the checklist" or "Book a 20-minute call". Repeat it lower on the page, but don't add competing goals.
4. Proof near the action | Real client logos, a verbatim testimonial or a specific result, only with permission.
5. Answer the objections | Price range, timeline, what happens after they submit, and a short FAQ.
6. A short form | Ask only for what you need now. Every extra field needs a reason.
7. Fast on mobile | Most ad clicks happen on phones, so test load speed and form usability there.
:::

The wording of the button matters more than its color. See [call to action (CTA)](/glossary/call-to-action-cta/) for how to write it. Once the page is live, improve it with [conversion rate optimization](/glossary/conversion-rate-optimization/) and controlled [A/B testing](/glossary/a-b-testing/).

:::faq
### What is a good landing page conversion rate?
Unbounce's 2024 benchmark puts the cross-industry median at 6.6%, with industry medians from 3.8% to 12.3%. Your own baseline matters more. Compare each version against your previous one, and against pages with similar offers and traffic sources.
### Should a landing page have navigation?
Usually not, or very little. Removing the main menu keeps attention on the goal. Keep a logo, a link to your privacy policy and any legal links. For pages that also need to rank in search, light navigation can help readers who want more context.
### Do I need a different landing page for every ad?
Not for every ad, but for every distinct audience or offer. Ads in the same ad group with the same promise can share a page. When the message, audience or offer changes, the page should change too.
### Can landing pages rank in Google?
They can, if they're indexable and useful. Many campaign pages are thin or duplicate existing service pages, so teams set them to noindex. If a landing page targets a search query, give it enough substance to stand on its own.
:::
