Organic social media is the unpaid side of social: the posts, videos, comments, replies and conversations you share on platforms like LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and X without paying to boost them. People see it because they follow you, because someone shared it, or because the platform's feed decided it was worth showing. Organic social builds trust and relationships. It rarely builds reach on its own.

:::takeaways
- Organic social earns trust and conversation. Paid social buys reach and targeting. Most brands need both.
- In B2B, people outperform logos: posts from founders and staff usually travel further than brand-page posts.
- Measure conversations and pipeline influence, not follower counts.
:::

## Why organic social media still matters

Your buyers are there. The audience is huge and spread across many platforms.

Organic posts are where buyers check whether you're real, active and worth talking to. A prospect who clicks your ad often visits your profile next. An empty or stale page costs you that deal.

## Organic vs paid social

| | Organic | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Time and skill | Media budget plus time |
| Reach | Limited to followers and algorithm picks | As wide as your budget allows |
| Targeting | Hashtags, topics, timing | Job title, company, interests, retargeting |
| Best for | Trust, community, proof, feedback | Reach, launches, lead capture |
| Shelf life | Hours to days | As long as you fund it |

When an organic post performs well, that's a signal it may work as an ad. Many teams boost proven organic posts on [paid social](/services/paid-ads/) rather than writing ads from scratch.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Organic social is dead because reach keeps falling.
Fact: Brand-page reach is limited, but people-led posts, comments and short video still reach new audiences. Organic social also shapes what buyers see when they check you out.
:::

## How to grow organic social media

:::steps "A weekly routine that compounds"
1. Pick one or two platforms | Go where your buyers already talk shop. For most B2B teams that's LinkedIn plus one video platform.
2. Post from people, not just the page | Founders and specialists share lessons, opinions and behind-the-scenes work. The brand page reshares.
3. Use a simple content mix | Teach (how-tos), show (work and results with permission), and share a view (opinions you can defend).
4. Spend as long commenting as posting | Thoughtful comments on buyers' and peers' posts often bring more profile visits than your own posts.
5. Use native formats | Short video, carousels or document posts, polls. Tag sparingly; our [hashtag generator](/tools/free-hashtag-generator/) helps pick relevant ones.
6. Review monthly | Note which topics start conversations and which bring profile visits or DMs from your target audience.
:::

## What to measure

Skip vanity metrics. Track comments from your target audience, profile visits, DMs, clicks to your site, and "how did you hear about us?" answers that name social. These show whether organic social is feeding your pipeline. :::faq
### What is an example of organic social media?
A founder's LinkedIn post about a lesson from a client project, a short video showing how your product works, or a reply thread answering a customer's question. None of them are paid to appear.
### How often should a business post organically?
Consistency matters more than volume. Two to four good posts a week on one platform beats daily posts nobody engages with.
### Is organic social media free?
There's no media cost, but it takes time from skilled people. Treat that time as a real budget line.
### Should I focus on organic or paid social first?
Start organic so your profiles look active and you learn what resonates. Add paid once you have proven posts and a clear offer to promote.
:::
