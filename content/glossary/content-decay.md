Content decay is what happens when a page that once brought steady traffic starts to slide. Clicks drop month after month, often so slowly that nobody notices until a quarterly report. The page didn't break. The world around it moved: facts aged, competitors improved, and the search results page itself changed.

:::takeaways
- Decay is normal. Every page needs a review date, and money pages need one most.
- Diagnose before you rewrite: lower rankings, fewer clicks at the same rank, and changed intent need different fixes.
- A real refresh changes the substance. Changing the date alone is something Google's guidance explicitly warns against.
- Some decaying pages should be merged or retired, not refreshed.
:::

## What causes content decay

| Cause | What you'll see | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Outdated facts | Old years, prices, screenshots or tool names | Update facts, examples and sources |
| Stronger competitors | Rankings slip as newer, fuller pages appear | Add depth, original examples or a better format |
| Changed intent | The top results are now a different type of page | Rewrite to match, or target a different query |
| SERP changes | Rank is stable but clicks fall | Improve title and snippet; target questions an AI summary won't fully answer |
| Internal competition | A newer page on the same topic takes over | Merge the two (see keyword cannibalization) |

The fourth cause is growing. Google reported that AI Overviews had over 2 billion monthly users by mid-2025, and Pew Research found users clicked fewer links when an AI summary appeared. A page can hold position one and still lose clicks.

## Why content decay matters

Older pages often carry most of a site's backlinks and authority. Letting them decay wastes that value. On commercial pages, the cost is direct: a pricing guide that drops from position two to seven can cost leads every week. Catching decay early makes the fix small.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Updating the publish date makes Google treat a page as fresh.
Fact: Google's helpful content guidance lists changing dates to make pages seem fresh, without substantial changes, as a warning sign. Update the content, then the date.
Myth: Old content should be deleted to "clean up" a site.
Fact: Delete only pages with no traffic, links or purpose. Many old pages are worth more after a refresh than a brand-new page would be.
:::

## How to find and fix content decay

:::steps "A quarterly decay review"
1. Compare two periods | In Search Console, compare the last 3 months with the same 3 months a year earlier. Sort pages by click loss.
2. Shortlist the losers | Flag pages that lost 30% or more of their clicks and still matter to your business.
3. Diagnose each page | Check whether the drop is in position, in click-through rate at the same position, or in impressions overall.
4. Look at today's results | Search the main query. Note what the top pages do that yours doesn't.
5. Choose the action | Refresh, merge, redirect or retire, using the table below.
6. Re-check in 6 to 8 weeks | Compare clicks and position again, and log what you changed.
:::

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Still relevant, has links, lost ground to better pages | Refresh: new facts, examples, structure and sources |
| Overlaps with a stronger page on your site | Merge into the stronger page and 301-redirect |
| Topic no longer relevant to your business, has links | Redirect to the closest relevant page |
| No traffic, no links, no purpose | Retire it with a 410 or 404 |

### What a real refresh includes

:::checklist "Refresh checklist"
- Re-answer the main question in the first two or three sentences
- Replace outdated facts, prices, screenshots and tool names
- Add one original element: a worked example, your own data or a clear recommendation
- Update internal links to your newer pages
- Fix or replace broken outbound links
- Then update the "last reviewed" date
:::

Pages written as [evergreen content](/glossary/evergreen-content/) decay more slowly, but they still need a scheduled review. Track the results with the methods in [SEO analytics](/glossary/seo-analytics/).

:::faq
### How do I know if a page is decaying or just seasonal?
Compare the same months year over year rather than month to month. A seasonal page dips and recovers at the same time each year. A decaying page shows lower numbers than the same period last year, often across several months in a row.
### How often should I refresh content?
Review money pages, such as service and pricing pages, every quarter and guides at least every six months. Refresh when facts change or when the page loses a meaningful share of clicks, not on a fixed schedule for its own sake.
### Does refreshing old content really help rankings?
It often does when the refresh makes the page more useful, more accurate or a better match for current intent. Superficial edits rarely help. Google's guidance focuses on substance, not on how recently a page was touched.
### Should I change the URL when I update a page?
No. Keep the URL so you keep its links and history. Change the URL only if the topic changes completely, and then 301-redirect the old one.
:::
