Guest posting is writing an article for someone else's website, under your own name. The best reason to do it is reach: you get in front of an audience that already trusts the publication. A link back to your site may come with it, but if the link is the only reason for the post, you're in territory Google's spam policies call link spam.

:::takeaways
- Write guest posts for readers and reputation first. Treat links as a side benefit.
- Google's link spam policy names links with optimized anchor text in guest posts distributed on other sites.
- Pick publications your buyers actually read, even if they're small.
- If you host guest posts, keep editorial control and qualify links with rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored".
:::

## When guest posting helps, and when it hurts

| Helpful guest posting | Risky guest posting |
|---|---|
| A few articles a year in respected industry publications | Dozens of posts a month on any site that accepts them |
| A byline and a natural link to your site or author page | Exact-match keyword anchors pointing to money pages |
| Original insight the host's readers value | Spun or generic articles written to carry a link |
| The editor reviews and edits the piece | "Write for us" sites that publish anything for a fee |

The difference is intent and scale. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites" as an example of link spam. A thoughtful article with a byline link isn't the problem. A link-placement campaign is.

## Why guest posting still matters

The audience is the prize. A well-placed article puts your thinking in front of buyers who don't know you yet, and gives you something credible to share with prospects. It also strengthens your expertise signals: a named author with published work across respected sites is easier for readers, search engines and AI tools to recognize as a real expert.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Google has banned guest posting.
Fact: Google targets guest posting used for link schemes, especially at scale with keyword-rich anchors. Writing for respected publications to reach their readers is a normal part of building a reputation.
Myth: Only high-authority sites are worth writing for.
Fact: A niche newsletter or trade blog your exact buyers read can be worth more than a big site with a general audience. Relevance beats raw metrics such as [domain authority](/glossary/domain-authority/).
:::

## How to do guest posting well

:::steps "From shortlist to published article"
1. Shortlist publications | List 15 to 20 sites, newsletters and trade publications your buyers read. Check they publish contributor articles with real editing.
2. Study what they publish | Read their recent articles. Note gaps your experience could fill.
3. Pitch one specific idea | Send the editor a short pitch: the headline, three key points, why you're qualified, and links to past writing.
4. Write the best piece you can | Include first-hand examples and opinions you couldn't find elsewhere. Follow their style guide.
5. Keep links natural | Link where it helps the reader. Accept the editor's decision on links and attributes.
6. Promote and reuse | Share the article with your network and sales team, and link to it from your author page.
:::

### If you host guest posts

Hosting guest content brings its own risk. Google's site reputation abuse policy targets third-party content published mainly to benefit from the host site's ranking signals, without close involvement from the host. Keep guest posts on-topic, edit every piece yourself, and mark outbound links in contributed articles as rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored" where appropriate. That's how our own Journal handles contributions: see our [write-for-us guidelines](journal:/write-for-us/).

:::checklist "Guest post quality check"
- The publication's readers match your buyers
- The article would be worth reading without any link in it
- Anchor text is your name, brand or a natural phrase
- You'd be happy for a prospect to read it
:::

:::faq
### Is guest posting still worth it?
Yes, for reach and reputation. A good article in a publication your buyers read can bring direct leads, speaking invitations and credibility in sales conversations. It's not worth it as a volume link-building tactic, which carries real risk under Google's link spam policy.
### Should guest post links be nofollow?
Links that were paid for, or placed as a condition of publishing, should be qualified with rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". A natural editorial link that the host's editor chose to include doesn't need it. If in doubt, let the host decide.
### How do I find guest posting opportunities?
Start with publications you and your customers already read. Ask customers which newsletters and sites they follow, look for industry associations with blogs, and check where competitors' founders have written. Avoid sites whose main offer is "guest posts for sale".
### What should I write about in a guest post?
Write about something you know first-hand: a process you run, a mistake you made, data you've collected or an opinion you can defend. Generic overviews are easy to get rejected and rarely get read.
:::
