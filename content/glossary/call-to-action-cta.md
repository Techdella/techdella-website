A call to action (CTA) is the part of a page, ad or email that tells people what to do next. It's usually a button or a link: "Book a 20-minute call", "Get the checklist", "Start your free trial". A good CTA is specific about the action and the benefit, and it matches how ready the reader is to act.

:::takeaways
- Use a verb, the thing they get, and a reason or detail that removes doubt.
- Match the CTA to the reader's stage: a cold reader won't book a sales call.
- One primary CTA per page or email. Secondary links are fine, but they shouldn't compete.
- Say what happens after the click, especially for forms and calls.
:::

## A simple formula for writing CTAs

**Verb + what they get + detail that reduces risk**

| Weak CTA | Why it's weak | Stronger CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Submit | Describes the form, not the benefit | Get my growth audit |
| Learn more | Vague; no reason to click | See how the audit works |
| Contact us | Unclear what happens next | Book a 20-minute intro call |
| Sign up | No value stated | Get one practical guide every two weeks |
| Download | Download what? | Download the 8-page checklist |

First-person phrasing ("Get my audit") and specific details ("20-minute", "8-page") both make the action feel concrete. Whether they lift results for your audience is something to test, not assume.

## Match the CTA to the funnel stage

The biggest CTA mistake isn't wording. It's asking for too much, too soon. A reader who just discovered you from a blog post isn't ready for a sales call.

| Stage | Reader's state | Fitting CTAs |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Learning about the problem | Read the guide, get the checklist, subscribe |
| Consideration | Comparing approaches | See pricing, view a case study, use the calculator |
| Decision | Ready to choose | Book a call, request a proposal, start a trial |

This mapping comes from the [digital marketing funnel](/glossary/digital-marketing-funnel/). Each stage needs a next step that feels like a small, natural move.

## Why CTAs matter

Every page should have a job, and the CTA is how the page does it. Without a clear next step, interested visitors leave and may never come back. In B2B, where buyers increasingly research on their own, the CTA often has to carry the moment a salesperson used to handle.

:::myth-fact
Myth: The button color is what makes a CTA work.
Fact: Contrast matters for visibility, but the offer and wording matter far more. A clear, valuable offer in a plain button beats a vague one in a bright color.
Myth: More CTAs on a page give people more chances to convert.
Fact: Several competing goals split attention. Repeat the same primary CTA down a long page, but don't add different ones that compete.
:::

## How to write and place a call to action

:::steps "Write, place and check a CTA"
1. Define the one action | Decide what the reader should do next on this page, given where they are in the funnel.
2. Write three versions | Use the formula. Keep each under about five words on the button, with detail in a line of text nearby.
3. Place it where the decision happens | Put it after the reader has enough information: near the top for simple offers, after proof and pricing for big ones.
4. Repeat on long pages | Add the same CTA after key sections so readers don't scroll back up.
5. Remove the risk | Next to the button, say what happens next: "No obligation. We will email you to arrange a time."
6. Make it easy to tap | On mobile, buttons should be large, well spaced and easy to reach with a thumb.
:::

### CTAs in different channels

- **Web pages:** one primary button, repeated; a secondary text link for readers who need more information.
- **Emails:** one main link or button. In a newsletter, one featured action plus a few secondary links.
- **Ads:** platform CTA buttons are fixed options, so the ad copy carries the specific promise.
- **Social posts:** often a plain-text invitation rather than a hard sell.

Clear wording is a [copywriting](/glossary/copywriting/) skill. For pages built around a single CTA, see [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/), and to compare versions properly, use [A/B testing](/glossary/a-b-testing/).

:::faq
### What is a call to action in marketing?
It's the instruction that tells your audience what to do next, such as subscribe, download, book or buy, along with the button or link that lets them do it. Every piece of marketing should have one, even if it's as small as "read the next guide".
### Where should I place a CTA on a page?
Place it where the reader is ready to decide. For a simple, free offer, near the top works. For a high-value service, place it after the explanation, proof and pricing, then repeat it at natural pause points on long pages.
### How many CTAs should a page have?
One primary action, which can appear several times. Secondary options, such as "see pricing", are fine if they support the same goal. Avoid giving equal weight to unrelated actions like subscribe, book and download on the same page.
### How do I measure CTA performance?
Track clicks on the CTA divided by page visitors (CTA click-through rate) and, more importantly, completed conversions such as submitted forms. A CTA that gets clicks but leads to an abandoned form signals a problem with the next step, not the button.
:::
