Link building is the deliberate work of getting relevant websites to link to yours. The aim is twofold: more qualified visitors from those sites, and stronger signals that help your pages rank. The safe version of link building is really about earning links, by creating something people want to reference and making sure the right people see it. The unsafe version, buying or trading links, breaks Google's spam policies.

:::takeaways
- Earn links with assets people want to cite: original data, tools, expert commentary and useful guides.
- Google's link spam policy covers buying links, excessive link exchanges and keyword-rich links in guest posts or press releases.
- Pick two or three tactics that fit your strengths and do them every month.
- Measure referring domains from relevant sites, not total link count.
:::

## Why link building matters

[Backlinks](/glossary/backlinks/) remain one of the signals search engines use to judge which pages deserve trust. For competitive keywords, strong content without links often stalls on page two. Good link building also brings direct benefits: referral traffic from people already interested in your topic, and brand mentions that shape how buyers and AI tools describe you.

The risk is real too. Google's spam policies list link spam alongside cloaking and scaled content abuse. Its systems can ignore manipulative links, and severe cases get manual actions.

## Link building tactics compared

| Tactic | Effort | Risk | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original data or research | High | Low | Earning links from journalists and bloggers who cite numbers |
| Free tools and templates | High upfront | Low | Ongoing links from "best tools" lists and how-to articles |
| Digital PR and expert commentary | Medium | Low | Links from news and trade media |
| Broken link building | Medium | Low | Replacing dead resources on relevant pages |
| Partner and customer links | Low | Low | Integration pages, case studies, supplier directories |
| Guest posting | Medium | Medium | Reaching a new audience, if done editorially |
| Buying links or link exchanges | Low | High | Nothing. It breaks Google's link spam policy |

Broken link building works because the web loses links constantly. Ahrefs' [link rot study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/link-rot-study/) found that at least 66.5% of links to sites over the last nine years were dead. Every dead link on a relevant page is a chance to suggest a working replacement.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Guest posting is banned by Google.
Fact: Google's link spam policy targets links with optimized anchor text in guest posts distributed on other sites. Writing a genuine article for a relevant publication is fine. Mass guest posting for keyword links is not.
Myth: Link building is just outreach emails.
Fact: Outreach only works when there's something worth linking to. Most of the work is creating that asset.
:::

## How to do link building, month by month

:::steps "A sustainable link building routine"
1. Audit where you stand | Count referring domains for you and three competitors. Note which sites link to competitors but not to you.
2. Build one linkable asset per quarter | A small survey, a benchmark, a calculator or a definitive guide on a topic in your field.
3. Claim the easy links | Ask partners, integrations, event organizers and associations you already work with to list you.
4. Pitch with a reason | Send short, specific emails to people who recently wrote about your topic. Explain what your asset adds.
5. Answer journalist requests | Respond to media queries in your expertise area with a quotable, specific answer.
6. Track and follow up | Log every pitch, reply and live link. Review what earned links and do more of it.
:::

:::checklist "Before you accept or pitch any link"
- The site covers your industry or audience
- The page would make sense to a reader even without your link
- No payment, swap or condition is attached (or the link is marked rel="sponsored")
- The anchor text is natural: your brand or a descriptive phrase

:::faq
### How long does link building take to show results?
New links can take weeks to be crawled and counted, and ranking changes from them often take two to six months. That's why link building works best as a steady monthly habit rather than a one-off campaign.
### Is it safe to pay for links?
Paying for a link that passes ranking credit breaks Google's link spam policy. You can pay for advertising or sponsorships, but those links should carry rel="sponsored". Paying for a writer's time to create a genuine article on your own site is a different matter.
### How many links should I build per month?
Focus on quality and relevance, not a quota. For many small B2B sites, two to five relevant editorial links a month is a strong pace. A sudden jump of hundreds of low-quality links looks unnatural and is the pattern to avoid.
### Can I rank without link building?
For low-competition, specific queries, yes. Many niche pages rank with few or no links. For competitive commercial keywords, it's rare. Check how many referring domains the current top results have to judge your chances.
:::
