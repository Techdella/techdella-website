Fintech marketing is the work of promoting a financial technology product, whether payments, lending, banking, investing, insurance or finance software, to people who are cautious with money by default. Most of standard marketing still applies, but two things change the game: trust has to come before almost anything else, and financial promotion rules limit what you can say and where you can advertise. Fintech marketing that works builds credibility early and gets compliance on board before launching campaigns.

:::takeaways
- Trust is the product. Security, regulation, real customers and clear pricing do more than clever ads.
- Ad platforms and regulators restrict financial promotions, so plan verification and approvals before launch.
- Search and content compound well in fintech, because buyers research carefully before committing money.
:::

## Why fintech marketing is different

| Factor | What it means for marketing |
|---|---|
| High-stakes decision | Buyers want proof, licenses and security details before they sign up |
| Regulation | Claims about returns, rates, fees and safety need care and often legal review |
| Ad platform rules | Many platforms require advertiser verification for financial services |
| Long B2B cycles | Finance, risk and IT all join the decision in B2B fintech |
| Activation gap | Sign-ups mean little until users connect accounts or make a first transaction |

Google Ads, for example, requires financial services verification for advertisers targeting certain locations, and its policy page currently lists 42 countries and territories, including the UK, much of the EU, Australia and India ([Google Ads Policy Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/12492484)). Check the rules for every market and platform you plan to use.

## Why search and content work for fintech

Fintech buyers research. They compare providers, read about fees and security, and look for proof from companies like theirs. That makes useful content and SEO a strong fit, because they meet buyers during that research.

## How to build a fintech marketing plan

:::steps "Six steps for a fintech launch or growth push"
1. Clarify the trust story | List your license or partner bank, security standards, investors and early customers. Put them on the site where decisions happen.
2. Map compliance early | Agree with your compliance lead which claims need approval and which disclaimers apply. Build a pre-approved claims library.
3. Verify ad accounts | Complete financial services verification on each platform before campaigns are due to launch.
4. Own the research phase | Publish clear pages on pricing, fees, security, comparisons and how-to guides. Answer the questions sales hears most.
5. Add partnerships and referrals | Integrations, accountants, communities and [referral programs](/glossary/referral-marketing/) borrow trust you don't have yet.
6. Measure activation, not sign-ups | Track first transaction or first integration, and cost per active user against [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) targets.
:::

For B2B fintech selling to banks or large companies, [account-based marketing](/glossary/account-based-marketing/) helps reach every person in the buying group.

:::faq
### How is fintech marketing different from traditional bank marketing?
Banks lean on long-standing brand trust and branches. Fintechs have to build trust quickly online, usually with a smaller budget, so they rely more on clear product pages, proof, content and partnerships.
### What are the biggest challenges in fintech marketing?
Earning trust without a long track record, meeting financial promotion rules, high acquisition costs, and turning sign-ups into active users.
### Which marketing channels work best for fintech companies?
Search and content for research-driven buyers, partnerships and referrals for borrowed trust, and paid ads once accounts are verified and messaging is approved.
### Do fintech ads need approval?
Many platforms require advertiser verification for financial services in certain countries, and regulators in markets like the UK require financial promotions to be fair, clear and not misleading. Get compliance input before launch.
:::
