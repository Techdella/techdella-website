Content intelligence is the practice of using data and AI to understand how your content performs and to decide what to create, refresh or retire next. Instead of publishing on instinct, you look at search demand, rankings, engagement, conversions and competitor coverage, then let that evidence set the editorial plan. Content intelligence tools automate parts of the analysis, but the method works with free data too.

:::takeaways
- Content intelligence answers three questions: what's working, what's missing, and what to fix first.
- Most of the data you need is already in Search Console, GA4 and your CRM.
- AI speeds up the analysis. People still decide what's worth saying.
:::

## Why content intelligence matters now

Producing content has never been cheaper. Knowing whether it helped is harder than ever. Clicks from search are shifting as AI Overviews and AI assistants answer more questions directly, so page views alone tell you less than they used to.

Content intelligence closes that gap. It tells you which pages earn leads, which ones are decaying, and where competitors answer questions you don't.

## What content intelligence looks at

| Signal | What it tells you | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Search demand and rankings | Which topics people look for and where you stand | Google Search Console, keyword tools |
| Content decay | Pages losing clicks over 90 days or more | Search Console comparisons |
| Engagement | Whether people read, scroll and click on | GA4 |
| Conversions and assisted revenue | Which content starts or helps deals | GA4 plus CRM, see [marketing attribution](/glossary/marketing-attribution/) |
| Topic coverage | Gaps against competitors and buyer questions | SEO tools, sales call notes |
| AI visibility | Whether AI answers mention or cite you | A fixed prompt set, checked monthly |
| Language and tone | Readability and consistency | NLP and readability tools |

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
You don't need a platform to start. I'd begin with a monthly review: Search Console for pages gaining or losing clicks, GA4 for what people do after they land, and the CRM for which pages show up before a deal. Write down three decisions each month. If the data isn't changing what you publish or update, you're collecting it for nothing.
:::

## How to start with content intelligence

:::steps "A quarterly content intelligence routine"
1. Export your page data | Pull 12 months of clicks and impressions per page from Search Console, plus conversions per page from GA4.
2. Sort pages into four buckets | Grow (rising, converting), refresh (decaying but valuable), merge (overlapping) and retire (no traffic, no purpose).
3. Map what's missing | List the questions buyers ask on sales calls. Check which ones have no page, or only a weak one.
4. Group topics into clusters | Organize pages into [topic clusters](/glossary/topic-clusters/) so each hub links to its supporting pages.
5. Prioritize by business value | Rank the work by likely leads, not by search volume alone.
6. Re-measure next quarter | Compare the same metrics. Keep what moved the numbers.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Content intelligence means buying an expensive platform.
Fact: Platforms save time at scale. A small team can run the same analysis with Search Console, GA4, a spreadsheet and a clear set of questions.
:::

## Content intelligence tools

Tools fall into a few groups: SEO content tools that compare your draft with ranking pages, research tools that show which topics earn links and shares, competitor tools that track rivals' keywords and ads, and analytics platforms that tie content to pipeline. Many now add AI summaries. Pick based on the decision you need to make, and avoid tools that push you to copy what already ranks. Google's guidance rewards original, helpful content, and AI tools such as [AI writing tools](/glossary/ai-writing-tools/) work best on a brief built from your own data.

:::faq
### What is the difference between content intelligence and content analytics?
Content analytics reports what happened: views, time on page, conversions. Content intelligence adds the "so what": it combines those numbers with search and competitor data to recommend what to do next.
### Do I need AI for content intelligence?
No. AI helps sort large sites and spot patterns faster, but the core method (measure, bucket, prioritize, re-measure) works by hand on a site with a few hundred pages.
### How often should I run a content intelligence review?
Quarterly for most B2B sites. Review money pages more often if they drive most of your leads.
### Which metric matters most?
The one closest to revenue that you can measure reliably. For most B2B sites that's qualified leads or assisted pipeline per page, not page views.
:::
