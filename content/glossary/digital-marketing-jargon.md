Digital marketing jargon is the shorthand marketers use for channels, metrics and methods: SEO, PPC, CTR, CAC, ROAS, AEO and dozens more. Some of it is useful precision. Some of it hides a simple idea behind an acronym. If you're a founder or manager working with marketers or an agency, knowing the key terms helps you ask better questions and spot when a report is measuring activity instead of results.

:::takeaways
- Most jargon falls into four groups: channels, metrics, funnel stages and methods.
- The metrics closest to money (cost per lead, CAC, LTV, pipeline) matter more than reach metrics.
- If someone can't explain a term in one plain sentence, ask them to.
:::

## Why digital marketing jargon matters

Jargon is fine inside a team that shares the definitions. It becomes a problem when it replaces clarity: a report full of "impressions" and "engagement rate" can look healthy while leads fall. Knowing the vocabulary lets you hold the work to account. It also helps when you read our own [digital marketing](/glossary/digital-marketing/) guides.

## Channel terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| SEO | Search engine optimization: earning unpaid rankings in search results |
| AEO / GEO | Answer and generative engine optimization: getting cited in AI answers |
| SEM | Search engine marketing, usually meaning paid search ads |
| PPC | Pay-per-click: ads you pay for only when clicked |
| Paid social | Ads on LinkedIn, Meta, TikTok and similar platforms |
| Organic | Unpaid reach: search rankings, social posts, word of mouth |
| ABM | Account-based marketing: targeting named companies, not broad audiences |

## Metric terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Impressions | How many times something was shown |
| Reach | How many different people saw it |
| CTR | Click-through rate: clicks divided by impressions |
| CPC / CPM | Cost per click / cost per thousand impressions |
| Conversion rate | Share of visitors who take the action you want |
| CPL | Cost per lead |
| CAC | Customer acquisition cost: total cost to win one customer |
| LTV / CLV | Lifetime value: what a customer is worth over the relationship |
| ROAS | Return on ad spend: revenue divided by ad cost |
| MQL / SQL | Marketing- and sales-qualified lead |

## Funnel and method terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| TOFU / MOFU / BOFU | Top, middle and bottom of the funnel: awareness, consideration, decision |
| Lead magnet | Something useful offered in exchange for an email address |
| Nurture | Follow-up emails or content that build trust over time |
| Retargeting / remarketing | Showing ads to people who already visited you |
| A/B test | Comparing two versions to see which performs better |
| CRO | Conversion rate optimization: improving how many visitors act |
| Attribution | Deciding which touchpoints get credit for a conversion |
| UTM | Tags on links that tell analytics where a visit came from |
| E-E-A-T | Experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust: how Google describes quality |

:::myth-fact
Myth: A high CTR means a campaign is working.
Fact: CTR measures interest in the ad. A campaign works when clicks turn into qualified leads and customers at a cost you can afford.
:::

## How to use jargon without getting lost

:::checklist "Questions to ask in any marketing report"
- What does this metric mean in one sentence?
- How does it connect to leads, customers or revenue?
- Compared with what: last month, last year, a target?
- What will we do differently because of this number?
- What's our cost per qualified lead and our [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/)?
:::

When you write for buyers, drop the jargon. Your customers search for their problem in their own words, not in marketing acronyms. Pages written in plain language tend to read better, rank for the phrases people actually use, and are easier for AI tools to quote. :::faq
### Why do marketers use so much jargon?
Partly for speed: acronyms are shorter. Partly habit. The danger is when jargon hides weak results, so always ask how a metric connects to revenue.
### What are the most important digital marketing terms to know?
Conversion rate, cost per lead, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value and pipeline. They tell you whether marketing is paying for itself.
### What's the difference between SEO, SEM and PPC?
SEO earns unpaid search rankings. PPC is paying per click for ads. SEM is an older umbrella term, now mostly used to mean paid search.
### What do AEO and GEO mean?
They describe optimizing for AI-generated answers, such as Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT, so your brand is mentioned or cited. They build on good SEO rather than replacing it.
:::
