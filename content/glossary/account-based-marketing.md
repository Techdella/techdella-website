Account-based marketing (ABM) is a B2B approach where you pick specific companies you want as customers and market to them directly, rather than casting a wide net and waiting to see who responds. Marketing and sales agree on a target account list, map the people involved in buying, and run coordinated, personalized outreach across ads, email, content, events and sales conversations. ABM treats each high-value account almost as a market of its own.

:::takeaways
- ABM flips the funnel: choose accounts first, then create demand inside them.
- It only works when marketing and sales share the account list, the goals and the reporting.
- Start small: 20 to 50 well-chosen accounts are enough to learn whether ABM fits.
:::

## ABM vs traditional inbound

| | Inbound / broad demand gen | Account-based marketing |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Content and offers that attract anyone relevant | A named list of target companies |
| Audience | Individuals | Buying groups within accounts |
| Personalization | By segment or persona | By account, sometimes by person |
| Success metric | Leads (MQLs) | Account engagement, pipeline and revenue per account |
| Sales role | Follows up on leads | Plans and runs plays with marketing |

They work well together. Inbound fills the top with interested companies; ABM concentrates effort on the accounts worth the most.

## Why account-based marketing matters

B2B deals involve several people, long cycles and large contract values, so a lead from one person rarely tells you much. And most of your target accounts aren't buying right now: the [LinkedIn B2B Institute's research with Ehrenberg-Bass](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) (2021) found 95% of potential B2B buyers aren't ready to buy today. ABM keeps you visible and relevant to the right accounts until they are, and makes sure every person in the buying group already knows you when the project starts.

## The three tiers of ABM

| Tier | Accounts | Personalization | Typical tactics |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-to-one | 5 to 20 strategic accounts | Fully bespoke per account | Custom research, executive events, tailored proposals |
| One-to-few | 20 to 100 accounts in clusters | Per industry or shared problem | Industry pages, cluster-specific ads and emails |
| One-to-many | Hundreds of accounts | Light, data-driven | Account-targeted ads, intent data, dynamic content |

:::myth-fact
Myth: ABM is only for enterprise companies with big budgets.
Fact: A small B2B team can run one-to-few ABM with LinkedIn company targeting, a CRM and well-researched outreach. The discipline matters more than the software.
:::

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
Start smaller than you think. A list of 30 accounts you've actually researched beats 500 names pulled from a database. I ask teams one question before any ABM work: can sales and marketing agree on the same list and the same reason each account should care? If the answer is no, the tooling won't fix it. Get that agreement first, then build the ads and emails around it.
:::

## How to start account-based marketing

:::steps "A first ABM program in one quarter"
1. Define your ideal customer profile | Use [market segmentation](/glossary/market-segmentation/) and your best clients' data: industry, size, region, tech stack, triggers.
2. Build the account list with sales | Pick 20 to 50 accounts. Rank them by fit and by [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/) potential.
3. Map the buying group | For each account, list the decision maker, champions, users and blockers.
4. Research and personalize | Find each account's priorities from their site, hiring, news and earnings. Write messages that reference them.
5. Coordinate the plays | Account-targeted LinkedIn ads, tailored emails, relevant content, events and direct sales outreach, in a planned sequence.
6. Measure by account | Track engagement per account, meetings, opportunities and pipeline, not just leads.
:::

:::checklist "ABM readiness check"
- Sales and marketing agree on the account list and ownership
- Your CRM can track activity at the account level
- You have content relevant to each cluster's problems
- Someone owns each account's plan and reviews it every two weeks
:::

ABM changes the shape of your [sales funnel](/glossary/sales-funnel/): you measure how far each account has moved, not how many anonymous leads entered.

:::faq
### Is ABM only for enterprise companies?
No. Any B2B company with high-value deals and a definable set of ideal customers can use it. Smaller teams start with one-to-few programs.
### What technology do I need for account-based marketing?
A CRM that tracks accounts, LinkedIn Campaign Manager for company targeting, and an email tool. Dedicated ABM and intent-data platforms help at scale but aren't required to start.
### How long does it take to see ABM results?
Expect early engagement signals within weeks and pipeline effects over one or two sales cycles. ABM targets long, high-value deals, so patience is part of the plan.
### How is ABM measured?
By account: share of target accounts engaged, meetings booked, opportunities created, pipeline value and win rate against non-ABM accounts.
:::
