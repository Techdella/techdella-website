Evergreen content is content that keeps earning traffic and trust long after you publish it. It answers questions that don't expire: what something is, how to do it, how to choose between options. News, trend roundups and launch announcements are the opposite. They spike and fade. A good content program uses both, but evergreen pages are the ones that keep working while you sleep.

:::takeaways
- Evergreen topics have steady search demand year after year, not seasonal or news-driven spikes.
- Write so the page ages slowly: keep dated facts in one place and avoid "this year" phrasing in the core advice.
- "Evergreen" doesn't mean "never touch". Review each page on a schedule.
- Evergreen pages work best as pillars that newer, timely posts link back to.
:::

## How to tell if a topic is evergreen

Ask three questions. If the answer to all three is yes, the topic is a good evergreen candidate.

| Test | Evergreen | Not evergreen |
|---|---|---|
| Will people still ask this in three years? | "What is lead scoring?" | "Best marketing tools of this quarter" |
| Is demand steady across the year? | Flat or slowly growing search trend | Spikes around one event or season |
| Is the core answer stable? | The principles rarely change | The answer depends on this month's news |

Google Trends is the quickest way to check the second question. Look at five years of data, not twelve months.

## Why evergreen content matters

Evergreen pages compound. Once a page ranks, it can bring visits, links and leads for years with only periodic updates. That makes them the best return on writing time for small teams. They also make natural pillar pages for [topic clusters](/glossary/topic-clusters/), because newer posts can link back to them.

There's a trust benefit too. A clear, accurate definition or how-to guide is the kind of page other writers cite, and the kind of source AI answers can draw on.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Evergreen content never needs updating.
Fact: Examples, screenshots, tools and numbers age even when the core idea doesn't. Without reviews, evergreen pages suffer [content decay](/glossary/content-decay/) like any other.
Myth: Evergreen content should avoid statistics.
Fact: Sourced numbers make content more credible. Keep them in clearly dated callouts or tables so they're easy to update without rewriting the page.
:::

## How to write evergreen content

:::steps "Write a page that ages slowly"
1. Start from a lasting question | Use [keyword research](/glossary/keyword-research/) and sales calls to find questions buyers ask every year.
2. Answer first | Put the direct answer in the first two or three sentences, then explain.
3. Separate stable and dated material | Keep principles in the body and put prices, tool lists and statistics in tables or callouts with a source and year.
4. Avoid time words in the core | Write "currently" sparingly and avoid "this year" or "recently" in advice that should last.
5. Add an original example | A worked example or your own experience is what makes the page better than others on the same topic.
6. Set a review date | Record when the page was last reviewed and who checked it.
:::

### Evergreen formats that work for B2B

- **Definitions and glossaries:** what a term means and why it matters to the buyer.
- **How-to guides:** setting up a process, such as an email welcome sequence or a sales pipeline.
- **Decision guides:** how to choose between two approaches, with a clear comparison table.
- **Checklists and templates:** reusable tools people bookmark and share.
- **Calculators:** simple tools for questions like cost, ROI or budget.

:::faq
### What is an example of evergreen content?
A guide such as "How to calculate customer acquisition cost" is evergreen: the formula doesn't change, and people search for it every month. A post titled "Ad cost changes this quarter" isn't. Its value fades once the quarter ends.
### What is the opposite of evergreen content?
Time-sensitive content: news, event coverage, trend reports, seasonal campaigns and limited offers. It can drive strong short-term traffic and social engagement, but demand drops quickly once the moment passes.
### Is evergreen content good for SEO?
Yes, because steady demand means steady traffic once the page ranks, and lasting pages tend to collect links over time. It still has to be genuinely useful and kept accurate to hold its rankings.
### How much of my content should be evergreen?
Most small B2B teams do best with the majority evergreen, because each page keeps working. Add timely posts when you have a real point of view on news in your industry, and link them to your evergreen pillars.
:::
