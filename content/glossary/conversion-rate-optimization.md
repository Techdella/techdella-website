Conversion rate optimization (CRO) is the work of getting more value from the visitors you already have. Instead of buying more traffic, you find out why people leave without acting, fix what stops them, and measure the effect. Good CRO starts with research, not with guessing which button color to test.

:::takeaways
- Conversion rate = conversions ÷ visitors × 100, measured for one clear goal.
- Start with research: analytics, session recordings, form data and conversations with customers.
- Prioritize ideas by expected impact, confidence and effort, then test the best ones.
- Raising conversion rate lowers your cost per lead on every channel at once.
:::

## How to calculate conversion rate

**Conversion rate = (conversions ÷ visitors) × 100**

Be specific about both parts. "Visitors" might be sessions or users; "conversions" should be one goal, such as qualified demo requests. Mixing newsletter sign-ups with sales inquiries produces a number that hides what's happening.

### Why CRO pays off: a worked example

An illustrative case: you spend $4,000 a month on ads and send 1,000 visitors to a page that converts at 3%. That's 30 leads at about $133 each. If CRO lifts the page to 4%, the same spend brings 40 leads at $100 each. You've cut cost per lead by a quarter without touching the ad budget. That's why CRO directly lowers [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/).

## Why conversion rate optimization matters

Traffic costs more every year, and B2B buyers increasingly research on their own before talking to anyone. Gartner found that 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience. That puts more of the selling job on your pages: they have to answer questions, build trust and make the next step easy without a salesperson present.

:::myth-fact
Myth: CRO means running lots of A/B tests.
Fact: Testing is one tool. Many sites don't have enough traffic for reliable tests, and research-led fixes to obvious problems, such as broken forms or unclear pricing, often deliver more.
Myth: Best practices you read online will work on your site.
Fact: Your audience and offer are specific. Use best practices as ideas to test or check, not as guaranteed wins.
:::

## The CRO process

:::steps "Five steps, repeated every month"
1. Measure the funnel | Set up key events in analytics for each step: landing, form start, form submit, qualified lead. Find the biggest drop.
2. Research the why | Watch session recordings, review form field drop-off, read sales call notes and ask recent customers what nearly stopped them.
3. Write hypotheses | "Because we saw X, we believe changing Y will improve Z." One change, one reason, one metric.
4. Prioritize | Score each idea for impact, confidence and ease from 1 to 10 and start with the highest totals.
5. Test or ship | With enough traffic, run an A/B test. Without it, ship clear fixes and compare before and after over a full sales cycle.
:::

### Prioritizing ideas: a simple scoring model

| Idea | Impact | Confidence | Ease | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cut the demo form from 9 fields to 4 | 8 | 7 | 9 | 24 |
| Add pricing range to the service page | 7 | 6 | 8 | 21 |
| Rewrite hero headline to match ad copy | 6 | 6 | 9 | 21 |
| Redesign the whole homepage | 7 | 4 | 2 | 13 |

Scores are illustrative. The pattern isn't: focused fixes near the conversion point usually beat big redesigns.

### Where to look first

- **Forms:** too many fields, unclear errors, no autofill on mobile.
- **[Landing pages](/glossary/landing-page/):** headlines that don't match the ad, competing calls to action.
- **Trust:** no proof near the action, no explanation of what happens after submitting.
- **Speed and mobile:** slow, shifting pages lose people before they read anything. See [mobile optimization](/glossary/mobile-optimization/).

:::faq
### What is a good conversion rate?
It depends on the goal, offer, traffic source and industry. A free checklist converts far higher than a sales demo. Use benchmarks for context, but compare yourself mainly with your own past performance on the same page and traffic source.
### How much traffic do I need for CRO?
For reliable A/B testing, you typically need hundreds of conversions per variant over a few weeks. Below that, focus on research-led fixes, user testing and before-and-after comparisons. CRO still works at low traffic; it just relies less on statistics.
### Which tools do I need for CRO?
An analytics tool with key events, such as Google Analytics 4, a session recording and heatmap tool, form analytics, and a way to survey visitors. A testing tool comes later, once you have enough traffic to test.
### How long does CRO take to show results?
Obvious fixes, such as a broken mobile form, can show results within days. A structured program usually needs two to three months to build research, run the first tests and show a measurable trend.
:::
