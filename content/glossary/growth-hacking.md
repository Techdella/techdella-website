Growth hacking is an experiment-led approach to growth: you form a hypothesis, run a small, cheap test, measure the result and scale what works. The term came from startups, where small teams had to find growth without big budgets. Today the method is usually called growth marketing, and it covers the whole customer journey, from how people find you, to how quickly they get value, to whether they stay and refer others.

:::takeaways
- Growth hacking is a process, not a bag of tricks. The edge comes from running many small, well-measured tests.
- Look beyond acquisition: activation and retention fixes often beat new traffic.
- Avoid "hacks" that break platform rules or trust. They rarely last and can cost you the channel.
:::

## Why growth hacking matters

Most teams spend months on big launches and learn slowly. A growth approach learns weekly. By testing small changes (an onboarding email, a pricing-page headline, a referral reward) you find what moves the numbers before committing big budgets. It also forces clarity: you can't run an experiment without a clear metric and a baseline.

## The growth funnel growth teams work on

| Stage | Question | Example experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | How do people find us? | New lead magnet for one segment |
| Activation | Do they get value fast? | Shorter signup, guided first step |
| Retention | Do they come back? | Day-7 tips email, usage alerts |
| Referral | Do they bring others? | Double-sided [referral](/glossary/referral-marketing/) reward |
| Revenue | Do they pay, and more over time? | Annual plan discount, upgrade prompt |

## How to score experiment ideas

Growth teams generate more ideas than they can test. A simple scoring method such as ICE (impact, confidence, ease) helps decide what goes first. Score each from 1 to 10 and average them.

| Idea | Impact | Confidence | Ease | ICE score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rewrite pricing-page headline | 6 | 6 | 9 | 7.0 |
| Add onboarding call for new clients | 8 | 7 | 5 | 6.7 |
| Launch a TikTok channel | 5 | 3 | 4 | 4.0 |

The headline test goes first: decent impact, good evidence, and quick to ship. Re-score after each result.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Growth hacking is about clever shortcuts and viral tricks.
Fact: Lasting growth comes from steady experiments on real customer problems. Shortcuts that rely on loopholes, spam or fake engagement tend to break, get penalized or damage trust.
:::

## How to run growth experiments

:::steps "A weekly experiment loop"
1. Pick one north-star metric | The number that best reflects value delivered, such as qualified demos booked or active customers.
2. Find the biggest leak | Look at the funnel stage with the worst drop-off. That's where tests pay back fastest.
3. Write a hypothesis | "If we change X for Y audience, Z will improve by N, because…" Write down the baseline.
4. Score and pick | Use ICE to choose one to three tests for the week.
5. Run a clean test | Change one thing. Use an [A/B test](/glossary/a-b-testing/) where traffic allows, or a before-and-after with a fixed time window where it doesn't.
6. Decide and document | Keep, kill or iterate. Log every result, including failures, so the team stops repeating them.
:::

Many of the highest-value tests sit in [conversion rate optimization](/glossary/conversion-rate-optimization/): small changes to pages and forms that get more from traffic you already have. Track every win against [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) so growth stays profitable.

:::faq
### What's the difference between growth hacking and digital marketing?
Digital marketing covers all online channels. Growth hacking is a way of working across them: rapid experiments, tight measurement and attention to the whole funnel, including product and retention.
### Do I need technical skills to do growth hacking?
Basic analytics, spreadsheet and landing-page skills go a long way. For product experiments you'll need a developer or no-code tools.
### How long does it take to see results?
Individual tests can show results in one to four weeks. The compounding effect of many small wins usually shows over two or three quarters.
### Is growth hacking only for startups?
No. Established companies use the same experiment methods, often under names like growth marketing or experimentation programs.
:::
