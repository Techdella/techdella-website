LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads let a company sponsor posts written by individual LinkedIn members, such as its founders, employees, creators or other members, so the post reaches a targeted paid audience while still appearing under the person's name. LinkedIn describes them as a way to promote content from LinkedIn members, creators or employees ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1399568)). The author must approve each sponsorship.

## Why LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads matter

B2B buyers trust people more than logos. A founder's honest post about a mistake they made usually earns more attention than a polished company-page ad with the same message. Thought Leader Ads let you put paid targeting behind that personal voice.

:::callout tip "What LinkedIn reports back"
Campaign Manager reports on Thought Leader Ads like other sponsored content, and LinkedIn notes that engagement metrics include member follows and clicks to the member's profile. Members can also see aggregated organic plus paid impressions on their own post ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1399568)).
:::

They are a strong fit for [account-based marketing](/glossary/account-based-marketing/): the same accounts see your founder's thinking repeatedly before sales ever reaches out.

## How Thought Leader Ads work

| Rule | Detail (per LinkedIn Help) |
|---|---|
| Who can create them | Page super admins, content admins or Sponsored Content posters on the company Page tied to the ad account |
| Whose posts | Employees, creators, and other members, including 1st, 2nd and 3rd-degree+ connections |
| Permission | The author receives a request and can approve or reject it, and can turn on auto-approval |
| Profile requirements | The member's profile must show their full name; the post must be public |
| Not eligible | Celebrations, documents, polls, multi-image posts and reposts (sponsor the original instead) |
| Formats and objectives | Single image, video, event, article and newsletter posts; brand awareness and engagement, plus video views for video |

Because objectives are awareness and engagement focused, Thought Leader Ads work best as the top of a sequence. You then retarget engaged members with lead-gen forms or conversion campaigns.

## Example: founder-led awareness to pipeline

A hypothetical cybersecurity firm targets 400 named accounts.

1. The CEO publishes two posts a week for six weeks. The four best organic performers are sponsored at $1,200 each, targeted to the 400 accounts.
2. Campaign Manager shows 3,100 members from 260 of those accounts engaged.
3. A retargeting campaign offers those engagers a 2-page security checklist through a LinkedIn lead-gen form. 85 download it.
4. Sales calls the 85, books 11 meetings and opens 4 opportunities.

Total paid spend: $4,800 + $1,500 retargeting = $6,300. Cost per opportunity: $6,300 ÷ 4 = **$1,575**. Whether that's good depends on deal size, which is the point: judge the sequence, not the post.

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
The founders who do well with these ads were already posting before they paid anything. Sponsorship amplifies a voice; it can't invent one. My advice is to post consistently first, sponsor only the posts that earned real comments from the right people, and plan the retargeting step before you launch. Otherwise you're paying for likes with nowhere to send the people who gave them.
:::

## How to run LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads

:::checklist "Thought Leader Ads checklist"
- Pick 1–3 voices with real expertise and the time to post consistently
- Post organically first and sponsor only posts that already earn genuine comments
- Use eligible formats: text with single image, video, event, article or newsletter
- Send the sponsorship request and agree with the author how long you will promote it
- Target by account list, job function and seniority rather than broad interests
- Retarget engagers with a clear next step: a resource, a webinar or a call
- Track meetings and opportunities in your CRM, not just likes
- Never put words in the author's mouth: their post, their voice, their approval
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Thought Leader Ads only work for employees' posts.
Fact: Since 2024, LinkedIn has let companies sponsor posts from members outside the company too, such as customers, partners or creators, as long as the author approves.
:::

:::faq
### Can I sponsor a post without the author's permission?
No. The author receives a sponsorship request and must approve it. They can also revoke permission later, which stops the ad.
### Can I run lead-gen forms on Thought Leader Ads?
LinkedIn lists brand awareness, engagement and video views as the supported objectives. Most teams use Thought Leader Ads to warm up an audience, then retarget engagers with a lead-gen campaign.
### Can I edit the post before sponsoring it?
The ad uses the member's post as published. If something needs to change, the author edits their own post. That keeps the voice authentic.
### Which posts make the best Thought Leader Ads?
Posts that already perform organically: a specific opinion, a lesson learned, a short how-to or a behind-the-scenes story. Generic announcements rarely work better just because a person posted them.
:::
