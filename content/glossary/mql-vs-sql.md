The difference between an MQL and an SQL is who has qualified the lead and how far. A **marketing-qualified lead (MQL)** is a lead that marketing's rules say is worth sales' attention: they fit your ideal customer and have shown interest. A **sales-qualified lead (SQL)** is a lead a salesperson has spoken to or reviewed and confirmed as a real opportunity: right need, right authority, realistic timing. Every SQL was an MQL first. Most MQLs never become SQLs, and that's normal.

## Why the MQL vs SQL distinction matters

Most friction between marketing and sales comes from undefined handoffs. Marketing reports 300 leads; sales says they're all junk. Both may be right, because they mean different things by "lead".

:::callout tip "Buyers arrive late and decided"
6sense's 2024 Buyer Experience Report, a survey of 2,509 recent B2B buyers, found that 81% already have a preferred vendor at first contact and that buyers initiate first contact more than 80% of the time ([6sense, 2024](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/2024-buyer-experience-report/)). By the time a lead becomes an SQL, much of the decision has been made, so the MQL stage is where marketing earns its place on the shortlist.
:::

Clear definitions let you measure each stage, fix the weakest one, and send the right conversion signal to ad platforms.

## How MQLs and SQLs differ

| | MQL | SQL |
|---|---|---|
| Who qualifies | Marketing rules, often a [lead score](/glossary/lead-scoring/) | A salesperson |
| Based on | Fit data and digital behavior | A conversation or review: need, budget, authority, timing |
| Typical trigger | Score threshold, demo request, high-intent content | Discovery call confirms a real opportunity |
| Next step | Sales accepts or rejects it | An opportunity or deal is created |
| Owner of the metric | Marketing | Sales |

Many teams add a stage between them: **SAL (sales-accepted lead)**, meaning sales has agreed to work the lead, but hasn't qualified it yet. It exposes leads that sit untouched.

## Example: one quarter's funnel

A hypothetical B2B software company reviews Q3:

- 1,200 new leads → 240 MQLs (**20%**)
- 240 MQLs → 192 SALs (**80%** accepted by sales)
- 192 SALs → 58 SQLs (**30%**)
- 58 SQLs → 14 won deals (**24%**)

The weak point isn't volume. It's SAL → SQL at 30%. Call notes show many MQLs came from a broad ebook offer that attracted students and consultants. Marketing tightens the fit criteria and adds a company-size question to the form. Next quarter, MQLs drop to 180, but SQLs rise to 65. Fewer MQLs, more pipeline.

## How to define MQL and SQL for your team

:::checklist "Handoff agreement checklist"
- Write the MQL definition in one sentence: fit criteria plus the action or score that triggers it
- Write the SQL definition: the questions sales must confirm, such as need, budget range, decision role and timeline
- Agree a response time for MQLs, for example first contact within one business day
- Add a rejection reason field so sales explains every rejected MQL
- Track conversion rates at every stage, every month
- Send SQL and closed-won events back to Google and Meta as offline conversions
- Review definitions together each quarter using the rejection reasons
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: More MQLs means marketing is doing a better job.
Fact: MQL volume is easy to inflate with broad offers. The test is how many MQLs become SQLs and revenue. Report the conversion rate, not just the count.
:::

Signals like [intent data](/glossary/intent-data/) can help you spot accounts worth contacting before anyone fills a form.

:::faq
### What is an example of an MQL?
A marketing director at a target-size company who downloads a pricing guide and visits the pricing page twice in a week, pushing their lead score over your threshold. They fit and they're showing interest, but nobody has spoken to them yet.
### What is an example of an SQL?
That same marketing director after a discovery call confirms they have budget approved for next quarter, are evaluating three vendors and need a decision in 60 days. Sales creates an opportunity.
### What is a good MQL to SQL conversion rate?
It varies widely by industry, deal size and how strict your MQL definition is. Track your own rate over time rather than chasing a benchmark. A falling rate usually means MQL criteria are too loose.
### Is a demo request an MQL or an SQL?
Usually an MQL, or a fast track to sales acceptance. It shows strong intent, but sales still needs to confirm need, authority and timing before it counts as an SQL.
:::
