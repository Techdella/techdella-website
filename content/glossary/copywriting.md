Copywriting is writing with a job to do: get a specific reader to take a specific action. A headline that earns a click, a landing page that earns a sign-up, an email that earns a reply, a product page that earns a purchase. Good copywriting starts with the reader, not the writer: what they want, what worries them, and what would make the next step feel easy and safe.

:::takeaways
- Copywriting is measured by action. If nobody acts, it didn't work, however well it reads.
- People scan more than they read, so structure and the first line carry most of the weight.
- Use your customers' words. The best copy is often found in sales calls and reviews, not invented.
:::

## Copywriting vs content writing

| | Copywriting | Content writing |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Get an action now | Inform, teach, build trust over time |
| Length | Usually short | Often long |
| Where | Ads, landing pages, emails, product pages, CTAs | Guides, articles, glossaries, newsletters |
| Measured by | Clicks, sign-ups, sales | Traffic, engagement, assisted conversions |

The two overlap. A long guide still needs copywriting in its headline, intro and calls to action. That's why copywriting sits inside every [content marketing](/glossary/content-marketing/) plan.

## Why copywriting matters

Most visitors don't read. They scan. Jakob Nielsen's analysis of browsing data found that on an average web page, users have time to read at most 28% of the words, and 20% is more likely ([Nielsen Norman Group, 2008](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)). So the headline, subheads, first sentences, bullet points and button labels do most of the persuading. Copy that buries the point loses the reader before it gets there. In 2026 the same discipline helps with AI answers: clear, answer-first sentences are the ones AI tools can lift and cite.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Great copy is clever copy.
Fact: Clear beats clever. A plain headline that names the reader's problem and the outcome usually outperforms wordplay that makes them work to understand it.
:::

## How to write copy that converts

:::steps "A five-part framework"
1. Name the reader and the moment | Who is reading this, and what just happened that brought them here? Write to that one person.
2. Lead with the outcome | The headline says what they get, in their words. "Get your first 20 qualified leads in 60 days" beats "Innovative growth solutions."
3. Prove it | Add specific proof: a number, a named client (with permission), a quote, a screenshot. Specifics beat adjectives.
4. Remove the risk | Answer the objection they're thinking: price, time, effort, lock-in. Put the answer near the button.
5. Ask for one action | One clear call to action with a label that says what happens next: "Book a 20-minute call," not "Submit."
:::

:::checklist "Before you publish any copy"
- The first line makes sense on its own
- Every claim is specific and true
- Sentences are short; read it with our [readability checker](/tools/free-readability-checker/)
- There's one main call to action
- It sounds like a person talking, not a brochure
:::

## SEO copywriting

SEO copywriting is writing pages that both rank and convert. Use the phrase people search for in the title, first paragraph and one subheading, then write for the human. Answer the question in the first two or three sentences, use subheads that match how people ask, and keep the call to action visible. Don't repeat keywords to hit a density target. Search engines understand meaning, and readers notice stuffing immediately.

## Test, don't guess

Opinions about copy are cheap. Results aren't. Run [A/B tests](/glossary/a-b-testing/) on headlines and calls to action where traffic allows, and watch how changes affect the [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/) conversion rate, not just clicks.

:::faq
### What does a copywriter do?
A copywriter researches the audience and offer, then writes the words for ads, landing pages, emails, product pages and sales materials, and works with designers and marketers to test what converts.
### Is copywriting still a useful skill with AI tools?
Yes. AI can draft quickly, but choosing the angle, the proof and what to leave out still takes judgment and customer knowledge. Strong copywriters use AI to go faster, not to think less.
### How long should copy be?
As long as it needs to be to answer the reader's questions, and no longer. Simple, low-risk actions need little copy. Expensive decisions need more proof and detail.
### What is the best way to learn copywriting?
Collect examples that made you act, study your customers' language in reviews and calls, write daily, and test your work against real results.
:::
