An email marketing campaign is a planned send, or series of sends, aimed at one audience with one goal. A product launch to existing customers is a campaign. So is a five-email nurture sequence for people who downloaded a guide, or a win-back series for contacts who stopped clicking. What makes it a campaign, rather than just "an email," is that it has a defined audience, a single call to action and a way to tell whether it worked.

:::takeaways
- One campaign, one goal, one call to action. Everything else is a distraction.
- Segment before you write. The same message to everyone usually underperforms.
- Judge campaigns on clicks, replies and conversions, and do the math before you send.
:::

## Types of email marketing campaigns

| Campaign type | Who gets it | Goal | Usually sent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome series | New subscribers or leads | Deliver the promise, introduce you | Automatically, over 1 to 2 weeks |
| Lead nurture (drip) | People who downloaded or signed up | Move to a sales conversation | Automatically, over 3 to 6 weeks |
| Product or service launch | Customers and engaged leads | Awareness and early sign-ups | Once, plus a follow-up |
| Event or webinar | A target segment | Registrations and attendance | Invite, reminder, replay |
| Promotional offer | Engaged contacts | Sales within a deadline | Two or three sends |
| Re-engagement | Contacts with no clicks in months | Win back or remove | Two or three sends, then suppress |

Ongoing newsletters aren't campaigns in this sense, but each issue benefits from the same discipline: one main idea and one clear next step. For the channel as a whole, see [email marketing](/glossary/email-marketing/).

## Why campaign planning matters

Mailbox providers watch how people react. Gmail asks bulk senders to keep spam complaints below 0.30%, support one-click unsubscribe and authenticate their domain ([Google Workspace Admin Help](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Sending the wrong message to the wrong segment raises complaints and hurts every later send, not just this one.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Sending to your whole list gets the most results.
Fact: Untargeted sends raise unsubscribes and complaints, which can push future emails into spam. A smaller, relevant segment often brings more total conversions over time.
:::

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
Every campaign we write starts with a one-line brief: who it's for, the one thing we want them to do, and what we'll leave out. If the brief needs two calls to action, it's two campaigns. I'd rather send a short email to a small, engaged segment than a long one to the whole list, because a burst of complaints follows you into every send after it.
:::

## How to build an email marketing campaign

:::steps "Seven steps from goal to report"
1. Set the goal and the number | For example: 15 demo bookings from a webinar replay campaign.
2. Choose the segment | Pick contacts by behavior and fit, such as attendees who didn't book, in your target industries.
3. Do the funnel math | Work out how many sends, clicks and visits you need (see the example below).
4. Write one idea per email | A specific subject line, a short body, one call to action. Test subject lines with our [subject line analyzer](/tools/email-subject-line-analyzer/).
5. Build the landing page | Match the email's promise exactly. A mismatched [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/) loses clicks you've already paid for in attention.
6. Test before sending | Check links, mobile layout, dark mode and plain-text version. Run an [A/B test](/glossary/a-b-testing/) on one element if the list is large enough.
7. Report and learn | Record clicks, conversions, unsubscribes and complaints, and note what you'd change next time.
:::

## Worked example: campaign math

Say you want 15 demo bookings from a 3,000-contact segment. Your past campaigns show a 3% click rate and a 10% booking rate from the landing page.

- 3,000 contacts × 3% click rate = 90 visitors per send
- 90 visitors × 10% booking rate = 9 bookings per send
- Two sends to non-clickers gets you close to 15, if the second send performs about as well

If the math doesn't add up, change the plan before you send: a bigger segment, a stronger offer or a better page. Use your own rates, not industry averages.

:::faq
### What is an email drip campaign?
A drip campaign is a set of emails sent automatically on a schedule or after a trigger, such as a guide download. Each email moves the reader one step closer to a decision.
### How many emails should a campaign have?
Use as many as the goal needs. Launches often need two or three; nurture sequences usually run four to seven. Stop when replies and clicks drop off.
### What makes a good email subject line?
Specific, honest and short enough to read on a phone. It should tell the reader what's inside, not trick them into opening.
### How do I check competitors' email campaigns?
Subscribe to their newsletters and download their lead magnets with a work address. Note their timing, offers and calls to action, then make yours more useful rather than copying them.
:::
