Black hat SEO is any tactic that tries to rank a page by fooling search engines rather than by being the best answer. It includes old tricks such as hidden text and keyword stuffing, and newer ones such as mass-producing AI pages or renting space on a trusted site. Google lists these practices in its spam policies, and its systems and reviewers act on them. Some black hat tactics work briefly. Few last, and the cleanup often costs more than the gain.

:::takeaways
- Google's spam policies name the tactics, so "we didn't know" isn't much protection.
- Newer policies target scaled content abuse, site reputation abuse and expired domain abuse.
- Penalties range from ignored links to pages or whole sites dropping out of results.
- If a vendor guarantees rankings or sells links by the hundred, walk away.
:::

## Black hat tactics and the safer alternative

| Tactic | What Google calls it | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Buying links or large link swaps | Link spam | Earn links with useful assets and outreach |
| Keyword-rich guest posts at scale | Link spam | Write genuine articles for relevant publications |
| Hundreds of near-identical pages, human or AI-written | Scaled content abuse | Fewer pages, each with original substance |
| Near-duplicate city or keyword pages | Doorway abuse | Location pages only where you have real presence |
| Showing search engines different content than users | Cloaking | Serve the same content to everyone |
| Hidden text or links | Hidden text and link abuse | Put content where readers can see it |
| Repeating keywords unnaturally | Keyword stuffing | Write naturally for the reader |
| Publishing third-party content on a trusted site to borrow its rankings | Site reputation abuse | Publish under editorial control, or on the partner's own site |
| Buying an expired domain to rank unrelated content | Expired domain abuse | Build your own domain's reputation |
| Marking up fake or hidden reviews | Structured data policy violation | Mark up only real, visible content |

Every item in the middle column comes from [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). The policy on scaled content abuse applies however the content is made, by people, by AI or by both.

## Why black hat SEO is a bad bet

The risk is lopsided. A tactic might lift rankings for a few weeks, but when Google's systems catch up, you can lose more than you gained. Severe cases get a manual action in Search Console. Recovering means cleaning up and filing a reconsideration request, which can take months. Meanwhile, the brand damage of spammy links and pages stays visible to buyers.

There's also a reputation cost beyond Google. AI answers and buyers both judge your brand by what the web says about you. A footprint of paid links and thin pages doesn't build trust.

:::myth-fact
Myth: AI-written content is black hat by definition.
Fact: Google's policy targets content produced at scale mainly to manipulate rankings, however it's produced. AI-assisted content that a person edits and adds real value to isn't the problem.
Myth: Negative SEO from competitors is a common cause of ranking drops.
Fact: Google says that in most cases it can assess which links to trust without your help. Most drops have other causes: updates, technical issues or stronger competitors.
:::

## How to stay on the right side

:::steps "Protect your site in five steps"
1. Read the policies once | Skim Google's spam policies with anyone who works on your site, including freelancers.
2. Vet vendors | Ask exactly how links are earned and see examples. Refuse packages priced per link.
3. Check Search Console monthly | Look at Manual actions and Security issues, and act immediately if anything appears.
4. Audit inherited sites | If you bought a domain or took over an old site, review its backlinks and history before investing.
5. Put people in the content loop | Every page should have a named author and reviewer who checks facts and adds something original.
:::

:::checklist "Red flags in an SEO proposal"

:::faq
### What is the difference between black hat and white hat SEO?
White hat SEO follows search engine guidelines and focuses on helping users: useful content, sound technical setup and earned links. Black hat SEO breaks those guidelines to manipulate rankings. "Grey hat" describes tactics in between, which often become black hat as policies tighten.
### Can Google penalize my site for black hat SEO?
Yes. Its automated systems can demote or ignore spammy content and links, and human reviewers can issue a manual action that affects pages or the whole site. Manual actions show in Search Console with a description of the problem.
### How do I recover from a manual action?
Fix the cause: remove or disavow paid links, delete or improve spammy pages, or take down cloaked content. Then submit a reconsideration request in Search Console explaining what you did. Recovery depends on how thorough the cleanup is.
### Is buying an old domain black hat?
Not automatically. Buying a domain to continue a related business is fine. Google's expired domain abuse policy targets buying an expired domain mainly to rank low-value, unrelated content on its old reputation.
:::
