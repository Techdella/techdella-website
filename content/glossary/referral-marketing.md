Referral marketing is the practice of encouraging existing customers to recommend you to people they know, in a structured, trackable way. It takes the word of mouth you already get and makes it repeatable: you ask at the right moment, make sharing easy, track who referred whom, and thank or reward the referrer. Referral marketing works because a recommendation from someone you trust skips most of the doubt a stranger's ad has to overcome.

:::takeaways
- Recommendations from people we know are the most trusted source of brand information.
- The ask matters more than the reward. Ask right after a customer gets a win.
- Set rewards from customer value and acquisition cost, and track every referral to revenue.
:::

## Why referral marketing works

:::stats "Trust is the advantage" highlight=0
88% | of consumers trust recommendations from friends and family most, across 56 countries | Nielsen Trust in Advertising Study, 2021 | https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2021/trust-in-advertising-study-reveals-real-life-situations-and-humour-as-the-top-attributes/
:::

Referred customers arrive pre-qualified: the referrer usually knows whether they're a fit. In B2B, a recommendation from a peer or adviser often decides which vendors make the shortlist at all. Referrals also tend to cost less than paid acquisition, which improves your [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/).

## Types of referral programs

| Type | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Customer referral | Customers share a link or code; both sides get a reward | SaaS, ecommerce, fintech apps |
| Partner referral | Advisers, agencies or consultants introduce clients | B2B services and software |
| Ask-based (no reward) | You ask happy clients for introductions at key moments | High-value B2B services |
| Community or ambassador | Engaged fans get perks and early access | Products with strong communities |

Referral programs reward customers. [Affiliate marketing](/glossary/affiliate-marketing/) pays outside partners as a business activity. Many companies run both.

## Setting the reward: a worked example

| Input | Example |
|---|---|
| Average customer lifetime gross profit | $2,400 |
| Current paid acquisition cost per customer | $600 |
| Reward: $150 credit to referrer + $150 off for new customer | $300 per referred customer |
| Saving versus paid acquisition | $300 per customer |

The reward is half your paid cost, generous enough to motivate, and still leaves most of the [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/). Run the same math with your own numbers. For high-value B2B services, a thank-you gift or a donation often works better than cash, which can feel awkward between professionals.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A bigger reward always brings more referrals.
Fact: People refer when they're happy and the ask is easy. A large reward without a great experience brings low-quality referrals or none at all.
:::

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
Most referral programs fail at the ask, not the reward. Happy clients rarely think to refer you unless you make it easy and specific. I'd ask right after a clear win, name the kind of company you help best, and give them a short intro they can forward. Then thank every referrer personally, whether or not the deal closes.
:::

## How to start a referral marketing program

:::steps "From first ask to a repeatable program"
1. Find your happiest customers | Use NPS, renewals, reviews or simply account manager knowledge.
2. Pick the moment | Ask right after a win: a goal hit, a project shipped, a renewal signed.
3. Make it easy | One link or code, a short message they can forward, and a landing page for referred visitors.
4. Choose a fair reward | Double-sided rewards (both people benefit) usually feel better than one-sided ones.
5. Track everything | Tag referral links, record the referrer in your CRM, and follow referred leads to revenue.
6. Thank people every time | Even when the referral doesn't convert, thank the referrer and tell them what happened.
:::

:::faq
### What is the main goal of referral marketing?
To win new customers through recommendations from existing ones, at a lower cost and with higher trust than most paid channels.
### Is referral marketing suitable for small businesses?
Yes. Small businesses often run on referrals already. A simple structure (when to ask, how to track, how to thank) makes them more predictable.
### Should B2B companies offer cash for referrals?
Sometimes, especially with partners. With clients, many prefer non-cash thanks, and some industries restrict gifts, so check client policies and local rules.
### How do I track referrals?
Give each referrer a unique link or code, store the referrer on the new contact in your CRM, and report referred revenue monthly.
:::
