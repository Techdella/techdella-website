AI writing tools are apps built on large language models (LLMs) that turn a prompt into text: outlines, first drafts, rewrites, summaries, headlines and grammar fixes. Used well, AI writing tools cut the time between a blank page and an edited draft. Used badly, they produce pages that sound like everyone else's and say nothing new.

:::takeaways
- AI writing tools are drafting and editing aids. They don't know your customers, your numbers or your opinions.
- Google doesn't penalize AI-assisted content. It penalizes unhelpful content made at scale to manipulate rankings.
- The value you add (first-hand examples, data and a point of view) is what makes a page worth ranking.
:::

## What AI writing tools actually do

Most tools fall into four groups. Many products now do all four.

| Type | What it does | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| General assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) | Draft, rewrite and reason from a prompt | Outlines, first drafts, summaries | Confident errors and invented sources |
| Editing tools (Grammarly and similar) | Grammar, clarity, tone | Polishing finished drafts | Flattening your voice |
| SEO writing tools | Compare your draft with ranking pages | Spotting missing subtopics | Copying what already ranks |
| Short-form generators | Ads, subject lines, social posts | Volume of variants to test | Generic hooks |

## Why AI writing tools matter for your marketing

Almost every marketing team now uses them. The harder question is whether they make the work better.

That gap is the point. Speed is easy to buy. Insight isn't. On search, Google's position has been stable since its [February 2023 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content): "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines." What its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) do target is scaled content abuse, which includes using generative AI tools "to generate many pages without adding value for users."

:::myth-fact
Myth: Google can detect AI text and ranks it lower.
Fact: Google ranks pages on helpfulness and E-E-A-T, however they were produced. A thin page fails whether a person or a model wrote it.
:::

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
Every draft that comes out of an AI tool at TechDella goes through a person who adds something the model couldn't know: an example from a client call, a judgment we'd stand behind, a screenshot from our own work. If an editor can't find anything to add, we don't publish the piece. That one rule protects quality better than any detector or style guide.
:::

## How to use AI writing tools without hurting quality

:::steps "A safe AI writing workflow"
1. Start with your own brief | Write the angle, the audience and the proof you have before you open the tool. The tool fills structure, not substance.
2. Draft in sections, not in one go | Ask for one section at a time. Short prompts with context give tighter, more specific text.
3. Replace every generic claim | Swap statements like "many businesses struggle" for your own example, number or screenshot.
4. Check every fact and source | Models invent statistics and links. Open each source yourself or delete the claim.
5. Edit for voice and readability | Read it aloud. Cut filler. Run it through our [readability checker](/tools/free-readability-checker/).
6. Have a named person review it | A real author and reviewer sign off, which is also what Google's E-E-A-T guidance looks for.
:::

:::checklist "Before you publish an AI-assisted draft"
- It answers the search question in the first two or three sentences
- It contains at least one thing only your team could write
- Every number links to its original source
- No phrases you'd never say out loud to a client
:::

## Choosing an AI writing tool

Pick by job, not by feature list. If you mostly write long guides, a general assistant plus your own editing beats a niche generator. If you write lots of ad variants, a short-form tool with brand-voice settings saves time. Check three things before you pay: whether your inputs are used for model training (and whether you can opt out), whether you can store a style guide, and whether it fits where you already write. For the bigger picture, see [content marketing](/glossary/content-marketing/) and [copywriting](/glossary/copywriting/).

:::faq
### Will using AI writing tools get my site penalized?
No, not by itself. Google says appropriate use of AI isn't against its guidelines. What gets penalized is publishing many low-value pages to manipulate rankings. Edited, accurate, useful AI-assisted content is treated like any other content.
### Can AI writing tools replace a copywriter?
They replace some typing, not the thinking. A copywriter knows the customer, picks the angle and decides what to leave out. AI makes a good writer faster. It doesn't make a weak brief stronger.
### Are free AI writing tools good enough?
For grammar fixes and short rewrites, often yes. For client work, check the data terms first: some free plans may use your inputs to train models. Paid business plans usually give you more control.
### How do I make AI-assisted content original?
Add what the model can't know: your own results, client stories (with permission), screenshots, and opinions you can defend. One real example is worth more than five extra paragraphs.
:::
