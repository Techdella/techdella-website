Schema markup is a block of code that tells search engines, in plain labels, what your page is about: this is an organization, this is its logo, this article was written by this person on this date. People never see it, but Google uses it to understand the page and to decide whether it qualifies for rich results such as review stars, breadcrumbs or product details.

:::takeaways
- Google recommends JSON-LD as the easiest format to implement and maintain.
- Schema markup can make a page eligible for rich results. It doesn't guarantee them.
- FAQ rich results now show only for authoritative government and health sites, and HowTo rich results are gone.
- Only mark up what's visible on the page. Hidden or misleading markup can cost you rich results.
:::

## Why schema markup matters

Structured data removes guesswork. Instead of inferring that "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" is the author, Google reads it directly. That clarity matters more now that search results and AI answers both depend on understanding entities: who you are, what you sell, and how your pages relate to each other.

It can also change how your result looks. A breadcrumb trail or product price can make a listing more useful, which can affect your [click-through rate](/glossary/click-through-rate/).

## What changed: rich results you can still get

Many older guides recommend markup that no longer pays off. In August 2023 Google limited FAQ rich results to authoritative government and health websites and deprecated HowTo rich results. Google also doesn't let a business show review stars from reviews it collects about itself on its own site.

| Schema type | Still useful? | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Organization | Yes | Your logo, name, social profiles and contact details |
| Article / BlogPosting | Yes | Author, dates and images on guides and posts |
| BreadcrumbList | Yes | Showing where a page sits in your site |
| Product, Offer | Yes, for real products | Price, availability, reviews from customers |
| LocalBusiness | Only with a real, staffed address | Hours, address, phone |
| Event, JobPosting, VideoObject | Yes, where relevant | Their specific rich results |
| FAQPage | Rarely | Only authoritative government and health sites get the rich result |
| HowTo | No | Deprecated in 2023 |

:::myth-fact
Myth: Schema markup is a ranking factor.
Fact: Google uses it to understand pages and to decide rich-result eligibility. A structured data manual action removes rich-result eligibility but, according to Google, doesn't affect how the page ranks.
Myth: More schema types on a page is better.
Fact: Mark up the main thing the page is about, completely and accurately. Markup must match content readers can see on the page.
:::

## How to add schema markup

:::steps "From zero to valid structured data"
1. Decide the page type | Is this page about your company, a service, an article, a product or a location? That decides the main schema type.
2. Write it in JSON-LD | Put one script block in the page, ideally generated by your CMS template so it stays in sync with the content.
3. Connect the pieces | Give your Organization a stable @id and reference it from articles, services and the website node, instead of repeating it.
4. Validate | Test with Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator before publishing.
5. Monitor | Check the Enhancements reports in Search Console for errors after each release.
:::

:::callout warning "Avoid duplicate markup"
Many WordPress sites output schema from the theme, an SEO plugin and a reviews plugin at once. The result is three conflicting Organization entries. Pick one source and switch the others off.
:::

### A minimal Organization example

A small company needs only a few properties to start: `@type` set to Organization, `name`, `url`, `logo`, and `sameAs` with links to your real social profiles. Add `founder` and `contactPoint` when you have them. Keep the details identical to your Google Business Profile and directory listings, which also helps [local SEO](/glossary/local-seo/).

Clear entity data is one of the foundations of [AI search optimization](/services/ai-search-optimization/), because AI answers need to know exactly who you are before they can mention you.

### What is the difference between schema markup and structured data?
Structured data is the general idea of labeling page content in a standard format. Schema markup usually means structured data that uses the schema.org vocabulary, which Google, Bing and others support. In everyday SEO conversations, the two terms mean the same thing.
### Do I need a developer to add schema markup?
Not always. Most SEO plugins add basic Organization, Article and Breadcrumb markup. For custom page types, services or connected entities, a developer can add it to your templates once, so it updates automatically whenever content changes.
### How long until rich results appear?
Google has to recrawl the page first, which can take days to weeks. Even valid markup isn't guaranteed to produce a rich result. Google decides based on the query and the page. Use the Rich Results Test to confirm eligibility.
### Does schema markup help with AI Overviews?
Google says there are no special requirements for AI features beyond normal SEO best practices. Schema still helps machines understand your entities accurately, so it's part of a sound setup, not a shortcut to being cited.
:::
