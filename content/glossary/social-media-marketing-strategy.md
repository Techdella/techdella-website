A social media marketing strategy is the plan behind your social activity: why you're on social, who you want to reach, which platforms you'll use, what you'll publish and promote, who's responsible, and how you'll know it worked. Without one, teams post because they feel they should, measure likes, and can't explain what social contributes to the business. With one, every post has a job.

:::takeaways
- Start from a business goal, then choose platforms and content. Not the other way round.
- Fit it on one page. A plan nobody reads won't guide anything.
- Review it quarterly. Platforms, formats and your audience's habits keep shifting.
:::

## Why a social media marketing strategy matters

Attention is spread thin. Including video platforms, online adults spend well over two and a half hours a day on social media, and use an average of 6.75 platforms a month ([DataReportal Digital 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-two-in-three-people-use-social-media)). You can't be everywhere, so the strategy is mostly about choosing: which audience, which platforms, which messages, and what you'll stop doing.

## What the strategy should include

| Section | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Business goal | The outcome social should support | 40 qualified leads a month from LinkedIn |
| Audience | Who, their problem and trigger | Finance leads at mid-size logistics firms |
| Platforms | Where that audience pays attention | LinkedIn (main), YouTube (support) |
| Positioning | The one idea you want to own | "Cash flow visibility in a week" |
| Content pillars | 3 to 4 recurring themes | How-tos, client results, industry takes, team |
| Formats and cadence | What and how often | 3 posts a week, 1 short video, 1 carousel |
| Paid support | What gets budget and when | Boost top posts monthly, lead gen ads quarterly |
| Roles | Who creates, approves, replies | Founder posts, marketer edits and schedules |
| Measurement | Metrics and review rhythm | Leads, cost per lead, pipeline, monthly review |

## Your audience comes first

Every choice above depends on a clear [target audience](/glossary/target-audience/). A plan aimed at "small businesses" will produce generic posts. A plan aimed at "operations managers at 50 to 200 person logistics firms in Lagos who are moving off spreadsheets" will produce posts those people stop to read.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Posting every day is the key to growth on social.
Fact: Volume without a clear audience and message just adds noise. A consistent schedule of fewer, sharper posts tends to build more engagement from the right people.
:::

## How to create a social media marketing strategy

:::steps "Build it in a week"
1. Audit what you have | List your profiles, recent results and what competitors do well. Archive or close profiles you can't maintain.
2. Set the goal and targets | Choose one primary outcome and a realistic monthly number.
3. Define the audience and message | One audience per plan, one core idea, three or four content pillars.
4. Choose platforms and formats | Pick one main platform and one supporting one. Plan native formats for each.
5. Plan the paid layer | Decide which content gets budget, for what objective, and when.
6. Set up tracking | Tag every link with our [UTM generator](/tools/free-utm-parameter-generator/) and connect leads to your CRM.
7. Schedule a monthly review | Keep what works, cut what doesn't, and rewrite the plan each quarter.
:::

## How to measure it

Track three layers: reach (impressions, followers in your audience), engagement (comments and shares from relevant people, profile visits), and outcomes (clicks, leads, pipeline, sales). Report outcomes first. Use [marketing attribution](/glossary/marketing-attribution/) and "how did you hear about us?" answers, because many social-influenced deals arrive through other channels.

:::faq
### What are the 5 Ps of a social media strategy?
Different frameworks use different Ps, often purpose, people, platform, plan and performance. They're a reminder of the core questions: why, who, where, what and how you'll measure.
### How long should a social media strategy be?
One page for the core plan, with a separate content calendar. If it's longer, it's probably a report, not a strategy.
### How often should I update my social media strategy?
Review results monthly and revise the strategy quarterly, or sooner if a platform changes how it distributes content.
### Can social media marketing improve SEO?
Not directly as a ranking factor, but social activity drives branded searches, links and mentions, which support search visibility and AI answers.
:::
