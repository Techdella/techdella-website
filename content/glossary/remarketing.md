Remarketing is advertising to people who already know you: visitors to your site, app users, video viewers, or contacts on your email list. Instead of reaching strangers, you show a relevant follow-up to someone who's already shown interest, such as a case study to a pricing-page visitor or a reminder to someone who started a sign-up. Remarketing is usually the most efficient part of a paid program because the audience is warm.

:::takeaways
- Most visitors don't convert on the first visit. Remarketing is how you get a second chance.
- Segment by what people did. A pricing-page visitor and a blog reader need different messages.
- Consent and privacy rules shape what you can track, so set them up before building lists.
:::

## Remarketing vs retargeting

The terms are used interchangeably. Google calls it remarketing, and in Google Ads the lists are now called "your data segments." Some marketers use "retargeting" for ads and "remarketing" for email follow-ups. The idea is the same: re-engage people who have interacted with you.

## How remarketing works

1. A tag (such as the Google tag or Meta Pixel) records visits and actions, or you upload a customer list.
2. The platform builds audiences from those signals, for example "visited pricing in the last 30 days."
3. You show ads only to that audience, often with a different message per segment.

On Google Ads, the platform's own rules set the limits:

:::stats "Google Ads data segment rules" highlight=0
30 days | default membership duration for website visitor segments | Google Ads Help, 2026 | https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472738
540 days | maximum membership duration | Google Ads Help, 2026 | https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472738
100 | minimum active users in the last 30 days for a segment to serve on Search, Display or YouTube | Google Ads Help, 2026 | https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472738
:::

## Privacy in 2026

Third-party cookies haven't disappeared from Chrome. In April 2025 Google said it would keep its current approach to third-party cookie choice and not roll out a new standalone prompt ([Privacy Sandbox blog, 2025](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-next-steps)). But Safari and Firefox block them by default, many users decline tracking, and consent rules in the EU and UK apply. The practical answer: rely more on first-party data (site tags with consent, customer lists, video and social engagement) and use Google's consent mode where required.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Remarketing means showing the same ad to everyone who visited until they buy.
Fact: That's how you annoy people and waste budget. Good remarketing changes the message by segment, caps frequency and stops once someone converts.
:::

## How to set up remarketing

:::steps "A remarketing setup that respects budget and people"
1. Install tags with consent | Google tag, Meta Pixel and LinkedIn Insight Tag, connected to your consent banner.
2. Build segments by intent | High: pricing, demo or sign-up pages. Medium: service pages. Low: blog readers.
3. Match the message to the segment | Pricing visitors get proof and a direct offer. Blog readers get a useful guide.
4. Set durations by sales cycle | Short for quick purchases, longer for B2B cycles, within the platform's limits.
5. Cap frequency and exclude converters | Remove customers and recent leads so you don't pay to reach them.
6. Send clicks to a matching page | A focused [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/) that continues the conversation.
:::

## How to measure it

Remarketing often looks great on last-click reports because it reaches people who might have returned anyway. Use [marketing attribution](/glossary/marketing-attribution/) views beyond last click, and where volume allows, run a holdout test: exclude part of the audience and compare conversion rates.

:::faq
### Is remarketing creepy to customers?
It can be if ads follow people everywhere for months. Keep frequency low, make ads helpful, stop after conversion, and respect consent choices.
### How much does remarketing cost?
Usually less per conversion than prospecting, because the audience is warm. The total depends on how much traffic you have to build audiences from.
### Do I need a lot of traffic for remarketing?
On Google Ads, a segment needs at least 100 active users in the last 30 days to serve. Smaller sites can combine segments or use longer durations.
### Can I remarket to my email list?
Yes, through customer list uploads on Google, Meta and LinkedIn, as long as you have the right consent and follow each platform's policies.
:::
