Customer lifetime value (CLV) is your best estimate of what a customer is worth over the whole time they buy from you. It answers a practical question: how much can we afford to spend to win and keep a customer like this? Calculated on gross profit rather than revenue, it's the number that keeps acquisition spending honest.

:::takeaways
- CLV is a forecast, not a fact. Use simple, transparent assumptions and update them as data comes in.
- Calculate it on gross margin, not revenue, or you'll overspend on acquisition.
- Segment it. CLV often varies a lot by customer type, channel and plan.
- Retention is usually the biggest lever: a longer relationship multiplies everything else.
:::

## Three ways to calculate CLV

Pick the one that matches your business model and data.

| Model | Formula | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | Average order value × purchases per year × years retained × gross margin | Repeat-purchase businesses |
| Subscription | (Average monthly revenue per account × gross margin) ÷ monthly churn rate | SaaS and retainers |
| Cohort-based | Sum of actual gross profit per customer in a cohort, month by month | Teams with 12+ months of data |

The subscription formula works because, on average, a customer stays about 1 ÷ churn months. At 2% monthly churn, that's 50 months.

### A worked B2B example

An illustrative agency retainer client pays $3,000 a month. Gross margin on the work is 50%, so each month contributes $1,500. Monthly churn across retainer clients is 4%.

CLV = $1,500 ÷ 0.04 = **$37,500** in gross profit.

If your [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) for these clients is $7,500, the CLV to CAC ratio is 5:1. If churn rose to 8%, CLV would halve to $18,750 and the ratio would fall to 2.5:1. Retention changes the economics more than almost anything else.

## Why customer lifetime value matters

CLV sets the ceiling on acquisition spend. Without it, teams judge channels on cost per lead and starve the ones that bring fewer but more valuable customers. It also shows which customers to look for: if one segment's CLV is triple another's, your targeting and [market segmentation](/glossary/market-segmentation/) should reflect that.

The link between retention and profit has been studied for decades. One often-cited example comes from Fred Reichheld and Christine Detrick of Bain & Company, writing in Marketing Management in 2003.

:::stats "Why retention matters" highlight=0
25%+ | increase in profit from a 5% increase in customer retention, in financial services | Reichheld and Detrick, Bain & Company, 2003 | https://media.bain.com/Images/MM_Loyalty_prescription_cutting_costs.pdf
:::

The study is old and specific to financial services, so treat the exact number as illustrative of the direction, not a forecast for your business.

:::myth-fact
Myth: CLV is total revenue from a customer.
Fact: Revenue-based CLV overstates value. Use gross profit, after the direct cost of delivering the product or service.
Myth: One CLV number is enough.
Fact: An average hides big differences. Calculate CLV by segment, plan or acquisition channel to see where the value really is.
:::

## How to increase customer lifetime value

:::steps "Raise CLV without discounting"
1. Improve onboarding | The first 90 days decide whether customers stay. Set clear early milestones and check in when they're reached.
2. Show results regularly | Send short, specific progress reports. Customers who see value renew.
3. Nurture with email | Use [email marketing](/glossary/email-marketing/) for onboarding sequences, useful updates and timely offers based on what each customer uses.
4. Offer the next logical step | Expansion should solve the customer's next problem, not just raise the bill.
5. Ask for referrals | Happy customers bring similar customers, often with higher CLV. See [referral marketing](/glossary/referral-marketing/).
6. Learn from churn | Interview customers who leave. Fix the top reason before spending more on acquisition.
:::

:::faq
### What is a good customer lifetime value?
A good CLV is one comfortably higher than what it costs to acquire the customer. In SaaS, a widely used guideline is CLV at least three times CAC. The absolute number matters less than the ratio and how quickly you recover your acquisition spend.
### What is the difference between CLV and LTV?
They're the same concept. CLV (customer lifetime value) and LTV (lifetime value) are used interchangeably. Some teams also use CLTV. What matters is that everyone uses the same formula, ideally based on gross margin.
### How often should I calculate CLV?
Recalculate quarterly, or whenever pricing, margins or churn change significantly. Early-stage companies with little history should treat CLV as a rough estimate and revisit it as real retention data builds up.
### Can customer lifetime value be negative?
Yes. If a customer costs more to serve than they pay, for example through heavy support, discounts or refunds, their lifetime gross profit can be negative. Segmenting CLV helps you spot and fix these accounts.
:::
