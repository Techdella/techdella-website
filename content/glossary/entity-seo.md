Entity SEO is the practice of helping search engines and AI systems recognize your brand, your people and your products as distinct, well-understood "things", each with consistent facts and clear relationships to other things. Keywords tell Google what a page is about. Entities tell it who is behind the page and how that connects to everything else it knows.

## Why entity SEO matters

Google moved from matching words to understanding things over a decade ago. When it introduced the Knowledge Graph in May 2012, it called the shift "things, not strings".

:::stats "The Knowledge Graph at launch"
500M+ | objects (people, places, things) in Google's Knowledge Graph at launch | Google, 2012 | https://blog.google/products/search/introducing-knowledge-graph-things-not/
3.5B+ | facts about and relationships between those objects | Google, 2012 | https://blog.google/products/search/introducing-knowledge-graph-things-not/
:::

Two reasons this matters more now. First, AI answers name brands, not pages: if a model can't tell your company from a similarly named one, you won't be recommended accurately (see [LLM visibility](/glossary/llm-visibility/) and [GEO](/glossary/geo/)). Second, consistent entity facts are a trust signal. Conflicting founders, addresses or service lists across the web make you look less certain.

## How entity SEO works

Search engines build an entity from many sources and connect it to others. Your job is to make the sources agree.

| Entity signal | Where it lives | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Site, social profiles, directories | One spelling and format everywhere |
| Description | Home, About, LinkedIn, directories | The same one-sentence "who we are" |
| Identity links | Organization schema sameAs | Links to your official LinkedIn, X, YouTube and other profiles |
| People | About page, author pages, Person schema | Real founders and authors with linked profiles |
| Relationships | Content, schema, partner pages | Clear links to services, industries, locations and clients |
| Corroboration | Third-party sites | Press, reviews and partners repeating the same facts |

Structured data is the most direct way to declare these facts. Google documents Organization markup, including logo, sameAs, address and contact fields, in its [structured data docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization). Accurate [schema markup](/glossary/schema-markup/) doesn't guarantee a knowledge panel, but it removes guesswork.

## Example: a naming collision

Imagine a hypothetical agency called "Apex Growth" in Lagos. There are dozens of companies named Apex worldwide. Searching the brand shows a US logistics firm's knowledge panel. An AI assistant asked about "Apex Growth's services" mixes the two.

The fix is entity work, the kind we include in our [SEO service](/services/seo/), not more keywords:

- Use the full name "Apex Growth Partners" consistently, with the same description.
- Add Organization schema with sameAs links to the official LinkedIn and Instagram pages.
- Link the founder's Person profile to the company and vice versa.
- Update the five directories that list the old name and an outdated address.

After recrawl, the brand query and AI answers have one consistent set of facts to draw on.

## How to do entity SEO

:::checklist "Entity audit for your brand"
- Write one canonical description: who you are, what you do, for whom, where
- Use it verbatim on your home page, About page, LinkedIn and key directories
- Add Organization schema with name, url, logo and sameAs on every page, emitted once
- Add Person schema for founders and authors, linked to real profiles
- Give every author a profile page with role, expertise and links
- Search your brand in Google and three AI assistants; list every wrong fact
- Fix the sources of wrong facts: old directories, press bios, partner pages
- Build topical depth with [topic clusters](/glossary/topic-clusters/) around the services you want to be known for
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Entity SEO means stuffing schema markup onto every page.
Fact: Google says structured data must match visible content. Markup declares facts; consistent pages and third-party sources are what make search engines believe them.
:::

:::faq
### Do I need a Wikipedia page for entity SEO?
No. Wikipedia is one strong source, but its notability rules exclude most small businesses. Consistent facts on your site, schema, LinkedIn, directories and credible press are enough to define your brand entity.
### How do I get a Google knowledge panel?
You can't request one directly. Panels appear when Google is confident about an entity. Consistent entity facts, Organization schema and third-party coverage increase that confidence. If a panel exists, you can claim it through Google's verification process.
### Is entity SEO the same as semantic SEO?
They overlap. Semantic SEO covers meaning and topic relationships in content. Entity SEO focuses on identifying and connecting specific entities, such as your company, people and products.
### Does entity SEO help with AI search?
Yes. AI tools describe brands from what the web says about them. Clear, consistent entity facts reduce errors and make it easier for tools to connect you to the right topics. See [LLM visibility](/glossary/llm-visibility/).
:::
