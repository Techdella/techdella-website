Search engine marketing (SEM) is paying to appear in search results when people look for what you sell. You choose keywords, write ads, set a budget, and pay when someone clicks. Most SEM happens on Google Ads, with Microsoft Advertising covering Bing and partner sites. Some people use SEM to mean all search marketing, including SEO, but in day-to-day use it usually means paid search.

:::takeaways
- SEM puts you at the top of search results within days. SEO takes longer but keeps working without paying per click.
- Ad position is decided by Ad Rank, calculated at auction time from your bid, ad quality and other factors, not by bid alone.
- Quality Score is a diagnostic, not an input to the auction. Use it to find weak keywords, ads and landing pages.
- Budget from your target cost per lead backwards, not from a round number.
:::

## How the search ads auction works

Each time someone searches, Google runs an auction among advertisers whose keywords match. According to [Google's Ad Rank documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722122), Ad Rank is calculated first to decide whether your ad can show at all, and then again to decide its position. It's based on factors including your bid, the quality of your ad and landing page, the context of the search, and the expected impact of your ad assets.

[Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), the 1 to 10 rating shown per keyword, is built from expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google is explicit that it's a diagnostic tool, not an input to the auction. A low score points to what's hurting you; fixing the underlying issue is what helps.

### A worked budget example

Using those averages as a starting point (your industry will differ): a $3,000 monthly budget at $5.42 per click buys about 553 clicks. At an 8.18% conversion rate, that's about 45 leads, or roughly $66 per lead. If your sales team closes 1 in 10 leads, that's 4 or 5 new customers, so your paid search [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) is about $670 before any agency or tool fees. Run this arithmetic with your own numbers before you set a budget.

## SEM vs SEO

| | SEM (paid search) | SEO (organic search) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Days | Months |
| Cost model | Pay per click | Pay for content and technical work |
| When you stop paying | Traffic stops | Traffic decays slowly |
| Best for | Testing offers, high-intent keywords, launches | Long-term demand capture, informational queries |
| Measured by | Cost per lead, return on ad spend | Organic leads, qualified traffic |

Most B2B companies benefit from both. Paid search data, such as which keywords convert, is also a fast input for [keyword research](/glossary/keyword-research/) on the SEO side.

:::myth-fact
Myth: The highest bid always wins the top spot.
Fact: Ad Rank includes ad quality, landing page experience and search context, so a more relevant ad can outrank a higher bid.
Myth: SEM only works for big budgets.
Fact: Small budgets work when they're focused on a few high-intent keywords, tight locations and one strong landing page. Spreading a small budget across broad terms is what fails.
:::

## How to plan your first search engine marketing campaign

:::steps "First campaign in six steps"
1. Set the target | Decide the cost per lead you can afford from your close rate and deal value.
2. Pick high-intent keywords | Start with terms that signal buying, such as "[service] pricing" or "[service] agency", and add negative keywords for job seekers and free-tool hunters.
3. Write focused ads | Match the keyword in the headline, state a clear benefit and add relevant assets such as sitelinks.
4. Build a matching landing page | One page per offer with a message that mirrors the ad. See [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/).
5. Track conversions properly | Import form submissions or qualified leads from your CRM, not just page views.
6. Review weekly | Cut search terms that spend without converting, and move budget to what works.
:::

:::faq
### How much should I spend on search engine marketing?
Work backwards from your goals. Decide how many customers you need, your close rate and your target cost per lead, then estimate clicks needed using your industry's CPC and conversion rate. Start with a test budget for four to eight weeks before scaling.
### Is SEM better than SEO?
Neither is better in general. SEM is faster and easier to control, which suits testing and urgent demand. SEO is slower but keeps bringing traffic without per-click costs. Most B2B companies use SEM for high-intent keywords while SEO builds.
### How quickly can SEM bring results?
Ads can show within a day of approval. Meaningful data takes a few weeks, because campaigns need enough clicks and conversions to show which keywords and ads perform. Smart bidding strategies also need conversion data before they work well.
### Should I advertise on Microsoft Bing as well as Google?
Often yes, once Google campaigns are working. Microsoft Advertising lets you import Google Ads campaigns, so setup is quick. Test it with a small share of budget and compare cost per lead.
:::
