A sales funnel maps the path from first contact to signed customer. At the top are all the leads you've captured. At each stage, some drop out: they aren't a fit, they aren't ready, or they choose someone else. At the bottom are the customers who buy. Measuring how many move from each stage to the next shows you where to focus and how much pipeline you need to hit a target.

:::takeaways
- A sales funnel starts once someone becomes a lead. The stages before that belong to the marketing funnel.
- Define each stage with a clear entry rule, so everyone counts the same way.
- Stage-to-stage conversion rates let you work backwards from a revenue goal to the leads you need.
- Fix the leakiest stage first; it usually beats adding more leads at the top.
:::

## Sales funnel vs marketing funnel

The two overlap but answer different questions. The [digital marketing funnel](/glossary/digital-marketing-funnel/) covers how strangers become aware, interested and ready to raise their hand. The sales funnel starts at that hand-raise and tracks qualification, proposals and deals. In a small company, one person may run both. They still need separate stages and metrics.

## The stages of a B2B sales funnel

| Stage | Entry rule (example) | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | Submitted a form or replied to outreach | Marketing |
| Marketing-qualified lead (MQL) | Fits the ideal customer profile and showed intent | Marketing |
| Sales-qualified lead (SQL) | Sales confirmed need, budget range and timing in a call | Sales |
| Opportunity / proposal | A scoped proposal was sent | Sales |
| Closed won | Contract signed | Sales |
| Retention and expansion | Renewed, upgraded or referred others | Account management |

Your stage names can differ. What matters is that each has a written entry rule and one owner. The last stage matters more than many teams admit: [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/) is built after the sale.

## Why a sales funnel matters

It turns "we need more sales" into a specific problem. Maybe you have plenty of leads but few become SQLs: that's a targeting or qualification issue. Maybe proposals stall: that's pricing, proof or competition. Each diagnosis leads to a different fix.

Consistency between stages also matters to buyers. Gartner found that many buyers notice when the story changes between your website and your sales team.

:::stats "What buyers notice" highlight=0
69% | of B2B buyers report inconsistencies between the seller's website and what sales reps tell them | Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers, 2025 | https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Buyers move through the funnel in a straight line.
Fact: Real B2B buyers loop back, involve new people and pause. The funnel is a measurement tool, not a description of every buyer's journey.
Myth: The fix for a weak funnel is always more leads.
Fact: If 2% of leads become customers, doubling leads doubles waste too. Improving a leaky middle stage often adds more revenue for less money.
:::

## How to build and use your sales funnel

### Work backwards from a revenue goal

An illustrative example. You want $240,000 in new annual revenue, and your average deal is $20,000 a year. You need 12 new customers.

| Stage | Conversion to next stage | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Closed won | | 12 |
| Proposals | 40% close | 30 |
| SQLs | 50% get a proposal | 60 |
| MQLs | 30% become SQLs | 200 |
| Leads | 40% become MQLs | 500 |

Each "Needed" number is the one below it divided by the conversion rate: 12 ÷ 0.40 = 30, 30 ÷ 0.50 = 60, and so on. Put your own rates in and you know how many leads your [lead generation](/glossary/lead-generation/) has to produce. If you raise MQL-to-SQL from 30% to 40%, you need 150 MQLs instead of 200, and 375 leads instead of 500.

:::steps "Set up your funnel in a week"
1. Write stage definitions | Agree entry rules for each stage with sales and marketing, in writing.
2. Build them into your CRM | Each deal or contact should sit in exactly one stage, with dates when it moved.
3. Pull three months of history | Calculate conversion between stages and average time spent in each.
4. Find the biggest leak | Compare each stage's conversion with your expectations and past quarters.
5. Fix one stage at a time | Change the offer, follow-up speed, qualification or proposal, then measure the next cohort.
:::

:::faq
### What are the stages of a sales funnel?
A common B2B version is lead, marketing-qualified lead, sales-qualified lead, opportunity or proposal, closed won, and retention or expansion. Simpler businesses may use awareness, interest, decision and action. The right stages are the ones your team can define clearly and measure.
### What is a good sales funnel conversion rate?
It varies by industry, deal size and lead source, so there's no single benchmark. Compare your stage rates with your own history and between lead sources. Referral leads, for example, often convert better than cold ones, which tells you where to invest.
### What is the difference between a sales funnel and a pipeline?
They describe the same flow from different angles. The funnel shows conversion rates between stages across many deals. The pipeline shows the specific open deals and their value right now. Use the funnel for planning and the pipeline for forecasting.
### How do I find where my funnel is leaking?
Calculate the conversion rate and average time for each stage over the last few months. The stage with the lowest conversion compared with your expectations, or where deals sit longest, is the leak. Then read the lost-deal notes for that stage to learn why.
:::
