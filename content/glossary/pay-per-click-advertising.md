Pay-per-click (PPC) advertising is a way of buying traffic where you pay only when someone clicks your ad. The best-known form is search advertising on Google and Bing: you choose keywords, write ads, set bids, and your ad can appear when someone searches for those terms. Social platforms and marketplaces also sell clicks. PPC is popular because it reaches people at the moment they're looking, and because every click, cost and conversion can be measured.

:::takeaways
- You don't win placements on bid alone. Ad quality and relevance also decide where you appear and what you pay.
- Search PPC captures existing demand. It can't create demand that doesn't exist yet.
- Work out your affordable cost per click from your conversion rate and customer value before you launch.
:::

## How pay-per-click advertising works

Every time a search triggers ads, an auction runs in a fraction of a second. On Google Ads, your position depends on your bid and on quality signals. Google summarizes quality as a Quality Score from 1 to 10, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454010)). Higher quality usually means better positions for the same or lower cost.

PPC sits inside a large and growing market: US internet advertising revenue reached $259 billion in 2024, up 15% on the year ([IAB and PwC, 2025](https://www.iab.com/insights/internet-advertising-revenue-report-full-year-2024/)).

## PPC platforms compared

| Platform | Strength | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Google Ads (Search) | High intent: people asking for a solution | Capturing demand for services and products |
| Microsoft Advertising | Similar intent, often lower competition | Extra reach, desktop-heavy B2B audiences |
| LinkedIn Ads | Targeting by job title, seniority and company | B2B offers where who matters more than what they search |
| Meta and TikTok Ads | Visual reach and interest targeting | Creating demand, retargeting |

Social platforms often bill per impression by default, but many campaigns can optimize for clicks, leads or conversions.

## How much to bid: a worked example

| Input | Example |
|---|---|
| Value of a new client (first-year gross profit) | $3,000 |
| Lead-to-client rate | 10% |
| Value of one lead | $300 |
| Landing page conversion rate (click to lead) | 5% |
| Break-even cost per click | $300 × 5% = $15 |

If clicks in your market cost $8, you have room to profit. If they cost $25, improve the page, the offer or the targeting before scaling. Use your own numbers, then compare the result with your target [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/).

:::myth-fact
Myth: The highest bidder always gets the top spot.
Fact: Ad quality and relevance count too. A more relevant ad and a better landing page can outrank a higher bid and pay less per click.
:::

## How to launch a PPC campaign

:::steps "Seven steps to a clean launch"
1. Set up conversion tracking first | Track form fills, calls and purchases in Google Ads and GA4, with consent mode where required.
2. Research keywords by intent | Start with high-intent terms. See [keyword research](/glossary/keyword-research/).
3. Structure tightly | Group closely related keywords so each ad matches the search.
4. Add negative keywords | Exclude irrelevant searches such as "jobs," "free" or "course" if they don't fit.
5. Write ads that mirror the search | Use the buyer's words, a clear benefit and proof.
6. Send clicks to a matching page | A focused [landing page](/glossary/landing-page/) with one call to action, not your homepage.
7. Review weekly, optimize monthly | Check search terms, cut waste, test ads, and shift budget toward what converts.
:::

## PPC and SEO together

PPC brings traffic in days and stops when the budget stops. SEO takes months and keeps working. Use PPC to test which keywords convert, then invest in SEO for the winners. Add [remarketing](/glossary/remarketing/) to bring back visitors who didn't convert the first time.

:::faq
### How much should I budget for PPC?
Start with enough to get meaningful data, roughly 30 or more clicks per keyword group per month, at your market's cost per click. Then scale what converts.
### Is PPC better than SEO?
Neither is better. PPC is faster and easier to control; SEO is slower and compounds. Most businesses benefit from both.
### How quickly will I see results from PPC?
Traffic starts within days of approval. Reliable conversion data usually takes four to eight weeks.
### Do I need a landing page for PPC?
Yes. Sending ad clicks to a page built for that search usually converts better and improves landing page experience, one of the parts of Quality Score.
:::
