A target audience is the specific group of people a piece of marketing is built to reach. It's narrower than your whole market: a campaign, landing page or email sequence each has its own target audience. You define it by who they are (role, company, location), what problem they have, and what makes them ready to act. Get it right and every later decision, from channel to headline to offer, gets easier.

:::takeaways
- Your target market is everyone who could buy. Your target audience is who a specific piece of marketing speaks to.
- In B2B, define audiences by role and problem first, demographics second.
- Build audiences from evidence: sales calls, CRM data and what buyers say in public, not guesses.
:::

## Target audience vs target market vs persona

These three get mixed up all the time. The difference matters, because each answers a different question.

| Term | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Target market | Who could buy from us at all? | Fintech startups in Nigeria and the US |
| Target audience | Who is this campaign for? | Heads of growth at seed-stage fintechs planning a launch |
| Buyer persona | What is one typical member like? | "Ada, 32, Head of Growth, reports to the CEO, owns the CAC target" |

A [market segmentation](/glossary/market-segmentation/) exercise splits the market into groups. You then choose which segment becomes the target audience for a given campaign.

## Why your target audience matters

Without one, you write for everyone and convince no one. Ad platforms make this costly fast: a broad audience on LinkedIn or Meta spends budget on people who will never buy. A defined audience lets you pick the right channel, write in the buyer's language and judge results fairly. It also sharpens your site for AI search: pages that say plainly who they're for and what problem they solve are easier for AI tools to match to a buyer's question.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A broader audience means more leads.
Fact: A broader audience usually means more clicks and fewer qualified leads. Narrowing the audience often lowers cost per qualified lead even when cost per click rises.
:::

## Ways to define a target audience

- **Firmographic:** industry, company size, funding stage, region. The base layer in B2B.
- **Role-based:** job title, seniority and who owns the budget.
- **Problem-based:** the trigger that creates the need, such as a new market launch, a failed agency or a hiring freeze.
- **Behavioral:** what they've already done: visited your pricing page, downloaded a guide, attended a webinar.
- **Psychographic:** values and attitudes, such as risk appetite or preference for in-house versus outsourced work.

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
The most useful audience work I do with founders is listening to their last five sales calls, not filling in a persona template. Who signed, what triggered the search, which words did they use? Narrowing the audience feels risky, but it makes every headline, ad and email sharper. You can always widen later once one segment is working.
:::

## How to find your target audience

:::steps "From guesswork to evidence"
1. Mine your best customers | List your 10 most valuable clients. Note role, company type, trigger and the words they used on the first call.
2. Read your CRM and analytics | Look for patterns in who converts, who churns and which pages they read before buying.
3. Listen in public | Use [social listening](/glossary/social-listening/) and review sites to collect the exact phrases buyers use about the problem.
4. Write the audience in one sentence | "Who + problem + trigger." If you can't, you have more than one audience.
5. Build a persona for each audience | Our free [persona builder](/tools/persona-builder/) turns your notes into a one-page profile the team can share.
6. Test and refine | Run a small campaign, check lead quality with sales, and adjust before scaling spend.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Defining the audience only by age and gender. In B2B, the role and the trigger matter far more.
- Copying a competitor's audience. Their best customers may not be yours.
- Never revisiting it. Audiences drift as you add services, enter markets or move upmarket. Review them at least once a year.
- Treating one persona as the whole buying group. Most B2B purchases involve several people, which is where [account-based marketing](/glossary/account-based-marketing/) helps.

:::faq
### What is an example of a target audience?
"Marketing leads at B2B SaaS companies with 20 to 200 staff who are launching in the US this year." It names a role, a company type and a trigger, which is enough to choose channels and write copy.
### How many target audiences should a business have?
Most small businesses do well with two or three. Each one should get its own message and, ideally, its own landing page.
### What's the difference between a target audience and a target market?
The target market is everyone who could buy. A target audience is the slice of that market a specific campaign or page is written for.
### How do I find my target audience on social media?
Start with your customer list, then check who engages with you and with competitors. Platform analytics and listening tools show job titles, interests and the language people use.
:::
