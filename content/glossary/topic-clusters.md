A topic cluster is a way of organizing content so one subject is covered thoroughly and connected clearly. A broad pillar page gives the overview. Several cluster pages each answer one specific question in depth. Every cluster page links to the pillar, and the pillar links back. The structure helps readers move from a general question to a specific one, and it helps search engines see which page is your main page for the topic.

:::takeaways
- One pillar page per topic, with 5 to 15 supporting pages that each target one narrower query.
- Internal links do the work: every cluster page links to the pillar and to its closest siblings.
- Build clusters around services you sell, so the pillar can lead to a sales page.
- Clusters prevent two pages competing for the same keyword.
:::

## Why topic clusters matter

Random blog posts compete with each other and leave gaps. A cluster forces you to plan: which question does each page answer, and which page is the main one? That planning reduces [keyword cannibalization](/glossary/keyword-cannibalization/) and makes it obvious where content is missing.

Clusters also match how people research. A buyer reads "what is B2B SEO", then "how long does SEO take", then "SEO agency pricing". If all three pages exist and link to each other, you keep that reader on your site through the whole journey.

There's an AI-search angle too. AI answers often break a question into several sub-questions. A site that answers the main question and its follow-ups clearly has more pages that can be found and cited for each part.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Topic clusters are a Google ranking factor.
Fact: Google doesn't reward a "cluster" label. What helps is the result: complete coverage, clear internal links, and no duplicate pages competing for one query.
Myth: A bigger cluster is always better.
Fact: Ten useful pages beat forty thin ones. Google's spam policies call out scaled content created mainly to manipulate rankings.
:::

## How to build a topic cluster

:::steps "Plan one cluster in an afternoon"
1. Pick a topic tied to revenue | Choose a subject close to a service you sell, broad enough for 5 to 15 subtopics.
2. List the questions | Use keyword research, sales calls and the "People also ask" box to list every question buyers have on the topic.
3. Choose the pillar | The broadest question with the most search demand becomes the pillar page. It covers each subtopic briefly.
4. Assign one page per question | Each remaining question gets its own cluster page, unless two questions share the same answer.
5. Link deliberately | Each cluster page links to the pillar with descriptive anchor text and to one or two related cluster pages.
6. Connect to the money page | The pillar links to the relevant service page, so readers have a next step.
:::

### A worked example

Here is a cluster for an agency that sells email marketing. The structure, not the exact titles, is what matters.

| Page | Role | Target query |
|---|---|---|
| B2B email marketing guide | Pillar | b2b email marketing |
| How to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC | Cluster | spf dkim dmarc setup |
| Welcome email sequence template | Cluster | welcome email sequence |
| Email deliverability checklist | Cluster | email deliverability |
| How often to send B2B newsletters | Cluster | b2b newsletter frequency |
| Email marketing services | Money page | email marketing services |

Every cluster page links up to the pillar. The pillar links down to every cluster page and across to the service page. Before writing, run a [content gap](/glossary/content-gap/) check to see which cluster pages competitors already have.

:::checklist "Cluster health check"
- Every cluster page links to the pillar in its first few paragraphs
- No two pages target the same primary keyword
- The pillar links to every cluster page and to one service page
- Each page is reviewed at least once a year
:::

:::faq
### How many pages should a topic cluster have?
Enough to answer the real questions buyers ask, usually 5 to 15 supporting pages. Let the question list decide. If two questions have the same answer, they belong on the same page. If a cluster grows past 20 pages, it's often two topics.
### What is the difference between a pillar page and a cluster page?
A pillar page covers a broad topic at a summary level and links out to detail. A cluster page goes deep on one narrow question and links back to the pillar. The pillar targets the broad keyword; each cluster page targets a specific long-tail one.
### Can I turn existing blog posts into a topic cluster?
Yes, and it's often the fastest win. Group related posts, pick or write a pillar, merge posts that overlap, redirect the merged URLs, and add the missing internal links. Many sites already have the content but not the structure.
### Do topic clusters work for small websites?
Yes. A small site may have one or two clusters, one per core service. Even three or four well-linked pages around a single topic are clearer to readers and search engines than ten unrelated posts.
:::
