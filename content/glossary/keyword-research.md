Keyword research tells you what your buyers actually type when they have a problem you solve. Done well, it gives you a short list of topics, each with a clear purpose and one target page. Done badly, it gives you a spreadsheet of high-volume words nobody on your team can turn into revenue.

:::takeaways
- Start from customer problems and sales calls, then use tools to size them.
- Intent beats volume. A 50-search keyword that signals buying can be worth more than a 5,000-search one that doesn't.
- Score keywords on business value, not just volume and difficulty.
- Map one primary keyword to one page to avoid cannibalization.
:::

## Why keyword research matters

Without it, teams write about what they find interesting rather than what buyers search for. Most pages then get no search traffic at all: Ahrefs found that 96.55% of the roughly 14 billion pages in its Content Explorer index got zero visits from Google.

Search behavior is also shifting. AI summaries now appear on a share of Google results, and people click less when they do. That makes intent research more important: you want queries where a buyer still needs to compare, evaluate or contact someone, not just get a one-line fact.

## The four things to judge for every keyword

| Signal | Question to ask | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | What does the searcher want: learn, compare, buy, or log in? | Look at the current top results |
| Demand | Roughly how many searches a month? | Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Console |
| Difficulty | How strong are the pages already ranking? | Tool difficulty score plus a manual look at the results |
| Business value | If we ranked, would it lead to a sale? | Your sales team and CRM |

Tools disagree on volume and difficulty, so treat them as ranges, not facts. Intent and business value come from judgment, and that's where most of the edge is.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Pick the keywords with the highest search volume.
Fact: High-volume terms are usually broad and informational. Specific, long-tail queries often convert better because the searcher already knows what they need.
Myth: You need the exact keyword repeated many times on the page.
Fact: Google understands synonyms and related phrasing. Use the keyword in the title, first paragraph and one heading, then write naturally.
:::

## How to do keyword research

:::steps "A keyword research process for a small B2B team"
1. Collect seed topics from buyers | Pull phrases from sales calls, support tickets, proposals and lost-deal notes. These are the words customers use, not the words you use.
2. Expand with tools | Put the seeds into a keyword tool and Search Console to find variations, questions and long-tail phrases.
3. Check the results page | Search each candidate. If the top results are definitions and you sell a service, the intent may not match.
4. Score each keyword | Rate business value and intent fit from 1 to 3, multiply them, and use difficulty as a tie-breaker.
5. Group and map | Group close variants into one topic and assign each group to one page, new or existing.
6. Review quarterly | Re-check rankings, new questions and AI answers for your main topics every quarter.
:::

### A worked scoring example

Here is how the scoring in step 4 plays out for an imaginary payroll software company. Numbers are illustrative.

| Keyword | Monthly searches | Intent fit (1–3) | Business value (1–3) | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| what is payroll | 9,000 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| payroll software for startups | 400 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| how to run payroll in Nigeria | 300 | 2 | 3 | 6 |

The biggest keyword scores lowest. The second keyword becomes a service or product page. The third becomes a guide that links to it. Grouping guides and service pages like this is the basis of [topic clusters](/glossary/topic-clusters/).

:::faq
### What is the best free tool for keyword research?
Start with Google Search Console if your site has traffic: it shows the real queries people used to find you. Google Keyword Planner gives volume ranges for free with an Ads account. Then look at the search results themselves. They show intent better than any tool score.
### How many keywords should one page target?
One primary keyword plus its close variants and related questions. If two keywords need different answers, they need different pages. If the same pages rank for both, one page can usually cover both.
### Is keyword research still useful with AI search?
Yes. AI answers still respond to questions people type, and Google says normal SEO practices apply to AI features. Keyword research now also includes listing the longer, conversational questions buyers ask AI tools and checking who gets mentioned in the answers.
### How often should I redo keyword research?
Do a full pass when you launch a new service or market, and a lighter review every quarter. Search Console will show new queries you're appearing for, which often reveals topics worth their own page.
:::
