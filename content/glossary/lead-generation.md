Lead generation is how a business finds people who might buy and starts a conversation with them. In practice, it's a system: something attracts the right people (content, search, ads, events, referrals), something gives them a reason to share their details (a useful resource, a tool, a consultation), and a process follows up and decides who is worth a sales conversation.

:::takeaways
- B2B buyers now do most of their research alone, so your content and offers do much of the early selling.
- Measure lead quality and pipeline, not just lead count.
- Match the offer to the buyer's stage: guides for early research, audits and calls for buyers ready to talk.
- Irrelevant outreach drives buyers away. Relevance beats volume.
:::

## How B2B buyers behave now

Buyer behavior has shifted toward self-service. That changes how lead generation has to work.

:::stats "What buyers told Gartner" highlight=0
61% | of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience | Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers, 2025 | https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience
73% | of B2B buyers actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach | Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers, 2025 | https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience
:::

The practical meaning: buyers want to find answers on their own terms, then talk to you when they're ready. Inbound channels that meet them during research tend to produce warmer leads than cold volume. One of our own clients, Paycape, saw 50% of its conversions come from inbound, as described in the [Paycape case study](/case-studies/paycape/).

## Inbound vs outbound lead generation

| | Inbound | Outbound |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Buyers find you through search, content, social, referrals | You reach out first: email, calls, LinkedIn messages, ads to cold audiences |
| Speed | Slower to build, compounds over time | Faster to start, stops when you stop |
| Lead warmth | Usually higher; they came to you | Usually lower; you interrupted them |
| Risk | Low | Damaged reputation if outreach is irrelevant |
| Best for | Sustainable pipeline | Targeted accounts, new markets, quick tests |

Most B2B teams need both. Outbound works best when it's narrow and relevant, such as [account-based marketing](/glossary/account-based-marketing/) to a defined list.

:::myth-fact
Myth: More leads always means more sales.
Fact: Volume from the wrong audience burns sales time and inflates acquisition cost. Track how many leads become qualified opportunities and customers.
Myth: Gating every piece of content gets you more leads.
Fact: Gating blocks research-stage buyers who aren't ready to talk. Keep most guides open and gate genuinely valuable resources such as templates, tools or audits.
:::

## How to build a lead generation system

:::steps "Six parts of a working system"
1. Define the ideal customer | Company size, industry, role and the trigger that makes them look for help. Write it down.
2. Create offers by stage | A guide or checklist for early research, a tool or case study for comparison, an audit or call for buyers ready to act.
3. Build capture points | Focused [landing pages](/glossary/landing-page/) and short forms, each tied to one offer.
4. Drive the right traffic | Search, paid ads, LinkedIn, partners and email to existing contacts, chosen by where your buyers research.
5. Qualify and route | Score leads by fit and behavior so sales sees the right ones first. See [lead scoring](/glossary/lead-scoring/).
6. Follow up fast and nurture the rest | Reply to sales-ready leads quickly. Put others into a relevant [email marketing](/glossary/email-marketing/) sequence.
:::

### How to measure lead generation

| Metric | Formula | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | Spend ÷ leads | Efficiency of each channel |
| Lead-to-opportunity rate | Opportunities ÷ leads | Lead quality |
| Cost per opportunity | Spend ÷ opportunities | Better comparison across channels |
| Customer acquisition cost | Total sales and marketing cost ÷ new customers | Whether growth is profitable |

[Customer acquisition cost](/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) is the number that tells you whether your lead generation actually pays.

:::faq
### What is the difference between lead generation and demand generation?
Demand generation builds awareness and interest in a problem and your approach to it, often without asking for contact details. Lead generation captures details from people showing interest. Strong demand generation makes lead generation easier and cheaper.
### What are the best lead generation channels for B2B?
It depends on where your buyers research. Search (organic and paid) captures active demand. LinkedIn suits role-based targeting. Email nurtures existing contacts. Referrals and partners often bring the highest-quality leads. Test two or three channels properly rather than many lightly.
### How much does lead generation cost?
It varies widely by channel, industry and deal size. For search ads, LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average cost per lead across industries at $66.69. Judge cost per lead against the value of a customer, not in isolation.
### What makes a lead qualified?
A qualified lead fits your ideal customer profile and has shown real buying intent, such as requesting a demo or pricing. Many teams split leads into marketing-qualified and sales-qualified stages, with clear definitions agreed between marketing and sales.
:::
