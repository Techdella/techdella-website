Podcast marketing is using podcasts to grow your business. It covers three things: running your own show, appearing as a guest on other people's shows, and buying ads or sponsorships on shows your buyers already listen to. It also covers promoting a podcast so it finds listeners. Podcasts give you something few channels do: 20 to 60 minutes of someone's attention, often while they're commuting or working out.

:::takeaways
- Podcast audiences are large and still growing, and much of the listening now happens on video platforms.
- In B2B, a show is as much a relationship tool as a reach tool: inviting target clients as guests opens doors.
- Plan distribution before you record. Most podcasts fail from lack of promotion, not lack of quality.
:::

## Why podcast marketing matters

80% | of Americans 12+ have ever listened to or watched a podcast | Edison Research, The Infinite Dial 2026 | https://ssrs.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-2026/
58% | consumed a podcast in the last month, about 167 million people | Edison Research, The Infinite Dial 2026 | https://ssrs.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-2026/
45% | consumed one in the last week | Edison Research, The Infinite Dial 2026 | https://ssrs.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-2026/
:::

Listening is habitual and long, which builds familiarity faster than short ads. Hosts become trusted voices, so a recommendation or a thoughtful guest appearance carries weight. For B2B brands, that trust is a direct route to [brand awareness](/glossary/brand-awareness/) among a niche audience.

## Three ways to use podcasts

| Approach | Effort | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your own show | High: weekly or biweekly production | Owning a topic, building relationships with guests | Slow audience growth |
| Guest appearances | Low: prep and one hour | Borrowing an established audience | Picking shows your buyers don't hear |
| Ads and sponsorships | Budget, little production | Reaching a defined niche quickly | Measuring results; use codes and dedicated URLs |

Many B2B teams start with guesting, then launch their own show once they have a clear angle and a list of guests they'd love to know.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A podcast needs thousands of downloads to be worth it.
Fact: For B2B, a show heard by a few hundred of the right people, with target clients as guests, can pay for itself through relationships alone.
:::

## How to promote a podcast

:::steps "A promotion plan for every episode"
1. Launch with three episodes | New listeners can binge, and it signals the show is serious.
2. Publish on video too | Put full episodes on YouTube and cut 30 to 60 second clips for LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok.
3. Build an episode page | A page on your site with a summary, key points, a transcript and links. It helps search and AI tools understand the episode.
4. Let guests share easily | Send each guest clips, a quote card and suggested copy on release day.
5. Use your email list | Feature each episode in your newsletter with one takeaway, not just a link.
6. Swap and guest | Appear on related shows and trade promos with hosts who serve the same audience.
:::

## Podcasts and account-based marketing

Inviting people from your target accounts as guests is one of the most natural [account-based marketing](/glossary/account-based-marketing/) moves available. You give them a platform, learn about their challenges, and build a relationship without a sales pitch. Keep it genuine: the episode has to be useful to listeners, not a disguised demo.

:::faq
### Is podcast advertising worth it?
It can be for niche audiences, because hosts' recommendations carry trust. Use a unique code or URL per show and judge it on leads or sales over a few months, not a single episode.
### How long should a B2B podcast episode be?
Long enough to cover the topic well, usually 20 to 45 minutes. Consistency of schedule matters more than exact length.
### How do I measure podcast marketing?
Track downloads and video views as reach, then add the outcomes: guest relationships that became clients, traffic to episode pages, sign-ups and "how did you hear about us?" answers.
### Do I need expensive equipment to start?
No. A good USB microphone, a quiet room and remote recording software are enough to sound professional.
:::
