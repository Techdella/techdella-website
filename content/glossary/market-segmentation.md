Market segmentation is the process of dividing a broad market into smaller groups of buyers who share similar needs, characteristics or behaviors. Once you see the segments, you can choose which ones to focus on, shape your offer for them, and speak to each in its own language. Market segmentation is what turns "everyone who could buy" into a clear decision about who you're really for.

:::takeaways
- Segmentation is a choice tool. Its value is in deciding which segments to prioritize and which to ignore.
- In B2B, segment by firmographics, needs and buying triggers, not only by demographics.
- A good segment is big enough to matter, reachable, and different enough to need its own message.
:::

## Types of market segmentation

| Type | Splits buyers by | Consumer example | B2B example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographic | Age, income, education, role | Students vs retirees | Founders vs heads of marketing |
| Firmographic | Industry, company size, revenue, stage | — | Seed-stage SaaS vs 500-person manufacturers |
| Geographic | Country, region, city, time zone | Urban vs rural | Nigeria vs US vs UK clients |
| Psychographic | Values, attitudes, priorities | Eco-conscious shoppers | Risk-averse vs growth-at-all-costs teams |
| Behavioral | Usage, purchase history, engagement | Frequent vs occasional buyers | Trial users who invited teammates vs those who didn't |
| Needs-based | The job they need done | Convenience vs price | "Need leads now" vs "need a long-term channel" |

Most useful segmentations combine two or three of these, for example firmographic plus needs-based.

## Why market segmentation matters

Different buyers want different things. Speaking to all of them with one message usually means speaking clearly to none. Segmentation lets you put budget where returns are highest, write messages that match each group's problem, and build offers that fit. It's also the foundation of personalization, which most B2B marketers now practice: 89% say their organization personalizes content ([Content Marketing Institute and MarketingProfs, 2025](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/press-room/new-b2b-research-finds-winning-marketing-teams-are-building-fundamentals-not-just-playing-with-prompts)).

:::myth-fact
Myth: More segments means better targeting.
Fact: Every segment needs its own message, content and budget. Too many segments spread a small team thin. Three to five well-chosen segments usually beat fifteen.
:::

## An example from our work

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
When we start positioning work, I ask the founder to name the customers they'd happily clone. Their answers usually point to one or two segments with a shared, urgent problem. We write the core message for them first and treat everyone else as secondary. It can feel like turning business away, but a message built for a specific buyer tends to land far better than one written for anyone.
:::

## How to segment your market

:::steps "From data to a focus decision"
1. Gather customer data | Export clients from your CRM with industry, size, region, deal value, sales cycle and retention.
2. Look for natural groups | Which customers share needs, triggers and outcomes? Group them, then name each group.
3. Size and score each segment | Score on size, ability to pay, fit with your strengths, ease of reaching them and [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/).
4. Pick your priority segments | Choose one to three. Write down which segments you won't actively pursue.
5. Define the target audience per segment | Turn each priority segment into a specific [target audience](/glossary/target-audience/) and persona. Our [persona builder](/tools/persona-builder/) helps.
6. Tailor and test | Build segment-specific pages, emails and ads. Compare conversion and deal quality after a quarter.
:::

:::checklist "Is it a good segment?"
- Measurable: you can count and identify members
- Substantial: big enough to justify its own effort
- Reachable: you know the channels to reach them
- Different: they respond to a different message or offer
- Actionable: you can serve them well today
:::

:::faq
### What is the main goal of market segmentation?
To decide which groups of buyers to focus on and how to serve each one, so marketing spend goes where it's most likely to pay back.
### How many segments should a business create?
Enough to reflect real differences, but few enough to serve well. Most small and mid-size businesses prioritize two to four.
### Is market segmentation only for large companies?
No. Small businesses benefit most, because they can't afford to market to everyone. Even a simple split by industry or need sharpens messaging.
### How often should segmentation be reviewed?
Once a year, or whenever you launch a new service, enter a new market or see your best customers change.
:::
