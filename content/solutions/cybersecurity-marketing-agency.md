compliance_notes: |
  - Certification and attestation claims: only show SOC 2, ISO 27001, FedRAMP, Cyber Essentials or similar marks you currently hold. Saying “FedRAMP authorized” while “in process,” or using the AICPA SOC logo outside its terms, is misleading. The FTC has acted against companies for deceptive security claims.
  - Ad platform rules: Google Ads prohibits ads for hacking services and spyware under its dishonest behavior and enabling dishonest behavior rules, so offensive security and pen-testing ads need careful wording.
  - Threat research: follow coordinated vulnerability disclosure. Do not publish exploit details for unpatched issues in marketing content, and keep customer incident details anonymized with written approval.
  - Prospecting data: outbound to security teams in the EU and UK falls under GDPR and PECR. Keep data minimal and opt-outs easy, because security buyers notice.

1. **Build a trust center before anything else.** We publish current certifications, data handling, subprocessors and a disclosure policy in one place. Security buyers check this first, and gaps stall deals.
2. **Rewrite core pages around evidence.** We replace broad claims with what the product detects or prevents, how it deploys and where it stops. Skeptical buyers trust specifics and honest limits.
3. **Publish framework and problem pages with your engineers.** We map your product to the controls and problems buyers search for. This earns practitioner traffic and gives AI assistants accurate facts to cite.
4. **Create one practical offer for early evaluators.** A readiness checklist or control-mapping template gives buyers a reason to share their details. Security evaluations start long before a sales call.
5. **Set up account-level scoring and alerts.** Sales hears when several people from one target account are researching. Outreach then reaches buying groups when they are active.
