compliance_notes: |
  - Children's data: in the US, COPPA requires verifiable parental consent before collecting personal data from children under 13, and the FTC updated the COPPA Rule in 2025. In the UK, the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code applies to online services likely to be used by children.
  - Student records: products used by US schools must respect FERPA limits on student education records. Do not use school-provided data for marketing.
  - Ad targeting of minors: Google blocks ad personalization for users under 18, and Meta limits advertisers to age and location targeting for teens. Market to parents and schools, not to children directly.
  - Outcome claims: claims about grades, job placement or salaries need evidence. The FTC has taken action against education providers over misleading earnings and job claims. In Nigeria, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires a parent or guardian's consent for children's data.

1. **Split the site into three clear paths.** We create separate entry points for learners, parents and institutions. Each audience has different fears and goals, and one pitch cannot answer all of them.
2. **Audit data collection for children's privacy.** We check forms, analytics and ad tags against COPPA and similar rules before any campaign launches. A privacy problem with minors' data costs far more than a slow quarter.
3. **Redesign onboarding around the first lesson.** We define the first meaningful win and build reminders and progress emails around it. Most edtech drop-off happens before that first session ends.
4. **Publish search pages for specific skills and exams.** We target the topics learners and parents already search for. These visitors have intent, and a sample lesson shows what free content does not.
5. **Build a pilot offer for schools and training buyers.** Institutions want proof before they commit, so a short pilot or sample curriculum gives them a low-risk first step.
