To start a delivery business, choose a niche, register the business, license and insure your vehicles for carrying other people's goods, price every drop from your true cost per mile, and sign businesses that ship every week. Most delivery startups fail on pricing, not demand: fuel, maintenance, waiting time and failed deliveries quietly eat the margin.

This guide covers how to start a delivery business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, including licensing, startup costs, pricing maths and how to win contracts.

:::takeaways
- Contract clients (shops, pharmacies, ecommerce sellers) are the backbone. Ad hoc jobs fill gaps.
- Price per drop from cost per mile plus time. Add surcharges for waiting, extra stops and failed deliveries.
- Insurance for carrying goods for hire is different from personal vehicle insurance. Get the right one.
- Proof of delivery and live tracking are what business clients pay for.
:::

## Which delivery niche should you start with?

| Niche | Vehicle | Typical clients | Revenue pattern | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same-day local parcels | Motorbike, car, small van | Shops, online sellers, individuals | Per drop | Low price competition |
| Ecommerce last mile | Van, motorbikes | Online stores, marketplaces | Per drop, daily volume | Failed deliveries, returns |
| B2B supplies (pharmacy, lab, auto parts) | Car, small van | Pharmacies, clinics, garages | Contract, scheduled runs | Strict time windows, handling rules |
| Food and grocery | Motorbike, bicycle, car | Restaurants, grocers | Per order | Peak-hour demand, temperature |
| Bulky items and furniture | Larger van, two-person crew | Furniture stores, movers | Per job | Vehicle cost, injury risk |

[B2B](/glossary/business-to-business/) niches pay more reliably than consumer deliveries. A pharmacy that needs two runs a day is a better first client than fifty one-off customers.

## What licenses and insurance does a delivery business need?

**United Kingdom.** Register as a sole trader or limited company ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)). You need a goods vehicle operator licence for vehicles over 3,500 kg gross plated weight. Vans over 2,500 kg carrying other people's goods for hire or reward to the EU and some other European countries need a standard international licence ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/being-a-goods-vehicle-operator)). Insure for hire and reward and goods in transit. Employers' liability insurance is mandatory once you hire, with at least £5 million cover ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/employers-liability-insurance)).

**United States.** Register with your state and get an EIN ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)). Commercial auto insurance is needed for business deliveries; personal policies often exclude them. If you operate commercial vehicles in interstate commerce, check the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's rules on USDOT numbers. City rules on parking and loading also apply ([SBA licensing guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits)).

**Nigeria.** Register with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/). Courier and logistics operators are regulated through NIPOST under its courier regulations; check current licensing requirements and fees before you take third-party parcels ([Aelex overview of the courier regulations](https://www.aelex.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/The-Regulation-of-Courier-and-Logistic-Companies-in-Nigeria..pdf)). Register vehicles and riders with your state, and check state rules on commercial motorcycles and tricycles in the areas you plan to serve.

## How do you price deliveries so they make money?

Work out your cost per mile and per hour, then build a per-drop price. Worked example (illustrative), US van courier:

- Fuel: van does 20 miles per gallon; fuel at $3.50 → $3.50 ÷ 20 = **$0.175 per mile**.
- Maintenance, tires and depreciation: **$0.25 per mile**.
- Insurance and phone: $500 a month ÷ 2,000 miles = **$0.25 per mile**.
- Vehicle cost per mile: $0.175 + $0.25 + $0.25 = **$0.675**.
- Driver time: $22 an hour.

A 12-mile same-day run that takes 50 minutes: (12 × $0.675) + (50 ÷ 60 × $22) = $8.10 + $18.33 = **$26.43** cost. Price it at **$35**, a 32% markup on cost, with a waiting charge after 10 minutes.

Contract maths: a pharmacy paying $35 per run, two runs a day, 22 days a month is 2 × 22 × $35 = **$1,540** a month from one client. Three clients like that cover a van and a driver.

In Nigeria, most local deliveries are priced by zone, not by mile. Use the same arithmetic to set zone prices: fuel per km, maintenance, rider pay per hour, plus a margin. Recalculate when fuel prices change.

## How to start a delivery business: step by step

:::steps "Launch in 45 days"
1. Pick a niche and zone | One client type and a delivery area you can cover with one vehicle.
2. Register and insure | Business registration, vehicle licensing, goods-in-transit and liability insurance.
3. Set prices | Per-drop or per-zone prices, waiting time, extra stops, failed-delivery fee.
4. Set up tracking and proof of delivery | Photos, signatures or OTP codes, shared with the client automatically.
5. Sign two anchor clients | Offer a two-week trial at contract rates to a pharmacy, shop or online seller.
6. Hire carefully | Background-check riders and drivers, and write down cash-handling rules.
7. Review margins monthly | Track revenue per vehicle per day and cost per drop.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: More deliveries always means more profit.
Fact: Underpriced deliveries lose money faster as volume grows. Know your cost per drop before you add vehicles.
Myth: Personal car insurance covers delivery work.
Fact: Personal policies commonly exclude carrying goods for payment. Get commercial, hire-and-reward and goods-in-transit cover that matches what you carry.

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a delivery business?
A single-motorbike courier in Nigeria can start with a few million naira including the bike. A one-van courier in the US or UK typically needs a used van, commercial insurance and a fuel float, often $15,000 or £12,000 and up. Leasing reduces the upfront cost.
### Do I need a license to start a delivery business?
UK: an operator licence for vehicles over 3,500 kg, and a standard international licence for vans over 2,500 kg doing paid work in Europe. US: state registration, commercial insurance and FMCSA rules if you cross state lines with commercial vehicles. Nigeria: CAC registration and NIPOST courier licensing.
### Is a delivery business profitable?
It can be if you price per drop from real costs and keep vehicles busy with contract work. Margins are thin on ad hoc consumer deliveries.
### How do I get delivery contracts?
Start with local businesses that ship daily: pharmacies, labs, shops and online sellers. Offer a short trial, then a monthly agreement with agreed prices and service levels.
### Should I build my own delivery app?
Start with off-the-shelf dispatch and tracking tools. Build your own once your volume and processes are stable and the tools are holding you back.
