To start a beauty salon business, decide what you'll offer and how you'll operate (a home studio, a rented chair or room, or your own premises), then get the licences your location requires, fit out the space, and set up online booking. Your profit depends on how full your chairs are and how many clients rebook before they leave.

This guide covers how to start a beauty salon business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, with licensing rules, a cost table and the maths behind chair rental versus employing staff.

:::takeaways
- In the US, every state requires a cosmetology or barber licence, and many also license the salon itself.
- In the UK, there's no national hairdressing licence, but London boroughs license treatments such as nails and massage.
- Start smaller than you think: a rented room or chair proves demand before you sign a long lease.
- Rebooking at the till is the cheapest marketing you'll ever do.
:::

## Step 1: Choose your services and salon model

Decide what you're known for. A braiding studio, a natural-hair salon, a barbershop, a nail bar and a lash studio all need different equipment, licences and marketing. Pick a lead service and add two or three that the same client buys.

Then choose a model:

| Model | Upfront cost | Risk | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home or mobile studio | Lowest | Low | You have existing clients and local rules allow it |
| Rent a chair or room in someone else's salon | Low | Low | You want premises without a lease |
| Your own salon, you work alone | Medium to high | Medium | Your diary is full and you're turning clients away |
| Your own salon with chair renters or staff | High | Higher | You want to earn from other stylists' work too |

## Step 3: Budget your startup costs

Planning estimates for a small salon with two to four stations. These are not quotes; fit-out costs depend heavily on the building.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit and first months' rent | $3,000–$15,000 / £2,500–£12,000 | Depends on city and lease terms |
| Fit-out (plumbing for basins, electrics, flooring) | $5,000–$40,000 / £4,000–£30,000 | Backwash basins need plumbing and drainage |
| Chairs, stations, dryers, nail desks | $3,000–$15,000 / £2,500–£12,000 | Buy used where hygiene allows |
| Opening product stock | $1,000–$5,000 / £800–£4,000 | Retail stock adds margin but ties up cash |
| Licences, registration, insurance | $500–$3,000 / £300–£2,500 | State or council fees plus public liability and treatment risk cover |
| Booking software, website, branding | $500–$5,000 / £400–£4,000 | Online booking is non-negotiable |

For a home studio or rented chair, you can often start with tools, stock, insurance and booking software alone. In Nigeria, budget early for a generator or inverter (dryers and steamers draw heavy power), water storage, and stock bought from verified distributors to avoid fake products. Get naira quotes when you're ready to buy; imported equipment tracks the exchange rate.

## Step 4: Do the chair maths

Whether to rent chairs or employ staff is the biggest financial decision a salon owner makes.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- You have 4 stations. You work one; three are available.
- **Chair rental:** each renter pays $250 a week. 3 × $250 × 52 = **$39,000 a year**, with little management but no share of their sales.
- **Employed stylists:** each brings $1,200 a week in services. After wages, products and payroll costs at roughly 60% of that, you keep $480 each. 3 × $480 × 50 weeks = **$72,000 a year**, but you carry hiring, training, sick cover and quiet weeks.

Rental is steadier and simpler. Employment earns more when the diary is full and costs more when it isn't. Many owners start with renters and switch to staff once demand is proven.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A beautiful salon fills itself.
Fact: Location and decor help, but clients still have to find you, trust you and book easily. Map listings, reviews, referrals and simple booking do the heavy lifting.
Myth: Discounts are the best way to get new clients.
Fact: Deep discounts attract one-time bargain hunters. A first-visit offer tied to rebooking, or a referral reward for existing clients, brings in clients who return.
:::

## Step 5: Set up booking, rebooking and retention

The fastest growth lever is rebooking. A client who rebooks before leaving costs nothing extra to win, and every repeat visit adds to what that client is worth over a year. That's [customer lifetime value](/glossary/customer-lifetime-value/) in practice.

- **Online booking** with deposits for long appointments to cut no-shows.
- **Rebook at checkout.** Ask every client to book their next visit before they leave.
- **Automated reminders** by SMS or WhatsApp 48 hours and 2 hours before.
- **A simple loyalty offer**, such as every sixth visit includes a treatment.

:::steps "Launch plan for your first 60 days"
1. Confirm your licences | Personal licence, premises licence or council registration, insurance.
2. Set up booking | Online booking with deposits, reminders and a clear cancellation policy.
3. Claim your listings | Google Business Profile, Apple Maps and relevant directories with photos and prices.
4. Soft launch | Invite existing clients and friends for a launch week and collect reviews.
5. Post your work | Before-and-after photos every week, tagged with your area.
6. Recruit renters or staff | Once your own diary is full, fill the other chairs.
:::

## Beauty salon business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a beauty salon business?
A home studio or rented chair can start with tools, stock, insurance and booking software. A small salon with premises costs much more: our planning estimate is roughly $13,000–$83,000 in the US or £10,500–£64,500 in the UK, mostly driven by rent and fit-out.
### What licences do I need to open a beauty salon?
In the US, a state cosmetology or barber licence, and often a salon establishment licence. In the UK, no national licence for hairdressing, but London councils license treatments like nails and massage. In Nigeria, register with the CAC and check state and local permits.
### Can I start a beauty salon from home?
Often, yes. Check zoning or planning rules, your tenancy or mortgage terms, and whether your state or council licenses home-based treatments. A separate, clean treatment area and proper insurance are essential.
### Should I rent out chairs or hire stylists?
Renting chairs gives steady income with less management. Hiring stylists earns more when the diary is full but adds payroll, training and risk. Many owners start with renters and move to staff later.
### How do I get my first salon clients?
Start with existing clients, then set up a Google Business Profile, post before-and-after photos weekly, and ask for reviews after every visit. Make booking easy and rebook clients before they leave.
:::
