To start a landscaping business, decide whether you'll focus on recurring maintenance, design-and-build projects or both, then register and insure the business, get any licences your work needs, and buy the equipment for your first services only. The businesses that last build dense routes of recurring clients, so travel time stays low and income stays steady.

This guide covers how to start a landscaping business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, with official licensing links, an equipment budget and route-based pricing maths.

:::takeaways
- Recurring maintenance gives predictable cash flow. Projects (patios, planting, irrigation) add margin.
- Applying pesticides commercially needs a licence in most US states and a certificate in the UK.
- In England, carrying clients' garden waste means registering as a waste carrier.
- Price by the visit and plan routes by postcode or neighbourhood to cut unpaid travel.
:::

## Step 1: Choose your services

Start with what you can deliver well with the kit you can afford:

| Service type | Examples | Income pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance | Mowing, hedge cutting, weeding, leaf clearance, seasonal tidy-ups | Recurring, weekly or fortnightly |
| Soft landscaping | Planting schemes, turfing, mulching, seasonal colour | One-off projects, often leading to maintenance |
| Hard landscaping | Patios, paths, fencing, retaining walls, decking | Larger projects, higher margin, more risk |
| Specialist | Irrigation, tree work, pest and weed control, lighting | Premium rates, licences and training needed |

A good starting mix is maintenance for cash flow plus one project service you're skilled at.

## Step 2: Register, license and insure

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business registration | LLC or sole proprietorship; free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) | Sole trader (Self Assessment once trading income tops £1,000) or £100 limited company ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Chemicals | Most states require a licence to apply pesticides; fertiliser rules vary by state ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/building-and-grounds-cleaning/grounds-maintenance-workers.htm)) | Anyone using professional plant protection products needs a specified certificate, or must be supervised by someone who holds one while training ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pesticides/using-pesticides/general/faq-agricultural-use-of-pesticides.htm)) | Use only approved products and follow label directions; ask your state ministry of environment about local rules |
| Waste | Local rules on green waste disposal and dumping | Register as a waste carrier with the Environment Agency if you transport waste; upper-tier registrations are renewed every 3 years for £130.25 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-renew-waste-carrier-broker-dealer-england)) | State waste management authorities regulate disposal; use approved dump sites |
| Other | Contractor licence for hardscape or irrigation in some states; local business licence ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits)) | VAT once turnover passes £90,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)) | Estate and facility access permits, staff ID |

Insurance is essential in every country: public or general liability (a flying stone through a window is a real claim), vehicle cover for business use, tools cover, and employers' liability or workers' compensation when you hire.

:::callout warning "Clients' garden waste is still waste"
In England, carrying waste that isn't yours as part of your business generally means upper-tier waste carrier registration. Fly-tipping or using an unregistered carrier can bring unlimited fines. Check the Environment Agency's guidance before your first green-waste run.
:::

## Step 3: Budget for equipment

Buy for your first services only. Rent specialist kit until a service pays for its own machine. These are planning estimates, not quotes.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |

| Mowers (push and ride-on) | $500–$8,000 / £400–£6,000 | Commercial-grade lasts longer under daily use |
| Trimmers, blowers, hedge cutters | $800–$3,000 / £600–£2,500 | Battery tools cut noise complaints and fuel costs |
| Hand tools, PPE, safety kit | $300–$1,000 / £250–£800 | Eye and ear protection, gloves, boots |
| Licences, registration, insurance | $500–$3,000 / £300–£2,000 | Pesticide training and waste registration where needed |

In Nigeria, petrol-powered tools are usually more practical than battery tools where charging is unreliable. Budget for fuel, spare parts (blades and belts wear quickly in heavy growth seasons), a vehicle or pickup, and uniforms and IDs for estate access. Get naira quotes on the day you buy.

## Step 4: Price by route, not by guess

The landscaping business model is about density. Ten clients on one street are worth more than ten clients spread across the city, because travel time is unpaid.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- A typical suburban lawn takes 40 minutes including edging and clean-up.
- Your target is $70 per working hour after fuel and equipment costs.
- Between nearby jobs you lose 10 minutes driving and unloading, so each job uses 50 minutes.
- Price per visit: 50 ÷ 60 × $70 = about **$58**.
- With 8 visits a day, 4 days a week, fortnightly, you can serve 64 clients.
- 32 visits a week × $58 = **$1,856 a week** of recurring income, before projects.

If those clients were spread out and travel was 25 minutes between jobs, you'd fit only 6 visits a day and earn about a quarter less for the same hours. That's why landscapers market street by street.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Landscaping is only a summer business.
Fact: Demand peaks in the growing season, but autumn leaf clearance, winter pruning, hard landscaping and gritting contracts can fill colder months. In Nigeria's climate, growth continues year-round, with heavier work in the rainy season.
Myth: The cheapest quote wins the contract.
Fact: Facility managers and estates care about reliability, insurance and communication. Turning up on the same day every week often matters more than a small price difference.
:::

## Step 5: Win and keep recurring clients

:::steps "Your first season"
1. Pick your patch | Choose three or four neighbourhoods or estates within 15 minutes of base.
2. Get listed | Set up a Google Business Profile with your service area and photos of real work.
3. Leaflet and door-knock | When you finish a job, offer neighbours a first-visit price for the same day next week.
4. Sell maintenance plans | Offer a fortnightly or monthly plan with a seasonal tidy-up included.
5. Approach facility managers | Estates, offices and churches buy annual contracts. Bring your insurance certificate.
6. Ask for reviews and referrals | A simple referral reward works well in close-knit streets.
:::

## Landscaping business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a landscaping business?
A maintenance-only start using a vehicle you already own can begin with mowers, trimmers, safety kit and insurance. With a used van or pickup and trailer, our planning estimate is roughly $10,600–$48,000 in the US or £8,000–£33,800 in the UK.
### Do I need a license to start a landscaping business?
Usually not for basic maintenance, but you'll need to register the business. Pesticide application requires a licence in most US states and a specified certificate in the UK. In England, transporting clients' waste needs waste carrier registration.
### How do I price lawn mowing and maintenance?
Time the job, add travel between nearby clients, and multiply by your target hourly rate after costs. Quote per visit or as a monthly plan, and review prices each year.
### How do I get landscaping clients fast?
Work street by street: offer neighbours a slot next to an existing job, leaflet the area, and keep your map listing full of fresh photos and reviews. Approach estates and facility managers for contracts.
### Should I offer hard landscaping too?
Only if you have the skills, insurance and cash flow for bigger projects. Patios and walls pay well, but mistakes are expensive. Many landscapers partner with a hardscaper first.
:::
