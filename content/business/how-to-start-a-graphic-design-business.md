To start a graphic design business, choose a niche, register the business, build a portfolio around that niche, and sell fixed-price projects that lead to monthly retainers. You don't need a studio or a loan. You need a reliable computer, licensed software, a clear offer and a way for buyers to find you.

This guide covers how to start a graphic design business in the US, the UK and Nigeria: what to register, what it costs, how to price, and how to keep work coming once your network runs dry.

:::takeaways
- Design is a crowded field. A niche (an industry or a deliverable) is what makes you easy to choose.
- Registration is cheap or free in all three countries. Software and hardware are your real startup costs.
- Price by project, cap revisions in writing, and offer a retainer after every successful project.
- Your portfolio site is a sales page, not a gallery. Show the problem, the work and the result.
:::

## Step 1: Choose a niche you can sell

A niche has two parts: who you serve and what you deliver. "Brand identities for fintech startups" is a niche. "Packaging and labels for food brands" is a niche. "Graphic design" is not.

Pick from where you already have proof. If you spent three years designing for a bank, fintech is your obvious first market. If friends run restaurants, start with menus, signage and social templates. In Nigeria, food, beauty and fintech brands buy design constantly. In the UK and US, SaaS, ecommerce and professional services are steady buyers.

Write one sentence you can put at the top of your website: "I design [deliverable] for [audience] so they can [result]."

## Step 2: Register the business

You can start as a sole trader almost everywhere, then incorporate when income or risk grows.

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simplest structure | Sole proprietorship, or a single-member LLC for liability protection ([SBA guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure)) | Sole trader; register for Self Assessment once you earn more than £1,000 in a tax year ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)) | Business name registration with the [Corporate Affairs Commission](https://www.cac.gov.ng/) |
| Company option | LLC or corporation, filed with your state | Private limited company: £100 online at Companies House ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) | Private company limited by shares, also via CAC |
| Tax ID | EIN, free from the IRS ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)) | Unique Taxpayer Reference from HMRC | Tax Identification Number (TIN) |
| Watch for | Some cities require a general business license ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits)) | VAT registration once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in 12 months ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)) | Nigeria's 2025 tax reforms changed company tax thresholds; professional services are treated differently, so check with an accountant |

Graphic design doesn't need a professional license in any of the three countries. What you do need is a contract that says who owns the work. Most clients expect full rights to final files once they pay. Keep the rights to your working files and the right to show the work in your portfolio, unless the client pays for exclusivity.

## Step 3: Budget your startup costs

These are planning estimates for a solo designer working from home. They are not quotes; check current prices before you buy.

| Cost item | Typical range (estimate) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Computer capable of design work | $800–$2,500 / £700–£2,000 | Buy once; a used machine is fine if it runs your software smoothly |
| Design software | Monthly subscription; check the vendor's current price | Adobe Creative Cloud, Affinity, Figma or Canva, depending on your niche |
| Fonts and stock licences | $0–$300 to start | Read the licence: commercial use and client hand-off are often separate |
| Domain and portfolio website | $50–$400 a year | Or a custom site if your niche buys on credibility |
| Business registration | Free (US EIN) to £100 (UK company) | Nigerian CAC fees are listed on the CAC portal at payment |
| Power backup (Nigeria) | Inverter or solar kit | Budget for it before anything else if grid power is unreliable where you work |
| Contract template / legal review | $0–$500 | A one-off review is worth it before your first big client |

In Nigeria, convert hardware costs at the current exchange rate on the day you buy; imported laptops and software subscriptions move with the naira.

## Step 4: Set prices with simple maths

Start with the income you need, then work back to a project price.

**Worked example (replace these numbers with yours):**

- Target income: $4,000 a month before tax.
- Business costs (software, website, internet, insurance): $300 a month.
- So you need $4,300 a month in fees.
- You can realistically bill 60 hours a month. The rest goes to sales, admin and learning.
- $4,300 ÷ 60 = about $72 per billable hour.
- A small brand identity takes you 25 hours, so quote at least $1,800 (25 × $72).

Now add a retainer: four social templates and two ad variations a month for $600. Three retainer clients bring in $1,800 a month before you sell a single new project. That is the difference between a freelance gig and a design business.

:::callout tip "Cap revisions in writing"
Two rounds of revisions per deliverable is a common, fair default. A third round is billed at your hourly rate. Put it in the proposal, not in an email after the fact.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need a design degree before you can start a design business.
Fact: Employers often ask for a degree, but clients buying from a business judge your portfolio and process. A niche portfolio with clear outcomes wins work that credentials alone don't.
Myth: Cheaper prices win more clients.
Fact: Low prices attract buyers who compare on price and leave on price. Clear scope, a deadline and a named outcome win better clients at higher fees.
:::

## Step 5: Build a portfolio that sells

Six to eight projects is enough. Each one should follow the same structure:

1. **The client's problem**, in one line.
2. **What you made**, with the final visuals.
3. **Why it works**, in two or three sentences.
4. **The result**, only if the client confirmed it and agreed to share it.

If you're new, make two or three self-initiated projects for your niche, and label them clearly as concept work. Behance and Dribbble help with discovery, but your own site is where buyers book. A dedicated site also lets you rank for searches like "packaging designer in Lagos" or "brand designer for SaaS", which is a [local SEO](/services/local-seo/) and content job, not a design one.

## Step 6: Win your first five clients

Your first clients usually come from people who already trust you. After that, you need a repeatable channel.

:::steps "First 90 days"
1. Tell your network | Announce the business on LinkedIn and WhatsApp with your one-sentence offer and one example project.
2. Ask former employers | Agencies and in-house teams overflow; offer to take overflow work at a fair day rate.
3. Pick one marketplace | Upwork, Fiverr or Contra can build early reviews. Pick one, write a niche profile and stop there.
4. Publish proof weekly | Post one before/after or process breakdown a week where your buyers spend time.
5. Turn every project into a retainer pitch | At hand-off, offer a monthly package for the work they'll need next.
6. Ask for referrals and testimonials | Ask within a week of delivery, while the result is fresh.
:::

## Step 7: Run it like a business

The admin side is where many designers lose money.

:::checklist "Before you take your first paid project"
- Business bank account separate from personal spending
- Proposal template with scope, timeline, revisions and price
- Contract covering payment terms, deposit and ownership of files
- 50% deposit before work starts on new clients
- Invoicing tool with automatic reminders
- Backup for client files (cloud plus one offline copy)
- A simple monthly record of income and expenses for tax
:::

As you grow, the next step is usually not hiring another designer. It's adding a service your clients already ask for, such as websites, motion or [brand strategy](/glossary/branding/), with a trusted partner.

## Graphic design business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a graphic design business?
If you already own a capable computer, you can start for the cost of software, a domain and a portfolio site, often a few hundred dollars or pounds. Buying a new machine adds roughly $800–$2,500. Registration is free in the US (EIN) and £100 for a UK limited company.
### Do I need a license to be a graphic designer?
No. Graphic design is not a licensed profession in the US, UK or Nigeria. You still need to register the business for tax, and some US cities require a general business license. Check your city's rules through the SBA's licensing guide.
### How do I price my first graphic design projects?
Work out the monthly income you need, add business costs, divide by realistic billable hours, and multiply by the hours a project takes. Quote the project price, not the hourly rate, and cap revisions at two rounds.
### Should I register as a sole trader or a company?
Start as a sole trader if you're testing demand. Move to an LLC (US) or limited company (UK, Nigeria) when you sign larger contracts, want liability protection, or an accountant shows a tax benefit.
### How do I find clients without social media?
Use your former employers, local business associations and agencies that need overflow help. A portfolio website that ranks for your niche and city brings in buyers who are already searching.
### Can I name my business after myself?
Yes. Using your own name is simple and builds a personal brand. If you plan to hire, a separate brand name can make the business easier to sell later. Try our [AI business name generator](/tools/ai-business-name-generator/) for ideas.
:::
