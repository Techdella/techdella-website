To start a social media influencer business, choose a niche you can create content about every week, build an audience on one or two platforms that suit your format, and register the business before brand money arrives. Then earn from several sources (brand partnerships, affiliate links, user-generated content for brands and your own products) and label every paid or gifted post as advertising. Treat it as a media business: consistent output, clear rates, contracts and records.

This guide covers how to start a social media influencer business in Nigeria, the US and the UK, with the disclosure rules, rate maths and income streams that make it sustainable.

:::takeaways
- A clear niche grows faster and attracts better-paying brands than general lifestyle content.
- Spread income across brand deals, affiliate links, UGC and your own products.
- The FTC (US), ASA (UK) and ARCON (Nigeria) all regulate influencer advertising.
- Own an email list and a website, because platforms change their rules and reach without warning.
:::

Here's how the rules compare:

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulator | Federal Trade Commission | Advertising Standards Authority (CAP Code), with the CMA and Trading Standards | Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) |
| When to disclose | Any financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand, including free or discounted products ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)) | When you're paid or otherwise connected to the brand; ads must be obviously identifiable ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)) | ARCON says sponsored content is advertising that needs approval before exposure ([ARCON](https://arcon.gov.ng/)) |
| How | In the post itself, not only in a bio or behind "more"; on screen and spoken in videos; repeated in livestreams | A clear "Ad" label, prominent and up front; vague labels like "gifted" or "thanks to…" are unlikely to be enough | Get approval for the advert, then label it clearly; ask the brand whether they've obtained approval |

:::callout warning "A discount code isn't a disclosure"
The ASA's research found most people didn't recognise posts with discount codes as ads. Add a clear "Ad" label at the start, even when a platform's paid-partnership tag is switched on, if you're unsure it's prominent enough.
:::

## Step 1: Choose a niche that can pay

Pick the overlap of what you can make content about every week, what an audience wants, and what brands sell. Examples that attract brand budgets:

- Personal finance and fintech (strict ad rules, so expertise is valued).
- Beauty, skincare and haircare, including natural hair.
- Tech, gadgets and apps.
- Food, recipes and restaurant reviews in one city.
- Parenting and family.
- Fitness and wellness.
- Travel and diaspora life.

A niche makes brand pitching easy: "I reach young professionals in Lagos who cook at home" is a clear audience a food brand can buy.

## Step 2: Pick your platforms and format

Choose platforms by format, not by trend:

| Your strength | Best-fit platforms | Content that works |
|---|---|---|
| Short video and personality | TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts | Hooks in the first two seconds, series formats |
| Long-form explaining | YouTube, podcasts | Tutorials, reviews, deep dives |
| Professional expertise | LinkedIn, X | Insights, carousels, commentary |
| Visual aesthetics | Instagram, Pinterest | Photo series, guides, saveable posts |

Start with one main platform and repurpose to a second. Use our free [hashtag generator](/tools/free-hashtag-generator/) for discovery research, but remember that content quality and consistency matter more than hashtags.

## Step 3: Register the business and keep records

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Sole proprietorship or LLC ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure)) | Sole trader; register for Self Assessment once trading income tops £1,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)) | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/) |
| Tax ID | Free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) | UTR from HMRC; VAT once turnover passes £90,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)) | TIN |
| Gifted products | Free products can count as income; keep records | Gifted items received for promotion can be taxable; keep records | Keep records of all brand payments and gifts |

Use a separate bank account and keep every contract, invoice and gifted-product note. It saves hours at tax time and protects you in a dispute.

## Step 4: Build multiple income streams

| Income stream | How it works | When it starts paying |
|---|---|---|
| Brand partnerships | Brands pay for posts, videos or campaigns | Once you have an engaged, niche audience |
| [Affiliate marketing](/glossary/affiliate-marketing/) | Commission on sales through your links | Early, if your audience buys |
| UGC creation | Brands pay you to make content for their own channels | Early; depends on skill, not follower count |
| Platform payouts | Creator funds, ad revenue sharing | Varies by platform and eligibility |
| Your own products | Presets, ebooks, courses, merchandise, coaching | Once you know what your audience asks for |

[User-generated content](/glossary/user-generated-content/) is the most accessible early income: brands pay for content they run as ads, and they judge your skill rather than your follower count.

## Step 5: Set your rates with maths

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- Your Reels average 8,000 views. You decide your base rate is $15 per 1,000 average views.
- One Reel: 8 × $15 = **$120**.
- Add usage rights if the brand wants to run it as an ad for 30 days: +50% = **$180**.
- Add exclusivity (no competing brands for 60 days): +30% of base = **$216**.
- A package of 2 Reels and 3 Stories might be quoted at $500.

Two packages a month ($1,000), affiliate income ($200) and two UGC videos at $150 each ($300) bring in **$1,500 a month**. None of these numbers is a market standard; they show how to build a rate card from your own average views and rights you grant. Review your rates every quarter as your averages change.

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need 100,000 followers before brands will pay you.
Fact: Brands work with small creators, especially in clear niches, and UGC work depends on your content skills, not your audience size.
Myth: The platform's paid-partnership tag always covers your disclosure.
Fact: The ASA says platform labels may be enough only if they're upfront, clear and prominent. If in doubt, add your own "Ad" label too.
:::

## Step 6: Pitch brands and protect yourself

:::steps "From creator to business in 90 days"
1. Build a media kit | Audience, average views, engagement, examples and your rate card.
2. List 30 brands | Brands already advertising in your niche, plus local businesses.
3. Pitch with an idea | A specific content idea for their product, not "let's collaborate".
4. Use a contract | Deliverables, deadlines, usage rights, exclusivity, payment terms and disclosure.
5. Report results | Share views, clicks and saves after each campaign.
6. Launch one owned product | Something small your audience already asks for.
:::

## Social media influencer business FAQs

:::faq
### How long does it take to make money as an influencer?
It varies widely. UGC and affiliate income can start within months if you create consistently in a clear niche. Brand partnerships usually follow once your average views and engagement are steady and you have examples to show.
### Do I need a large following to start an influencer business?
No. Brands work with small, niche creators, and UGC creation pays for content skills rather than audience size. Focus on a clear niche, consistent output and engagement.
### Do I have to label sponsored posts?
Yes. The FTC requires clear disclosure of material connections in the US, the ASA requires ads to be obviously identifiable in the UK, and ARCON requires sponsored content to be approved before it's published in Nigeria. Gifts count too.
### How much should I charge for a sponsored post?
Base your rate on your average views, then add fees for usage rights and exclusivity. Track results and raise rates as your averages and outcomes improve.
### Can influencers run their business from home?
Yes. Most creators work from home with a phone, lighting and editing apps. Register the business, keep records and use contracts like any other business.
:::
