To start a food truck business, you need a tested menu, a truck or trailer fitted to food-safety standards, a food business registration or health permit, and permission to trade where you park. Do the paperwork before you buy anything big: the rules on where you can trade decide which truck, menu and schedule make sense.

Below is how to start a food truck business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, including the official rules, a startup budget, and the maths that tells you whether a pitch is worth it.

:::takeaways
- Check trading rules for your city before you buy a truck. Location permissions shape everything else.
- In the UK, register with your council at least 28 days before trading. It's free.
- In the US, your local health department enforces a version of the FDA Food Code.
- Keep the menu to five or six items. Speed per order is what makes a shift profitable.
:::

## Is a food truck business profitable?

It can be. In the US, more food money now goes on eating out than on groceries. But the margin is made or lost on speed, food cost and pitch fees.

In Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt, the competition is often a roadside buka with very low prices, so a food truck usually wins on convenience, hygiene and a distinct menu rather than on price.

## Step 1: Pick a concept and test it cheaply

Your concept is your menu, your price point and your customer. A lunchtime truck near offices needs fast, one-handed food at a mid price. An events truck can charge more and serve slower, more theatrical dishes.

Test before you build:

- Cook your menu for 50 people at a market stall, a pop-up or a friend's event.
- Time each order. If an item takes more than three or four minutes, it'll cap your sales at peak.
- Cost every recipe to the gram. You need the food cost per plate to set prices.

## Step 2: Get the permits and registrations

Food trucks are regulated twice: once for food safety, and again for where you trade.

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food safety | Local or state health department permit. Most states and localities base their rules on the [FDA Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code), and the 2026 edition adds a mobile food establishment definition. Expect a vehicle inspection and a commissary (base kitchen) requirement in many cities. | Register your food business with the local council at least 28 days before trading. Registration is free and applies to mobile units and vans ([Food Standards Agency](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/getting-ready-to-start-your-food-business)). You'll get a hygiene inspection and rating. | State and local government environmental health officers inspect food vendors. If you sell pre-packaged products (bottled sauces, packaged snacks), [NAFDAC](https://nafdac.gov.ng/) registration applies to those products. |
| Where you can trade | City vending permits, parking rules and event permits; see the [SBA licensing guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits) | A street trading licence from the council for public streets ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/street-trading-licence)); private sites need the landowner's permission | Local government permits and market or estate management approval; rules differ by state and LGA |
| Business and tax | LLC or sole proprietorship; free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number); sales tax permit | Sole trader or £100 limited company ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)); VAT above £90,000 turnover | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Staff | Food handler or manager certification where required | Food hygiene training appropriate to the role | Ask your state environmental health office whether food handlers need medical screening certificates |

:::callout warning "Check the trading map before buying a truck"
Some cities cap the number of vending permits, ban trading near restaurants or limit hours. A beautiful truck with nowhere to park is an expensive garage ornament.
:::

## Step 3: Budget your food truck startup costs

These are planning estimates, not quotes. Truck prices vary hugely by size, age and fit-out.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Used truck or trailer, fitted | $30,000–$90,000 / £20,000–£60,000 | A trailer is cheaper but needs a tow vehicle |
| Kitchen equipment (if not included) | $8,000–$25,000 / £6,000–£18,000 | Fryers, griddle, fridge, extraction, fire suppression |
| Permits, registration, inspections | $500–$3,000 / £0–£1,500 | UK food registration is free; street trading fees vary by council |
| Generator or power hook-up | $1,500–$6,000 / £1,000–£4,000 | Quiet inverter generators matter at events |
| Branding and wrap | $2,000–$6,000 / £1,500–£4,500 | Your truck is your biggest ad |
| Opening stock and packaging | $1,500–$4,000 / £1,000–£3,000 | Compostable packaging is required in some areas |
| Insurance (first year) | $2,000–$5,000 / £1,000–£3,500 | Vehicle, public liability, product liability |
| Card reader and POS | $0–$1,000 / £0–£800 | Most customers expect to tap and pay |

In Nigeria, many founders start with a converted bus or a kiosk-style trailer rather than an imported truck, which brings costs down sharply. Budget for a generator and fuel, gas cylinders, water storage, and security for overnight parking. Get naira quotes from fabricators on the day you commit; prices follow the exchange rate.

## Step 4: Do the break-even maths

Every shift has to cover its own costs before it contributes to the truck.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- Average order: $14. Food and packaging cost per order: $4.50. So each order contributes **$9.50**.
- Shift costs: pitch fee $80, fuel and gas $45, two staff for 5 hours at $18 = $180. Total: **$305**.
- Break-even orders per shift: $305 ÷ $9.50 = **33 orders**.
- Fixed monthly costs (loan, insurance, commissary, software): $2,400. Over 20 shifts, that's $120 per shift, or another 13 orders.
- So you need about **46 orders per shift** before you make a profit.

At a lunchtime pitch open 11:30 to 2:00, 46 orders is roughly one every three minutes. That's why speed matters more than menu size, and why a busy event that charges a percentage of sales can be worth more than a cheap pitch with low footfall.

:::myth-fact
Myth: A food truck is a cheap way to open a restaurant.
Fact: It's cheaper than a restaurant fit-out, but trucks still carry vehicle, kitchen, insurance and pitch costs. The real saving is that you can move to where the customers are.
Myth: More menu items mean more sales.
Fact: Longer menus slow service and raise waste. A short menu you serve fast usually sells more per hour.
:::

## Step 5: Plan your locations and schedule

Treat locations like a portfolio. Mix three types:

1. **Regular weekday pitches** near offices, campuses or industrial estates for steady lunch trade.
2. **Weekend markets and events** for higher volumes and prices.
3. **Private bookings** (weddings, corporate days, film sets) for guaranteed minimum spend. Our [catering business guide](/business/catering-business/) covers how to price these.

Track orders per hour for each pitch for four weeks. Drop the weakest every month.

:::steps "Launch plan"
1. Confirm the rules | Speak to the health department or council and your city's trading office before buying.
2. Test the menu | Run two pop-ups and time every order.
3. Buy and fit the truck | Get the fit-out inspected or signed off before branding.
4. Register and insure | Food registration or permit, trading licence, insurance, business registration.
5. Book your first pitches | Secure two weekday pitches and one weekend event for the first month.
6. Announce your schedule | Post where you'll be each week on your website, Google Business Profile and Instagram.
:::

## Step 6: Market a business that moves

Your customers need to know where you are today. Make that the easiest thing to find:

- **A simple website** with this week's locations, your menu and a booking form for private events. Keep it fast on phones.
- **Google Business Profile** with your service area, hours and photos, and fresh posts each week. This is a [local SEO](/services/local-seo/) basic.
- **One social channel done well.** Instagram or TikTok short clips of food being made sell better than flat photos. See our [social media marketing](/glossary/social-media-marketing/) primer.
- **A loyalty prompt.** A stamp card or a WhatsApp broadcast list for regulars.

## Food truck business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a food truck business?
Our planning estimate for a used, fitted truck plus permits, branding, stock and insurance is roughly $45,000–$140,000 in the US or £30,000–£95,000 in the UK. A trailer or converted van can cut this substantially. In Nigeria, converted buses and kiosk trailers are common lower-cost starts.
### What permits do I need for a food truck?
You need food-safety approval (a health permit in the US, council food business registration in the UK, state environmental health checks in Nigeria) plus permission to trade at each location. Many US cities also require a commissary kitchen.
### Do I need to register my food truck in the UK?
Yes. Register with your local council at least 28 days before you start trading. It's free, can't be refused, and applies to mobile units. You'll still need a street trading licence for public streets.
### Can I run a food truck from home?
You can do admin and some prep at home only if your local rules allow it. Many US cities require a licensed commissary for prep, storage and cleaning, and UK councils inspect wherever food is prepared.
### How do I find good food truck locations?
Ask your council or city for permitted trading zones, then test office districts, campuses, markets and events. Track orders per hour at each pitch and keep only the ones that beat your break-even.
:::
