To start a mobile app development business, pick one type of client you understand, one technology stack you can deliver well, and one first offer that is small enough to sell quickly. Register the company, open your Apple and Google developer accounts, and build a portfolio of two or three apps people can actually download. Then price every project from what your time really costs.

The demand is real. But the gap between "I can code an app" and "I run a profitable app business" is mostly about scoping, pricing and finding clients. That is what this guide covers.

:::takeaways
- Specialize early: one client type and one stack make you easier to hire and faster to deliver.
- Your fixed costs are low. The real risk is underpriced, open-ended projects.
- Sell a paid discovery sprint first. It protects you and filters out unserious clients.
- Budget for app store rules: Google requires new personal accounts to run a closed test before launch.
:::

## What a mobile app development business actually sells

Clients rarely want "an app." They want an outcome: bookings without phone calls, a loyalty program, a field team that stops using paper forms, or a product investors can try. Your business turns that outcome into a working app and keeps it working.

Most app studios sell some mix of:

- **Discovery and scoping:** user flows, feature list, estimate.
- **UI/UX design:** wireframes and a clickable prototype.
- **Build:** native iOS (Swift), native Android (Kotlin), or cross-platform (Flutter, React Native).
- **Launch:** store listings, review submissions, analytics setup.
- **Maintenance retainers:** OS updates, bug fixes, small features.

The retainer is what makes the business stable. Every app needs updates when Apple and Google release new OS versions, so a client you launch is a client you can keep.

## How to start a mobile app development business, step by step

:::steps "From freelancer to app studio"
1. Choose a niche | Pick one client type (clinics, logistics firms, fintech startups, schools) and one problem you solve well. "Booking apps for clinics" sells faster than "apps for everyone."
2. Choose your stack | Cross-platform (Flutter or React Native) lets a small team ship iOS and Android from one codebase. Go native only when clients need deep device features.
3. Register the business | Form a company so contracts, payments and IP sit with the business, not you personally. See the country table below.
4. Open developer accounts | Create your Apple and Google developer accounts in the business name. Client apps should usually live in the client's own accounts, with you added as a team member.
5. Build two or three portfolio apps | Real, downloadable apps beat screenshots. Rebuild a common flow (booking, ordering, onboarding) and publish it.
6. Package your offers | Discovery sprint, fixed-scope MVP, and monthly maintenance. Each has a clear deliverable and price.
7. Write a contract template | Cover scope, change requests, payment milestones, IP transfer on final payment, and who owns store accounts.
8. Find the first five clients | Your network, startup communities, agencies that need overflow capacity, and one channel you commit to for 90 days.
9. Deliver and document | Ship on time, record a short demo video for each project, and ask for a written testimonial at handover.
10. Add retainers before headcount | Grow recurring maintenance revenue before you hire your second full-time developer.
:::

## How to register an app development company in the US, UK or Nigeria

| Country | Typical structure | Where to register | What to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | LLC (single or multi-member) | Your state's Secretary of State; free EIN from the IRS | The SBA says total registration cost is usually under $300, varying by state |
| United Kingdom | Private limited company (Ltd) | Companies House (£100 online, usually within 24 hours) | Register for Corporation Tax with HMRC; VAT once taxable turnover passes £90,000 |
| Nigeria | Private company limited by shares, or a business name to start | Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) portal | Check current CAC fees on the portal. A tech company under 10 years old may qualify for a Nigeria Startup Act label |

Sources: [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/register-your-business), [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number), [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company), [HMRC VAT](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register), [Nigeria Startup Act 2022](https://startup.gov.ng/doc/NIGERIA_STARTUP_ACT_2022_Final_Publication.pdf).

If you plan to sell to US clients from Nigeria or the UK, get advice on where to hold IP and how you'll invoice before you sign your first big contract. Fixing it later costs more.

## How much should you charge? A worked example

Start from your loaded day rate, then price fixed scopes on top of it. Here's the arithmetic with example numbers. Swap in your own.

- You want $72,000 a year in pay, plus $12,000 for tools, insurance, accounting and marketing. That's **$84,000**.
- You can bill about 120 days a year. The rest goes to sales, admin, learning and holidays. Being honest here is the whole point.
- $84,000 ÷ 120 = **$700 per billable day**.
- An MVP you scope at 35 days of work: 35 × $700 = $24,500. Add a 15% buffer for unknowns: **$28,175**.
- A maintenance retainer of 2 days a month: **$1,400 a month**. Five of those cover $7,000 of your monthly costs before you sell any new project.

If the market in your niche won't pay that, cut scope, not your rate. Smaller first versions also ship faster, which clients like. Our [guide to the minimum viable product](/glossary/minimum-viable-product/) explains how to cut a feature list down to what tests the idea.

## How to find your first app development clients

You don't need a big marketing budget. You need one repeatable channel and proof.

- **Your network:** tell every founder and business owner you know exactly who you build for. Specific beats general.
- **Agencies and studios:** many marketing and design agencies sell apps but don't build them. Offer white-label capacity.
- **Startup communities and accelerators:** founders need MVPs before they raise. Office hours and demo days are good places to meet them.
- **Search:** clients search phrases like "app developer for clinics" or "Flutter developer Lagos." A focused website with case studies and one page per niche can rank for these over time. Our team covers this in [lead generation](/services/lead-generation/).
- **Marketplaces:** fine for early reviews, but price pressure is high. Use them to build proof, then move clients to direct contracts.

## Myths that cost new app studios money

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need a large team to start an app development business.
Fact: A solo developer or a developer-designer pair can deliver fixed-scope MVPs. Hire when retainers cover a salary, not before.
Myth: Clients care most about your tech stack.
Fact: Most clients care about timeline, budget and whether the app works. Explain the stack in terms of cost and speed.
Myth: Publishing to Google Play is instant.
Fact: New personal developer accounts must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before publishing. Plan that into launch dates.
:::

## Launch checklist

:::checklist "Before you take your first paid project"
- Company registered and business bank account open
- Apple and Google developer accounts set up in the business name
- Contract template covering scope, change requests, milestones and IP transfer
- Two or three portfolio apps live and downloadable
- Discovery sprint, MVP and maintenance offers priced and written up
- One website page per niche you serve
- Insurance quotes collected
- Process for handing store accounts and code to the client
:::

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Fixed price with no fixed scope.** Every fixed price needs a written feature list and a change-request process.
- **Owning the client's store accounts.** It looks helpful, but it creates disputes. Publish under the client's account.
- **Skipping design.** A clickable prototype catches most scope arguments before code is written. See how we approach [UI/UX design](studios:/services/ui-ux-design/).
- **No maintenance offer.** Without retainers, you restart sales from zero every month.

:::faq
### How profitable is a mobile app development business?
It depends on utilization and pricing. A studio that bills 120 days a year at a rate built from real costs, and keeps several maintenance retainers, can be steadily profitable. Studios that take open-ended fixed-price work usually lose margin on overruns.
### Do I need to know how to code to start an app development company?
No, but you need someone who does and whom you trust. Non-technical founders often run sales, scoping and project management while a technical co-founder or contractors build. You still need enough knowledge to scope work and judge quality.
### Should I build native or cross-platform apps?
For most small-business and MVP projects, cross-platform frameworks such as Flutter or React Native ship iOS and Android from one codebase, which cuts cost. Choose native when the app relies heavily on device hardware, complex animation or platform-specific features.
### How long does it take to build an MVP app?
It depends on scope, so get a written estimate after a short discovery sprint rather than guessing. Whatever the build time, on Google Play also budget at least 14 days for the closed test that new personal developer accounts must complete.
### Can I run an app development business from Nigeria for US and UK clients?
Yes. Many teams do. Register properly, invoice in the client's currency where you can, overlap working hours for at least part of the day, and show case studies from similar markets. Clear communication matters as much as code quality.
:::
