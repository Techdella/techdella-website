To start a pet sitting business, choose your services, check whether any need a licence, buy pet business insurance, price per visit with clear surcharges, and build trust with every client through a meet-and-greet, reliable updates and reviews. You can start with no premises and little equipment. In England, though, boarding dogs in your own home or running dog day care needs a council licence, while dog walking does not.

This guide explains how to start a pet sitting business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, including licensing, insurance, pricing and how to fill your route.

:::takeaways
- Pet sitting in the client's home and dog walking usually need no special licence. Home boarding in England does.
- Insurance should cover animals in your care and lost keys, not just general liability.
- Price per visit by length, with surcharges for holidays, extra pets and medication.
- Trust is the product. Meet-and-greets, written care notes and photo updates win repeat clients.
:::

## Which pet sitting services should you offer?

| Service | Where | Typical length | Licence check | Revenue pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drop-in visits (feed, play, clean up) | Client's home | 20–60 minutes | Usually none | Daily during trips |
| Dog walking (solo or small group) | Neighborhood, parks | 30–60 minutes | Usually none; some parks limit dog numbers | Weekday recurring |
| Overnight house sitting | Client's home | 12–24 hours | Usually none | Holidays, peaks |
| Home boarding | Your home | Days to weeks | Council licence in England | Holidays, peaks |
| Doggy day care | Your home or premises | Working day | Council licence in England | Weekday recurring |
| Add-ons (pet taxi, basic grooming, medication) | Varies | Varies | Check local rules | Per task |

Dog walking gives the steadiest weekday income. Drop-ins and overnights spike around holidays, so recurring walkers carry you through quiet months.

## What licences and insurance do you need?

**United Kingdom.** In England, animal boarding businesses, including home boarders and day carers, need an animal activity licence from the local council under the Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving Animals) (England) Regulations 2018. Dog walking and dog grooming do not ([Waverley Borough Council](https://www.waverley.gov.uk/services/business-and-licensing/licences-and-registrations/licensing-of-activities-involving-animals)). Register as a sole trader once you earn over £1,000 a year from self-employment ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)). If you hire walkers, employers' liability insurance is mandatory ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/employers-liability-insurance)).

**United States.** Register the business and get a [free EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). Some cities and counties license pet sitting, dog walking or kennels, so check your local rules through the [SBA licensing guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits). Clients often expect you to be bonded and insured because you hold their keys.

**Nigeria.** Register a business name with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/). Keep up-to-date vaccination records for pets you board, and agree an emergency vet in writing with each owner before the first booking.

## How should you price pet sitting?

Price per visit by length, then add surcharges. Worked example (illustrative), US dog walker:

- 30-minute solo walk: $22.
- Route plan: 8 walks a day, grouped by neighborhood so travel between them averages 10 minutes.
- Daily revenue: 8 × $22 = **$176**.
- Weekly (5 days): $176 × 5 = **$880**. Over 48 working weeks: $880 × 48 = **$42,240** a year.
- Add holiday drop-ins at $28 and overnights at $85, and the year can pass $50,000 before costs and tax.

Common surcharges: public holidays (for example, 25% extra), each additional pet, medication, late bookings and visits outside your core area.

In Nigeria, many sitters price overnight and holiday care by the day, and dog walking by the month. Agree in writing who pays for food, vet visits and transport.

## How to start a pet sitting business: step by step

:::steps "From first client to a full route"
1. Choose services and area | Pick walks, drop-ins or overnights, and a tight service area.
2. Get trained | Pet first aid and basic behavior knowledge. Clients ask.
3. Register, license and insure | Business registration, any local licences, pet business insurance with care, custody and key cover.
4. Write your paperwork | Service agreement, pet profile form, vet authorization, key handling policy, cancellation terms.
5. Hold meet-and-greets | Meet every pet and owner before the first booking. Note routines, quirks and emergency contacts.
6. Set up updates | Photo or video updates and a short visit note after each visit.
7. Ask for reviews and referrals | Most new clients come from neighbors and vets.
:::

:::myth-fact

Fact: In England, home boarding and dog day care need a council licence under the 2018 regulations. Dog walking and sitting in the client's home generally don't.

:::faq
### Do I need a license to start a pet sitting business?
Often no for dog walking and sitting in the owner's home. In England, home boarding and dog day care need a council animal activity licence. In the US, some cities license pet services, so check locally. In Nigeria, register with the CAC.
### How much does it cost to start a pet sitting business?
Usually a few hundred dollars or pounds: insurance, first aid training, basic supplies and booking software. Home boarding costs more because of licensing and home setup.
### How much do pet sitters charge?
Most charge per visit or walk, by length, with extra for holidays, additional pets and medication. Overnight stays are priced per night. Work out your hourly rate including travel before you set prices.
### What insurance does a pet sitter need?
Pet business insurance covering public liability, animals in your care, custody and control, and lost keys. Add employers' liability in the UK if you hire.
### How do I get my first pet sitting clients?
Start with neighbors, friends and local vets. Set up a Google Business Profile, ask every client for a review, and offer meet-and-greets so owners can see how you handle their pets.
:::
