To start a fashion brand from scratch, you need four things before you need a big budget: a clear customer, a small first collection, a reliable maker, and a price that covers your real cost per piece. Most new clothing brands fail on stock, not design. They order too much of the wrong thing and run out of cash waiting for it to sell.

This guide walks through launching a fashion brand in small, low-risk steps, with registration and labeling basics for the US, UK and Nigeria, a startup-cost table and the pricing maths.

:::takeaways
- Start with three to six pieces, not a full collection.
- Produce small runs or pre-sell. Unsold stock is the biggest risk in fashion.
- Price from landed cost per piece, then check the wholesale math works.
- In the US, clothing labels must show fiber content, country of origin and who made or sells it.
:::

## Who will wear it? Define the brand before the clothes

A fashion brand is a promise to a specific person. "Affordable streetwear for everyone" is not a brand. "Modest workwear for women in Lagos who commute in heat" is. The narrower version tells you fabrics, cuts, price range, where to advertise and what to post.

Write down:

- **Who:** age range, city, job, what they wear now and where they buy it.
- **Why you:** fit, fabric, culture, size range, sustainability or price, but pick one lead reason.
- **Price band:** what your customer pays today for a similar item.

## How to start a fashion brand, step by step

:::steps "From idea to first drop"
1. Define the customer and the one reason they'd choose you | Write it in a sentence and test it on ten people who match.
2. Design three to six core pieces | Pieces that work together and that you can remake. Skip seasonal extras for now.
3. Make samples | Get one sample per style, wear-test it, wash-test it, and fix fit before any production.
4. Choose your maker | A local tailor or small workshop for runs under 50 units; a factory once you know what sells.
5. Register the business and protect the name | Register the company and check the name is free to trademark in your main market.
6. Label correctly | Fiber content, care instructions, country of origin and your business details, following your market's rules.
7. Build pre-launch demand | Share the making process, fittings and fabric choices for four to six weeks before launch.
8. Launch a small drop or pre-order | Sell a limited run or take pre-orders so production matches demand.
9. Reorder what sells | Restock winners. Drop or redesign slow pieces. Then add new styles.
:::

## Registration and labeling in the US, UK and Nigeria

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Register the business | LLC or sole proprietor with your state; free EIN from the IRS | Sole trader with HMRC, or Ltd with Companies House (£100 online) | Business name or company with CAC |
| Protect the name | Trademark search and filing with the USPTO | Trademark with the UK Intellectual Property Office | Trademark with the Trademarks Registry, Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment |
| Labels | Fiber content, country of origin and maker or seller identity are required; care labels are a separate FTC rule | Follow UK textile labeling rules for fiber content | Use clear fiber, care and maker details; check SON guidance for your product type |

## Pricing a clothing brand: the worked example

Price from your landed cost: everything it took to get one finished piece ready to sell.

- Fabric: $9 · Cutting and sewing: $8 · Trims, label and tag: $2 · Packaging: $1 · Shipping in: $2
- **Landed cost: $22 per shirt**
- Direct-to-customer price at roughly 3× landed cost: **$66**. After a 3% payment fee and $8 of ad spend per sale, you keep $66 − $2 − $8 − $22 = **$34**.
- Wholesale to a boutique at roughly half of retail: $33. Your margin: $33 − $22 = **$11**. Workable, but only at volume.

If the wholesale number doesn't work, either cut landed cost (fabric, quantity) or stay direct-to-customer until you can. The same logic applies in naira or pounds.

## How to market a new fashion brand

- **Show the making.** Fabric sourcing, fittings and sample fails build trust and pre-launch interest.
- **Use creators who already dress your customer.** Send pieces to five micro-creators whose followers match your buyer.
- **Run small paid tests.** Spend a little on two or three ad variations, keep the winner, and scale it. Our [paid ads](/services/paid-ads/) team covers how to structure tests.
- **Hashtags help discovery on some platforms.** Use specific ones over generic ones. Our free [hashtag generator](/tools/free-hashtag-generator/) gives you a starting list.
- **Own the customer list.** Collect emails and phone numbers at every sale. Drops sell faster to people who already bought.

:::checklist "Launch checklist"
- Customer and positioning written in one sentence
- Samples wear-tested and wash-tested
- Maker chosen, with a written price per unit and lead time
- Business registered and name checked for trademark conflicts
- Labels meet your market's rules
- Landed cost and retail price calculated per style
- Online store, size guide and returns policy live
- Pre-launch content plan for four to six weeks

:::faq
### How much money do I need to start a fashion brand?
It depends on how many styles you launch and how many units you make. A small drop of three to six styles in short runs keeps the first spend low. Pre-orders reduce the cash you need even further, because customers pay before you produce.
### Can I start a clothing brand without knowing how to sew or design?
Yes. You can hire a freelance designer, pattern cutter and tailor or factory. You still need to own the customer, the brand direction and quality control, and you should wear-test every sample yourself.
### How long does it take to launch a fashion brand?
Plan for sampling, production and a few weeks of pre-launch marketing. Sampling often takes more than one round to get the fit right, so build time for revisions rather than fixing a launch date first.
### What should a clothing label include?
In the US, the FTC requires fiber content, country of origin and the identity of the maker or seller, plus a care label under a separate rule. UK and Nigerian rules also expect clear fiber and care information. Check your market's regulator before printing.
### Should I sell online or in stores first?
Online first is usually cheaper and lets you test demand quickly. Pop-ups and boutiques work well once you have a few proven best-sellers and a wholesale price that still leaves margin.
:::
