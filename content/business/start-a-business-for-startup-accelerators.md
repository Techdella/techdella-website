Startup accelerators are fixed-length programs, usually around three months, that invest a small amount of money in early-stage companies in exchange for equity. In return you get mentoring, a structured push to grow, a peer group of founders, and introductions to investors at the end. They're competitive, and the startups that get in tend to show the same things: a strong team, a working product, real users and signs of growth.

This guide explains how accelerator deals work, what selection teams look for, and how to start and shape your business so you're ready to apply, from Nigeria, the US or the UK.

:::takeaways
- An accelerator is a trade: equity for money, mentoring, structure and investor access. Read the terms before you apply.
- Teams, traction and clarity get startups in. Pitch polish comes after those.
- You don't always need revenue, but you need evidence: users, usage, pilots or letters of intent.
- Apply when the program can speed up something already working, not to find out whether your idea works.
:::

## What is a startup accelerator?

An accelerator runs startups through a program in batches (also called cohorts). Most share these features:

- **Investment for equity**, usually via a SAFE or a small priced stake.
- **A fixed timeline**, often about three months.
- **Mentoring and workshops** on product, sales, fundraising and hiring.
- **A demo day** where startups pitch to investors.
- **An alumni network** you can use for years.

An **incubator** is usually earlier and looser: space, support and sometimes small grants, often with no fixed end date and no investment.

A [SAFE](/glossary/safe-agreement/) is an agreement to give the investor equity later, usually at your next priced funding round. Understand how it converts before you sign. Our glossary entry on [pre-seed funding](/glossary/pre-seed-funding/) explains where accelerator money fits.

## Dilution: a worked example

What does "7% for $125,000" imply? Plain arithmetic:

- $125,000 ÷ 0.07 = **about $1,785,700** post-money valuation for that SAFE.
- If you and a co-founder own 50% each before the deal, you each own 50% × 0.93 = **46.5%** after that 7%, before other SAFEs convert.
- The remaining $375,000 in YC's case converts on the terms of the lowest-cap SAFE issued before your priced round, so its dilution depends on what you raise next.

The question to ask is not "how much do I give up?" but "will this program make the company more valuable by more than the equity I give?"

## What do startup accelerators look for?

Programs differ, but selection usually turns on five things:

| Factor | What strong looks like | Weak signal |
|---|---|---|
| Team | Founders who can build and sell, have worked together, and know the problem first-hand | One founder, no technical capacity, no domain knowledge |
| Problem | Painful, frequent, and costing customers money or time | A "nice to have" with no evidence of pain |
| Progress | A working product with real users and growth week to week | Slides and a waitlist only |
| Market | Large or growing, with a clear first customer segment | "Everyone" as the target market |
| Clarity | You explain what you do in one sentence and answer hard questions directly | Jargon, vague answers, no numbers |

## How to start a business that's ready for accelerators

:::steps
1. Start with a problem you know | Founder-problem fit is persuasive. Explain why you, specifically, understand this problem.
2. Build the founding team | Cover building and selling. If you're solo, show how you'll cover the gaps.
3. Ship a minimum viable product | A working product, however basic, beats a prototype on slides.
4. Get real users | Pilots, paying customers or active free users. Track them weekly.
5. Show growth | A simple chart of a key metric rising week over week is the strongest thing you can bring.
6. Know your numbers | Users, revenue, retention, cash and burn rate. Answer instantly and accurately.
7. Get the company in order | Registered company, clean cap table, founder vesting, IP assigned to the company.
8. Write a clear application | Short, specific answers. Lead with progress and numbers.
9. Build relationships early | Talk to alumni, attend office hours and demo days, and ask for feedback before you apply.
:::

## Notes for founders in Nigeria, the US and the UK

**Nigeria.** Many global and Africa-focused accelerators accept Nigerian startups, and some require a holding company abroad. Get legal advice on structure before you sign. The Nigeria Startup Act 2022 lets eligible tech startups under 10 years old, with at least one-third Nigerian founder shareholding, apply for a startup label that gives access to incentives under the Act ([Nigeria Startup Act 2022](https://startup.gov.ng/doc/NIGERIA_STARTUP_ACT_2022_Final_Publication.pdf)).

**United States.** Many venture-backed accelerators and investors prefer a Delaware C-corp. Converting from an LLC later costs time and legal fees, so decide early if you plan to raise.

**United Kingdom.** A private limited company registered at Companies House (£100 online) is the usual vehicle ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)). Ask your accountant about UK investor tax relief schemes before you raise, because they can make your round more attractive to UK angels.

## When an accelerator is the wrong move

Accelerators suit some companies better than others. Think twice if:

- **Your business isn't built for fast growth.** A profitable local services company may never need venture money, and giving up equity for it can be a poor trade.
- **You can't commit the time.** Programs are intense. If you're still in a full-time job, you'll get less from them.
- **You're applying to find out if the idea works.** Validate first with customers. Programs speed up what's already moving.
- **The terms don't fit your plans.** Some programs take equity on terms that complicate later rounds. Have a lawyer read the documents.

Saying no to the wrong program keeps your cap table clean for the right one.

## Your application and pitch deck

Accelerator applications reward clarity. Keep each answer short and concrete:

- **What you do:** one sentence a non-expert understands.
- **Progress:** users, revenue, growth rate, pilots, with dates.
- **Why now:** what changed that makes this possible or urgent.
- **Competition:** who else solves it and why customers pick you.
- **Team:** why these founders, for this problem.

Your [pitch deck](/glossary/pitch-deck/) should follow the same order. Ten to twelve slides, with numbers on the traction slide, is plenty.

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need revenue to get into an accelerator.
Fact: Many programs accept pre-revenue startups, but they look for evidence such as active users, pilots or strong growth in usage.
Myth: Accelerators are mainly about the money.
Fact: The investment is often small. Founders choose programs for structure, mentoring, network and investor access.
Myth: A polished deck can make up for weak progress.
Fact: Selection teams look past design to team, problem and traction. Simple slides with real numbers do better.
:::

:::checklist "Accelerator-ready checklist"
- Founding team covers building and selling
- Working product with real users
- Weekly metric tracked and trending up
- Users, revenue, retention, cash and burn known by heart
- Company registered, founder vesting in place, IP assigned
- One-sentence description tested on non-experts
- Pitch deck with a traction slide built on real numbers
- Conversations with at least two alumni of the program
:::

:::faq
### What do startup accelerators look for?
Mostly a strong founding team, a real problem, progress such as users or revenue, a large or growing market, and founders who can explain all of it clearly. Progress and team usually matter most at the earliest stage.
### Do startup accelerators provide funding?
Many do, in exchange for equity. Y Combinator, for example, publishes a standard $500,000 investment split across two SAFEs. Others invest less or offer support without investment. Always read the current terms.
### Can a startup join an accelerator without revenue?
Yes, some programs accept pre-revenue startups. You'll still need evidence of demand, such as active users, pilots, letters of intent or strong usage growth.
### How long does an accelerator program last?
Many run for about three months. Techstars, for example, describes its accelerators as three-month programs. Check each program, since some are shorter, longer or remote.
### Should I join an accelerator or raise from angels directly?
It depends on what you need. If you'd benefit from structure, mentoring and a demo day audience, an accelerator can be worth the equity. If you already have strong traction and investor relationships, you may raise directly on better terms.
:::
