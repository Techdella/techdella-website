To start a home health care business, decide first whether you'll provide non-medical home care (help with washing, dressing, meals and companionship) or skilled home health (nursing and therapy). That choice sets your licences, staff, insurance and who pays you. Then register with the right regulator, hire and train carers, and build referral relationships with hospitals, GPs and social workers.

This guide explains how to start a home health care business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, with the regulators to contact, the staffing maths and a realistic budget.

:::takeaways
- Non-medical home care and skilled home health are different businesses with different licences.
- In England, providing personal care in people's homes generally requires CQC registration before you start.
- In the US, home health agencies need state licensure; billing Medicare also needs certification through a state survey.
- Your margin lives in the gap between what you charge per hour and what each care hour really costs you.
:::

## Step 1: Choose your care model

| Model | What you provide | Who usually pays | Regulation level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Companion and non-medical home care | Companionship, meals, errands, help with washing and dressing | Families, long-term care insurance, local authority (UK) | Medium: personal care is regulated in the UK and many US states |
| Skilled home health | Nursing, wound care, physiotherapy, medication management | Medicare and Medicaid (US), NHS-commissioned services (UK), insurers, families | High: clinical licences, certification and inspections |
| Introductory agency | Matches families with self-employed carers | Families | Lower, but rules on what counts as "introducing" are strict |
| Specialist care | Dementia, post-hospital discharge, palliative, paediatric | Mixed | High, with extra training requirements |

Most founders without clinical backgrounds start with non-medical care. Nurses and therapists can go straight to skilled care, but expect a longer, costlier licensing path.

## Step 2: Get licensed and registered

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Care regulator | State licensure for home health agencies; many states also license non-medical home care. To bill Medicare, you need enrollment and an initial certification survey by a state survey agency or approved accreditor ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/provider-enrollment-and-certification/certificationandcomplianc/downloads/certandcomplianceprocess.pdf)). | In England, providing personal care to people in their homes is a regulated activity that requires [CQC](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/registration/scope-registration/regulated-activities) registration. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own regulators. | Health facility regulation is state-led. In Lagos, [HEFAMAA](https://hefamaaportal.com.ng/) requires health facilities to register every year; confirm with them whether your home care model is covered. Other states have their own bodies. |
| Professional licences | Nurses and therapists licensed by state boards; aides may need state training or certification | Nurses registered with the NMC; carers trained to the Care Certificate standard is common practice | Nurses licensed by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria; check every clinician's current licence |
| Business registration | LLC or corporation; free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) | Limited company (£100 via [Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) is typical for CQC providers | Company registration with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Data | HIPAA if you're a covered entity handling health information | UK GDPR; care records are special category data | Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, overseen by the [NDPC](https://ndpc.gov.ng/) |

:::callout warning "Don't start care before registration"
CQC says it rejects many incomplete home care applications. Prepare your business plan, staff training plan, policies and statement of purpose before you apply, and don't deliver regulated personal care until you're registered.
:::

## Step 3: Budget your startup costs

These are planning estimates for a small non-medical agency. They are not quotes. Skilled home health agencies cost considerably more because of clinical staff, accreditation and longer time to first payment.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Licensing, registration and legal advice | $2,000–$15,000 / £1,500–£10,000 | Varies widely by state; CQC applications benefit from specialist advice |
| Policies, procedures and compliance setup | $1,000–$5,000 / £800–£4,000 | Templates help, but tailor every policy to your service |
| Office (small, or shared) | $0–$2,000 a month / £0–£1,500 a month | Some regulators require a registered office address |
| Insurance (first year) | $3,000–$12,000 / £2,000–£8,000 | Public liability, professional indemnity, employers' liability |
| Recruitment, DBS or background checks, training | $2,000–$8,000 / £1,500–£6,000 | Ongoing cost; budget per carer hired |
| Care management software | Monthly subscription | Rotas, visit logs, care plans, electronic medication records |
| Working capital | 2–3 months of payroll | Local authorities and insurers can pay weeks after the care is delivered |

In Nigeria, a lean start is a small team of nurses and caregivers serving families directly. Your payer may be a relative abroad arranging care for a parent in Lagos or Abuja, so make it easy to pay and get updates remotely. Budget for staff transport, phones for visit check-ins, and clear payment terms in naira or foreign currency.

## Step 4: Do the staffing maths

Your hourly charge has to cover far more than the carer's wage.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- Carer wage: $18 an hour.
- Employer taxes, holiday pay and benefits: about 20% → $3.60.
- Travel time and mileage between visits: $2.00 per care hour.
- Training, supervision and recruitment: $1.50 per care hour.
- Insurance, software and office: $2.00 per care hour.
- True cost per care hour: 18 + 3.60 + 2.00 + 1.50 + 2.00 = **$27.10**.
- To keep a 20% margin, charge at least $27.10 ÷ 0.8 = **about $33.90 an hour**.

If a local authority or insurer offers $28 an hour, you'd make less than a dollar per hour before management salaries. That's why many agencies mix publicly funded work with private-pay clients, and why visit scheduling that cuts travel time matters so much.

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need a medical background to start a home care business.
Fact: Non-medical home care can be run by non-clinicians with strong management, though a registered manager with care experience is often required. Skilled home health needs clinicians.
Myth: Marketing is the hardest part.
Fact: For most agencies, recruiting and keeping reliable carers is the hardest part. Without staff, you can't accept the referrals you win.
:::

## Step 5: Build referral relationships

Most home care clients arrive through someone they trust. Build relationships with:

- Hospital discharge teams and social workers.
- GPs and practice managers (UK), primary care doctors (US), and private hospitals (Nigeria).
- Care coordinators at insurers and local authorities.
- Churches, mosques, community groups and diaspora associations.

Families also search online, often in a hurry after a hospital stay. A clear website that answers "what care do you offer, where, and how fast can you start?" turns those searches into calls. [Referral marketing](/glossary/referral-marketing/) and [lead generation](/services/lead-generation/) work best together here.

:::steps "Launch plan"
1. Choose your model | Non-medical or skilled, private-pay or funded, and your service area.
2. Prepare compliance | Policies, care plans, safeguarding, training plan and statement of purpose.
3. Register | State licence, CQC registration or state health facility regulator, plus company registration.
4. Recruit your first carers | Hire before you market; background checks and induction training done.
5. Win your first referrals | Meet discharge teams, social workers and community groups with a one-page service sheet.
6. Audit monthly | Review visit logs, missed visits, complaints and staff turnover every month.
:::

## Home health care business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a home health care business?
A small non-medical agency needs money for licensing, compliance, insurance, recruitment and two to three months of payroll as working capital. Skilled home health agencies cost much more because of clinical staff, accreditation and slower payments. Get advice on your state or country's requirements before budgeting.
### Do I need a medical background to start a home care business?
Not for non-medical home care in most places, but regulators often require a registered manager with relevant experience. Skilled home health, which provides nursing and therapy, needs licensed clinicians.
### Do I need CQC registration for a home care agency?
In England, yes, if you provide personal care to people in their own homes. Introductory agencies with no ongoing role in how care is delivered may be exempt, but the rules are strict, so check CQC's guidance.
### How do home care agencies get clients?
Through referrals from hospitals, social workers, doctors and community groups, plus families searching online. A clear website, strong local search presence and fast response times turn those enquiries into clients.
### What is the difference between home care and home health care?
Home care usually means non-medical support such as washing, dressing, meals and companionship. Home health care means clinical services like nursing and therapy, which carry stricter licensing and, in the US, Medicare certification if you bill Medicare.
:::
