To start a real estate consulting business, pick an advisory niche (investment analysis, development feasibility, buyer advisory or property management consulting), confirm whether your services need a real estate licence or registration where you work, and sell your expertise through fixed fees, hourly advice or retainers. The fastest way into trouble is to start "consulting" and end up introducing buyers and sellers for a commission without the right licence.

This guide covers how to start a real estate consulting business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, including where the regulatory lines sit.

:::takeaways
- Advice is one thing; arranging or introducing a sale or let is regulated estate agency or brokerage work.
- In the US, every state licenses brokers and agents. In England, estate agency businesses must register with HMRC for anti-money laundering supervision before trading.
- In Lagos, real estate practitioners must register with LASRERA.
- Fixed-fee advice, written reports and retainers make income less dependent on deal timing.
:::

## What does a real estate consultant actually do?

A real estate consultant is paid for advice, analysis and decisions, not for closing a transaction. Typical services:

- **Investment analysis:** yield, cash flow and risk on a property or portfolio.
- **Buyer advisory:** helping a client choose a market, area and property type, often for diaspora or overseas investors.
- **Development feasibility:** land appraisal, planning risk, cost and sales assumptions.
- **Portfolio and asset strategy:** what to hold, refurbish, refinance or sell.
- **Property management consulting:** systems, tenant processes and cost control for landlords.

## Step 1: Choose a niche you can prove

The best niche sits where your experience meets a buyer with money and a problem:

| Your background | Possible niche | Typical client |
|---|---|---|
| Residential agent | Buyer advisory for relocators or first-time investors | Individuals, diaspora buyers |
| Surveyor or valuer | Development feasibility, acquisitions due diligence | Developers, funds |
| Property manager | Operations and cost consulting | Landlords, estates, housing providers |
| Investor | Portfolio strategy and deal analysis | New investors, family offices |
| Finance or banking | Financing structure and refinancing strategy | Investors, developers |

In Nigeria, diaspora investors buying in Lagos and Abuja often want an independent adviser to check titles, developers and prices before they send money. That's a clear advisory niche, but title verification and valuation can involve reserved professions, so partner with lawyers and registered estate surveyors.

## Step 2: Check where advice becomes regulated work

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licence for transactions | Every state requires a licence to act as a broker or agent. Licences rarely transfer between states ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/real-estate-brokers-and-sales-agents.htm)). Many states treat any compensated help with buying or selling as brokerage, so check your state's real estate commission before charging for "consulting". | No general licence, but businesses doing estate agency work (acting on instructions to buy, sell or let, or introducing parties) must register with HMRC for anti-money laundering supervision before trading; trading unregistered is a criminal offence ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/registration-guide-for-estate-agency-businesses)). Residential estate agents must also join an approved redress scheme. | In Lagos, LASRERA (the Lagos State Real Estate Regulatory Authority) requires real estate practitioners to register and renew. Valuation is the work of registered estate surveyors and valuers. Other states have their own rules. |
| Pure advice | Fee-only advice that doesn't involve negotiating or arranging a deal may fall outside brokerage in some states; get a written opinion | Advice-only work may fall outside the Estate Agents Act definition; check HMRC's guidance on what counts as estate agency work | Advisory firms still need CAC registration; keep valuation and legal work with licensed professionals |
| Business registration | LLC or corporation; free EIN ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)) | Sole trader or £100 limited company ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Insurance | Errors and omissions (professional liability) | Professional indemnity | Professional indemnity is uncommon but worth asking about for larger mandates |

:::callout warning "Get it in writing"
Before your first paid engagement, get a short written opinion from a property lawyer in your jurisdiction on whether your service list counts as licensed brokerage or estate agency. It costs far less than a fine or an unenforceable contract.
:::

## Step 3: Budget your startup costs

Consulting is a low-capital business. These are planning estimates, not quotes.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Registration and legal opinion | $300–$2,500 / £250–£2,000 | Company setup plus the licensing opinion above |
| Licence or HMRC registration (if needed) | Varies by state; HMRC charges application and per-premises fees | Only if your services cross into regulated work |
| Professional indemnity or E&O insurance | $800–$4,000 / £600–£3,000 a year | Required by many corporate clients |
| Data and research tools | $50–$500 a month | Market data, mapping, listing data subscriptions |
| Website, branding and report templates | $1,500–$8,000 / £1,200–£6,000 | Your reports are your product; make them look the part |
| Laptop and phone | $1,000–$2,500 | If you don't already have suitable ones |

In Nigeria, add LASRERA registration if you practise in Lagos, site-visit transport, and a budget for partner lawyers and surveyors you'll subcontract. Agree fees in naira or foreign currency upfront with diaspora clients.

## Step 4: Price advice, not deals

Commission pricing ties your income to deal timing. Advisory pricing gives you steadier cash flow.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- Investment analysis report: 12 hours of work at $120 an hour = **$1,440** fixed fee.
- Buyer advisory retainer: $800 a month for 3 months while a client searches = **$2,400**.
- Portfolio review for a landlord with 10 units: 20 hours = **$2,400**.

Sell 2 reports, 2 retainers and 1 portfolio review a month and you invoice 2 × $1,440 + 2 × $800 + $2,400 = **$6,880** that month, with retainers carrying into the next. Compare that with waiting four months for a single commission that might fall through.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Calling yourself a consultant means you don't need a licence.
Fact: Regulators look at what you do, not your job title. If you negotiate, arrange or introduce deals for a fee, you may be doing licensed brokerage or estate agency work.
Myth: Clients only pay when a deal closes.
Fact: Investors and developers regularly pay fixed fees for feasibility studies, due diligence and strategy, because a bad decision costs far more than the advice.
:::

## Step 5: Win your first clients

:::steps "First 90 days"
1. Write your service sheet | Three services with what's included, timelines and fixed prices.
2. Publish one sample report | A redacted or hypothetical analysis that shows your thinking.
3. Tell your network | Former colleagues, lenders, lawyers and developers are your first referrers.
4. Partner with complementary pros | Mortgage brokers, solicitors, accountants and surveyors refer clients who need advice.
5. Publish answers | Write about the questions clients ask: area comparisons, yield maths, buying from abroad.
6. Follow up | Send a monthly market note by email to everyone you've spoken to.
:::

## Real estate consulting FAQs

:::faq
### Do I need a real estate license to be a real estate consultant?
It depends on what you do and where. In the US, many states treat compensated help with buying or selling as brokerage, which needs a licence. In the UK, estate agency work requires HMRC registration. In Lagos, practitioners register with LASRERA. Pure advice may be exempt; get a legal opinion.
### How much does it cost to start a real estate consulting business?
It's a low-capital business: registration, a legal opinion, insurance, data tools, a website and report templates. Our planning estimate is a few thousand dollars or pounds, plus any licensing fees if your services require them.
### How do real estate consultants charge?
Fixed fees for reports and feasibility studies, hourly rates for advice, and monthly retainers for ongoing support. Some combine a fixed fee with a success fee, but success fees can trigger licensing rules.
### How do I get my first real estate consulting clients?
Start with your network, partner with lawyers, brokers and accountants, and publish useful analysis that answers the questions your ideal clients search for. A clear website and a regular email update turn interest into enquiries.
### Can I consult for diaspora investors buying in Nigeria?
Yes, and it's a strong niche. Work with Nigerian lawyers for title checks and registered estate surveyors for valuations, register with LASRERA if you practise in Lagos, and be clear in writing about what you do and don't do.
:::
