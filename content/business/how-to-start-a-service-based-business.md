To start a service-based business, choose one service for one type of client, register the business, price your work from your real costs and hours, and then build a client pipeline you can measure. The pipeline is what separates a business from freelancing that swings between too much work and none.

Most service founders are good at the service and less sure about selling it. This guide covers both: setup and pricing, then a simple system for getting clients every month.

:::takeaways
- One clear service for one clear client type is easier to sell and to refer.
- Your income is capped by your hours, so price from them.
- A pipeline is just numbers: leads, conversations, proposals, clients. Measure each stage.
- Follow-up and retention are cheaper than finding new clients.
:::

## What is a service-based business?

A service-based business sells expertise, time or skill instead of physical products: bookkeeping, web design, cleaning, IT support, coaching, tutoring, photography, consulting, repairs. Clients pay for an outcome, a task done or ongoing help.

The model has clear strengths and limits:

- **Low startup costs.** Your main asset is skill.
- **Fast to start.** You can sell before you have a website.
- **Income is capped by time.** Unless you add people, packages or recurring work, you can only serve so many clients.

## How to start a service-based business, step by step

:::steps
1. Choose one service | The one you're best at and that clients already pay for. You can add more later.
2. Define your ideal client | Industry, size, location, budget and the problem that makes them search for help.
3. Package it | Turn the service into a named offer with a clear deliverable, timeline and price.
4. Register the business | Sole proprietor or LLC (US), sole trader or Ltd (UK), CAC business name (Nigeria).
5. Set up the basics | Business bank account, contract template, invoicing, and a booking link.
6. Build a trustworthy online presence | A focused website, a Google Business Profile if you serve locally, and two or three examples of your work.
7. Start your pipeline | Pick one or two lead sources and track every inquiry.
8. Follow up consistently | Most deals are lost to silence, not to "no."
9. Keep and grow clients | Turn one-off jobs into retainers, maintenance plans or repeat bookings.
:::

## Pricing: start from your available hours

You can't sell more hours than you have. Work out your floor price first:

- Income target **60,000** + business costs **12,000** = **72,000** a year
- Billable hours: 25 a week × 45 weeks = **1,125**
- Floor rate: 72,000 ÷ 1,125 = **64 an hour**

Then package above that floor. A monthly bookkeeping package that takes 8 hours is at least 8 × 64 = 512. Price it at 600 so there's margin for admin and the odd extra request. Use the same arithmetic in naira or pounds.

## Pipeline maths: how many leads you actually need

A pipeline is a [sales funnel](/glossary/sales-funnel/) with numbers attached. Here's an illustrative example. Measure your own rates for a month, then fill them in.

- You want **4 new clients** a month.
- You win **1 in 3** proposals, so you need 4 × 3 = **12 proposals**.
- Half of discovery calls turn into proposals, so you need 12 × 2 = **24 calls**.
- One in four inquiries books a call, so you need 24 × 4 = **96 inquiries** a month.

96 inquiries a month is a lot for a new business. That's the point: the maths shows where to work. Raise the call-to-proposal rate with better qualifying questions. Raise the win rate with clearer proposals and proof. Every improvement shrinks the number of leads you need.

## Where service business leads come from

- **Referrals.** Ask every happy client who else they know with the same problem. Make it easy with a short message they can forward.
- **Search.** People search for services when they need them now: "bookkeeper for restaurants," "IT support near me." A site with one page per service and location can rank for these. Our [SEO](/services/seo/) team covers how.
- **Outbound.** A short, researched list of ideal clients and a personal message to each. Our [lead generation](/services/lead-generation/) work is built around this.
- **Partnerships.** Other service providers who serve your clients but don't compete with you.
- **Email.** A simple monthly email to past clients and inquiries keeps you top of mind when they're ready. See [email marketing](/services/email-marketing/).

:::myth-fact
Myth: Lower prices help a new service business win clients.
Fact: Low prices attract price shoppers and cap your income. Clear packages, proof and fast responses win clients more reliably.
Myth: You need a big following to get clients.
Fact: Many service businesses grow on referrals, search and direct outreach to a short list of ideal clients.
Myth: A lead that doesn't reply isn't interested.
Fact: Many buyers are busy, not uninterested. A polite, planned follow-up sequence recovers deals that silence would lose.
:::

:::checklist "Your pipeline system"
- One spreadsheet or CRM with every inquiry, its source and its stage
- A reply to every inquiry within one business day
- A discovery call script with qualifying questions
- A proposal template with scope, timeline, price and next step
- A follow-up schedule after each proposal
- A monthly review of leads, calls, proposals and wins
- A referral request after every finished project
:::

## Keep the clients you win

Winning a client costs time and money. Keeping one costs much less. Strong [customer retention](/glossary/customer-retention/) comes from a few habits: regular check-ins, reporting results in plain language, fixing problems quickly and offering an ongoing plan when a project ends. Ask for feedback at the end of every project, act on it, and tell the client what you changed. Clients who feel heard stay longer and refer more.

:::faq
### What is a service-based business?
A business that sells skills, expertise or time rather than physical products. Examples include bookkeeping, design, IT support, cleaning, coaching and consulting. Clients pay for an outcome or a task done well.
### How do I get clients for a new service business?
Start with referrals from people who know your work, then add one or two measurable channels such as local search, direct outreach or partnerships. Track every inquiry so you know which sources turn into paying clients.
### How should I price my services?
Work out your floor hourly rate from your income target, costs and realistic billable hours. Then package your work above that floor, so clients buy a clear outcome at a known price.
### Why is SEO important for service businesses?
Because people search for services when they have a problem right now. Showing up for searches like "service + city" brings inquiries from people ready to buy, and the pages keep working after you publish them.
### Do I need to register my service business?
In most cases, yes. Register as a sole proprietor or LLC in the US, a sole trader or limited company in the UK, or a business name with CAC in Nigeria. It protects your name, lets you open a business account and keeps your taxes clean.
:::
