To start a personal chef business, choose a service model, complete food safety training, insure the business, check whether your model needs food business registration or a health permit, and charge a service fee with groceries billed separately. A personal chef needs only a handful of regular clients to earn a full-time income, so the business is built on trust, consistency and referrals rather than volume.

This guide explains how to start a personal chef business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, covering service models, food safety, startup costs, pricing and how to land clients.

:::takeaways
- Where you cook changes the rules. Cooking in a client's kitchen and delivering food from your own kitchen are treated differently.
- Bill groceries separately from your service fee. It keeps pricing honest when food prices change.
- Ten loyal weekly clients can be a full business. Retention matters more than reach.
- Food safety training, allergen handling and insurance are the minimum, whatever your country.
:::

## Which personal chef service should you offer?

| Model | Where you cook | Typical client | Revenue pattern | Rules to check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly meal prep | Client's kitchen | Busy families, professionals | Weekly recurring | Usually lighter; check locally |
| Meal prep delivered | Your approved kitchen | Professionals, athletes, new parents | Weekly recurring | Food business registration or health permit |
| Private dinners and events | Client's home or venue | Hosts, small celebrations | One-off, seasonal | Venue rules; alcohol rules if serving |
| Special diets (medical, allergen-free, halal) | Either | Clients with specific needs | Recurring | Allergen controls; stay within your qualifications |
| Live-in or traveling chef | Client's homes | High-net-worth families | Contract | Employment and tax terms |

A "personal chef" usually cooks for several clients, often in their kitchens. A "private chef" usually works for one household. Both are valid businesses; your pricing and paperwork differ.

## What food safety rules apply to a personal chef business?

**United Kingdom.** Food businesses must register with their local authority at least 28 days before trading, including businesses trading from home or from mobile units ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/food-business-registration)). If you prepare meals in your own kitchen for delivery, you are a food business. If you only cook in clients' kitchens, ask your council how they treat your model. Register as a sole trader once self-employment income passes £1,000 in a tax year ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)).

**United States.** Health departments regulate food prepared for sale. Rules for cooking in a client's own kitchen differ from rules for food you prepare elsewhere and deliver, which generally needs a permitted kitchen. Ask your county or city health department, and check state and local licensing via the [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits). Get a [free EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number).

**Nigeria.** Register with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/). If you later sell packaged, labeled products (sauces, spice mixes, frozen meals) through shops, NAFDAC registration applies ([NAFDAC](https://www.nafdac.gov.ng/)). Keep a current food handler medical certificate; estates and corporate clients often ask for one.

**Everywhere.** Keep allergen records for every client, label meals with dates and reheating instructions, and agree in writing who is responsible for kitchen cleanliness and equipment.

## How should a personal chef price services?

Charge a service fee for your time and skill, and bill groceries at cost (or cost plus a small shopping fee). Worked example (illustrative), US weekly meal prep:

- Service: shopping + cooking 5 dinners for a family of four, in the client's kitchen.
- Your time: 1.5 hours shopping + 4 hours cooking and cleanup + 0.5 hours planning = **6 hours**.
- Target rate: $50 an hour → 6 × $50 = **$300** service fee per week.
- Groceries: billed at cost, for example $180.
- Client pays: $300 + $180 = **$480** a week.

Your income comes from the service fee. Six weekly clients is 6 × $300 = **$1,800** a week, or **$86,400** over 48 weeks, before costs and tax. That uses about 36 hours a week, leaving room for one or two private dinners.

For private dinners, price per guest with a minimum: for example, $95 per guest, minimum six guests, groceries included in the per-guest price. Agree the menu, guest count and dietary needs in writing a week before.

The same model works in naira and pounds. In Nigeria, where ingredient prices can change week to week, billing groceries separately protects your margin.

## How to start a personal chef business: step by step

:::steps "From cook to booked chef"
1. Choose your model | In-home meal prep, delivered meal prep, private dinners or a mix.
2. Get trained and insured | Food safety and allergen training, liability insurance.
3. Check registration | Food business registration (UK), health permit (US), CAC and any state requirements (Nigeria).
4. Build sample menus | Three weekly menus and two dinner-party menus with photos.
5. Write a client intake form | Allergies, dislikes, diet goals, kitchen equipment, storage space, access.
6. Do three trial cooks | Discounted trials for people who match your ideal client, in exchange for testimonials.
7. Set a weekly routine | Fixed days per client so you can plan shopping routes and prep.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Personal chefs need a commercial kitchen.
Fact: Many cook in clients' kitchens, which can need less approval. A commercial or registered kitchen becomes necessary when you prepare food elsewhere and deliver it.
Myth: Including groceries in one price is simpler for clients.
Fact: It hides your margin and exposes you when food prices rise. A service fee plus groceries at cost is clearer and fairer.
:::

:::faq
### Do I need a license to be a personal chef?
It depends on the model and location. Delivering food you cooked in your own kitchen usually needs food business registration (UK) or a health permit (US). Cooking only in clients' kitchens may need less, so ask your council or health department. In Nigeria, register with the CAC and keep a food handler medical certificate.
### How much do personal chefs charge?
Most charge a service fee for their time plus groceries at cost. Weekly meal prep is often priced per session; private dinners per guest with a minimum.
### How much does it cost to start a personal chef business?
Often a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars or pounds for training, insurance, equipment and marketing, since you can use clients' kitchens.
### What is the difference between a personal chef and a private chef?
A personal chef usually serves several clients, often cooking a week's meals in each client's kitchen. A private chef usually works for one household, sometimes full-time or live-in.
### How many clients does a personal chef need?
Fewer than most people think. Six weekly meal prep clients at a $300 service fee is $1,800 a week before costs. Retention matters more than finding many new clients.
:::
