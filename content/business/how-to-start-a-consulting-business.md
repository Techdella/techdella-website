You can start a consulting business without having worked as a consultant, because clients don't buy consulting experience. They buy a solved problem. If you've spent years doing something well inside a company, such as cutting supplier costs, setting up payroll, running HR for a 50-person team or launching products, that is consulting material.

To start, pick one problem for one type of client, turn it into a fixed-scope offer with a price, register the business, and land a small paid pilot through people who already trust you. This guide shows how, with the registration and tax basics for the US, UK and Nigeria.

:::takeaways
- "No experience" usually means no consulting title. Your job experience is the product.
- Narrow beats broad: "procurement audits for mid-sized hospitals" sells faster than "business consulting."
- Price the outcome as a package, not your hours.
- A paid pilot at a reduced price beats free work. It proves someone will pay.
:::

## What does a consultant actually do?

A consultant diagnoses a problem, recommends what to do, and often helps implement it. The work tends to fall into three shapes:

- **Diagnosis:** an audit or assessment with a written report and priorities.
- **Project:** a fixed piece of work with a deliverable, such as a new pricing model or a hiring process.
- **Advisory retainer:** a set number of hours or calls each month.

New consultants should lead with diagnosis. It's the easiest to scope, the easiest for a client to say yes to, and it often turns into a project.

## How to start a consulting business with no experience

:::steps "Seven steps to your first paying client"
1. List what you've already solved | Write down ten problems you've fixed at work and the result. Circle the ones a business would pay to fix again.
2. Choose one client type | Pick an industry and company size you understand. You'll speak their language, which is half the sale.
3. Write a one-line offer | "I help [client] get [result] in [time] through [method]." If you can't fill this in, the niche is still too broad.
4. Build a fixed-scope first offer | A 2- to 3-week diagnostic with a written report and a 60-minute debrief. Clear deliverable, clear price.
5. Register and set up | Form the business, open a separate bank account, and get a simple contract and invoice template.
6. Sell a paid pilot | Offer the diagnostic to three people in your network at a reduced launch price, in exchange for a testimonial if you deliver.
7. Publish proof | Turn each project into an anonymized one-page case study. That's how strangers start to trust you.
:::

## How to price consulting: a worked example

Hourly pricing punishes you for being fast. Price the package instead, but check it against an hourly floor so you never lose money.

- Target income: $90,000 a year. Add $10,000 for costs. Total: **$100,000**.
- Realistic billable hours: 20 per week × 44 weeks = **880 hours**. The rest is selling, admin and learning.
- Floor rate: $100,000 ÷ 880 = **about $114 per hour**.
- Your diagnostic takes 24 hours of work: 24 × $114 = $2,736. Round to a package price of **$3,000**.
- Sell two diagnostics and one $2,500 monthly retainer each month, and you're above target.

In Nigeria, the arithmetic is the same, but in naira and with local salaries as the benchmark. Many Nigerian consultants also serve diaspora-owned or foreign firms and price those projects in dollars or pounds.

:::callout tip "Your first price will feel too high"
That's normal. If the first five prospects all say yes immediately, your price is too low. Raise it for the next five.
:::

## How to get consulting clients when nobody knows you yet

- **Start with warm contacts.** Former managers, colleagues and suppliers already trust you. Send each a short note with your one-line offer and ask who they know with that problem.
- **Teach in public.** Write one practical post a week about the problem you solve. Specific, useful posts get forwarded to the people who hire.
- **Partner with adjacent providers.** Accountants, agencies and software vendors meet your clients first. Offer referral fees or co-delivered workshops.

## Myths about starting a consulting business

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need an MBA or a big-firm background to be a consultant.
Fact: Clients hire for a solved problem. Proven results in a specific area often matter more than a credential.
Myth: Working for free is the best way to get your first clients.
Fact: Free work attracts people who don't value it. A reduced-price pilot still proves demand and sets the right relationship.
Myth: More services means more clients.
Fact: A long service list makes it harder for buyers to know what you're best at. One clear offer is easier to refer.
:::

:::faq
### Can I start a consulting business with no degree?
Yes. Most consulting fields are not licensed, so what clients check is your track record. Some areas, such as legal, tax, medical or engineering advice, have professional rules, so check your field's regulator before offering regulated advice.
### What is the easiest consulting niche to start in?
The easiest niche is the one closest to work you've already done with measurable results. Someone who ran operations in logistics will sell logistics process audits faster than generic "business strategy," because they have examples and vocabulary.
### How much should a new consultant charge?
Work out your hourly floor from your income target, costs and realistic billable hours, then package your work above that floor. Many new consultants start with a fixed-price diagnostic so the client knows the cost up front.
### Do I need a website to start consulting?
Not on day one, but you'll need one soon. Prospects check you before they reply. A single page with your offer, process, a case study and a way to book a call is enough to start.
### Should I register as a sole trader or a limited company?
Sole trader (UK) or sole proprietor (US) is simpler and cheaper. A limited company or LLC separates business liability from your personal assets and can suit you better as income grows. In Nigeria, many consultants start with a CAC business name. Ask an accountant once revenue is steady.
:::
