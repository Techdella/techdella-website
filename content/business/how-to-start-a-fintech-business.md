To start a fintech business, pick a specific financial problem, decide early whether you will hold your own licence or partner with a licensed bank or provider, map the regulator for your model, build a minimum viable product with identity checks and security from the first release, and budget for compliance and trust as seriously as you budget for engineering. In fintech, the licence and the partner bank often decide your launch date more than your code does.

This guide explains how to start a fintech business in Nigeria, the US and the UK: models, licensing routes, costs and how to win users who are cautious with money.

:::takeaways
- Most early fintechs launch through a licensed partner (banking-as-a-service, a payment processor or a microfinance bank) before seeking their own licence.
- Regulators differ by model and country: CBN in Nigeria, FinCEN plus state regulators in the US, the FCA in the UK.
- KYC, fraud controls and data protection belong in the MVP, not in version two.
- Trust is a growth channel. Clear pricing, visible licensing and fast support convert cautious users.
:::

## Which fintech business model should you start?

| Model | What you do | Typical licensing route | Time to launch | Capital intensity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payments and collections | Help businesses accept or send money | Partner with a licensed processor, or hold a payment licence | Fast via partner | Medium |
| Wallets and e-money | Hold customer balances | Own e-money/mobile money licence or partner bank | Slow | High |
| Lending | Credit to consumers or SMEs | Lending licence (varies by country and state) | Medium | High (loan book) |
| Savings and investment | Help users save or invest | Partner with licensed managers or banks | Medium | Medium |
| B2B fintech software (invoicing, payroll, treasury) | Software that touches money | Often no licence if you don't hold or move funds | Fast | Low to medium |
| Regtech and compliance tools | KYC, AML, reporting for others | Usually no financial licence | Fast | Low to medium |

The quickest route to market is usually software that doesn't hold customer funds, or a regulated product launched through a licensed partner.

## What licences does a fintech business need?

**Nigeria.** The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) licenses payment businesses by category. Under a CBN circular of 9 December 2020, the minimum capital was set at ₦50 million for super agents, ₦100 million for payment terminal service providers (PTSPs) and payment solution service providers (PSSPs), and ₦2 billion for mobile money operators and switching and processing companies ([Templars, 2020](https://www.templars-law.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Newsletter-on-CBN-Payments-System-Circular.pdf)). Check the [CBN](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/) for any revisions before you plan capital. Lending, microfinance and savings products sit under other CBN or state frameworks. Register the company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/), and follow the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 ([NDPC](https://ndpc.gov.ng/)).

**United States.** Money services businesses must register with FinCEN within 180 days of being established and renew every two years ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/money-services-business-msb-registration)). Money transmission is also licensed state by state, so many startups partner with a licensed bank or provider at first. Lending, investment and crypto products have their own federal and state rules.

**United Kingdom.** The FCA authorizes or registers payment and e-money firms. Authorization as a payment institution is required where projected monthly average payment transactions exceed €3 million over 12 months; smaller firms can register as small payment institutions. E-money firms need authorization where average outstanding e-money exceeds €5 million ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/apply-emoney-payment-institution)). Applications must cover capital, safeguarding and wind-down arrangements.

Get a fintech lawyer involved before you design the product. The licence you need depends on exactly where money sits and who touches it.

## How much does it cost to start a fintech business?

| Cost item | Nigeria (NGN) | US (USD) | UK (GBP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company registration | CAC fee (check current fee) | State fees vary; EIN free | £100 online |
| Regulatory capital (if licensed) | ₦50 million to ₦2 billion depending on CBN category (2020 circular) | Set by each state for money transmitters | Set by FCA rules for your category |
| Legal and compliance setup | ₦10,000,000–₦60,000,000 | $50,000–$300,000 | £40,000–£250,000 |
| MVP design and build | ₦20,000,000–₦120,000,000 | $60,000–$300,000 | £50,000–£250,000 |
| KYC, fraud and infrastructure (monthly) | ₦500,000–₦5,000,000 | $2,000–$20,000 | £1,500–£15,000 |
| Security audit and penetration test | ₦5,000,000–₦25,000,000 | $15,000–$60,000 | £12,000–£50,000 |

Capital figures come from the sources above. All other amounts are estimates from typical quotes we've seen and vary widely by model. Treat them as early planning numbers, and confirm legal, compliance and banking costs with your advisers before you commit.

## What does a realistic fintech revenue model look like?

Worked example (illustrative), a B2B payments product in Nigeria that partners with a licensed processor:

- You charge merchants 1.5% per transaction; your partner charges you 1.0%.
- Your net take rate: 1.5% − 1.0% = **0.5%**.
- One merchant processes ₦20,000,000 a month → 0.5% × ₦20,000,000 = **₦100,000** a month in gross margin.
- To cover ₦15,000,000 of monthly costs (team, infrastructure, compliance), you need ₦15,000,000 ÷ ₦100,000 = **150 merchants** of that size.

That arithmetic is why fintechs focus early on transaction volume per customer, not just signups, and why many add software fees or value-added services on top of payments.

## How to start a fintech business: step by step

:::steps "From idea to compliant launch"
1. Pick one problem and one user | For example, "collections for Lagos schools" or "invoicing for UK freelancers paid in dollars".
2. Map the money flow | Where funds sit, who moves them, which party is regulated. This decides your licence route.
3. Choose your route | Own licence, licensed partner, or software that never holds funds.
4. Build a compliant MVP | KYC, transaction monitoring, audit logs, encryption and clear terms from version one.
5. Test with a small group | A closed pilot with real users and capped volumes.
6. Prepare compliance documents | AML policy, data protection policy, complaints process and incident response.
7. Launch, measure, then apply | Use traction and partner experience to support a licence application if you need one.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need your own licence before you can launch a fintech.
Fact: Many fintechs launch through a licensed partner and apply for their own licence once the model is proven. The right route depends on where customer funds sit.
Myth: Compliance can wait until after product-market fit.
Fact: KYC, AML controls and data protection affect your product design, partner approvals and launch timing. Retrofitting them is slower and more expensive.
:::

:::checklist "Before you onboard real users"
- Money flow mapped and licence route confirmed with a lawyer
- Company registered; regulator registrations started
- Partner bank or processor agreement signed (if using one)
- KYC and transaction monitoring in the product
- AML, data protection and complaints policies written
- Security testing completed
- Fees and terms published clearly
- Support channel staffed
:::

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a fintech company in Nigeria?
It depends on the model. Software that doesn't hold funds can start with an MVP budget and legal advice. Licensed payment businesses need CBN minimum capital: ₦50 million to ₦2 billion by category under the December 2020 circular, plus compliance and infrastructure.
### Do I need a CBN licence to start a fintech?
Only if you carry out a regulated activity yourself, such as switching, mobile money or payment processing. Many startups partner with a CBN-licensed provider first.
### What licences does a US fintech need?
Money services businesses register with FinCEN within 180 days and renew every two years. Money transmission also needs state licences. Lending and investment products have their own rules.
### What does the FCA require for a UK payments startup?
Payment institutions need FCA authorization above €3 million in projected monthly average transactions; smaller firms may register instead. E-money firms need authorization above €5 million average outstanding e-money.
### How long does it take to launch a fintech?
Through a licensed partner, a focused MVP can launch in months. Getting your own licence usually takes longer, depending on the regulator and how complete your application is.
:::
