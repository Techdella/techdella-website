To start a podcast business, decide first how the show will make money: sponsorships, selling your own products or services, paid subscriptions, or producing podcasts for other businesses. Then choose a narrow audience, launch with a few episodes and a schedule you can keep, and register the business before money comes in. Most podcasts that pay their way do it by serving a specific audience well, not by chasing huge download numbers.

This guide covers how to start a podcast business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, with a setup budget, a revenue model comparison and the ad rules for sponsored episodes.

:::takeaways
- Podcast listening is mainstream: most Americans aged 12+ have listened to or watched one.
- A small, focused audience can earn more through your own offers than through ads.
- Video matters: many listeners now watch podcasts too, so record video from day one if you can.
- Sponsored reads are advertising. Disclose them in every market.
:::

## Step 1: Choose your business model

| Model | How you earn | What you need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsorship and ads | Brands pay per episode or per thousand downloads | A consistent, measurable audience | Shows with a defined niche and steady growth |
| Your own products or services | The show builds trust; you sell coaching, courses, consulting or products | Something to sell and a way to capture leads | Experts and business owners |
| Paid membership | Listeners pay for bonus episodes, ad-free feeds or community | A loyal core audience | Shows with superfans |
| Production services | You produce podcasts for companies | Editing, audio and project management skills | Producers and editors |
| Live events and licensing | Live shows, syndication, clips licensed to media | An established brand | Mature shows |

For most new podcasters, the second model is the fastest to profit. A show with 500 listeners who are all potential clients can earn more than a general-interest show with 10,000 downloads.

## Step 2: Define your niche and format

Pick a listener you can describe in one sentence, such as "first-time founders in Lagos raising their first round" or "UK nurses thinking about leaving the NHS". Then choose a format you can sustain:

- **Solo:** easiest to schedule; needs strong ideas each week.
- **Co-hosted:** natural conversation; needs two committed people.
- **Interview:** builds networks and borrows guests' audiences; needs booking time.
- **Narrative:** high quality; takes the most production work.

Plan 10 episode ideas before you record. If you can't, the niche may be too narrow or not yours.

## Step 3: Budget your setup

These are planning estimates for a solo or two-person show, not quotes.

| Cost item | Planning estimate (US / UK) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| USB or XLR microphone | $60–$250 / £50–£220 each | A good dynamic mic forgives untreated rooms |
| Headphones | $30–$150 / £25–£130 | Closed-back for monitoring |
| Audio interface (for XLR mics) | $0–$200 / £0–£180 | Not needed for USB mics |
| Camera or webcam for video | $0–$400 / £0–£350 | A recent phone works to start |
| Room treatment | $50–$300 / £40–£250 | Soft furnishings and panels |
| Remote recording and editing software | $0–$40 a month | Many tools have free tiers |
| Podcast hosting | $0–$30 a month | Distributes your RSS feed to apps |
| Artwork and branding | $0–$500 / £0–£400 | Cover art must read at thumbnail size |

In Nigeria, add a power backup for recording sessions (a dropped session mid-interview is painful), noise control in busy areas, and reliable internet for remote interviews and uploads. Imported gear prices follow the exchange rate.

## Step 4: Register the business and follow ad rules

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business registration | LLC or sole proprietorship; free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) | Sole trader (Self Assessment once trading income tops £1,000) or £100 limited company ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Sponsored content | Disclose material connections clearly, in the audio itself and not only in show notes ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)) | Ads must be obviously identifiable; label sponsored content clearly ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)) | ARCON requires pre-exposure approval of adverts, including sponsored digital content ([ARCON](https://arcon.gov.ng/)) |
| Music and clips | Use licensed or royalty-free music; copyright applies to clips | Same; licences are needed for commercial music | Same; get written permission for music and clips |

:::callout warning "Say it in the audio"
The FTC says disclosures in videos should be both on screen and spoken, not just in a description. For audio-only episodes, say "this episode is sponsored by…" before the read.
:::

## Step 5: Do the sponsorship maths

Sponsorship income depends on downloads per episode and what a sponsor will pay per thousand. Here's how to estimate it.

**Worked example (replace with your numbers):**

- Average downloads per episode in the first 30 days: 2,000.
- Assume a sponsor pays $20 per 1,000 downloads for one mid-roll read.
- One read per episode: 2,000 ÷ 1,000 × $20 = **$40 per episode**.
- Four episodes a month: **$160 a month**.

Now compare the second model. If 2,000 listeners include 1% who buy a $300 coaching package in a year, that's 20 × $300 = **$6,000**. That's why many small shows earn more by selling their own offer than from ads. Sponsorship becomes meaningful at larger audiences or in niches where sponsors pay a premium to reach specific buyers.

:::myth-fact
Myth: You need a huge audience to make money from a podcast.
Fact: A small, specific audience can be very valuable if you sell your own services or products, or attract niche sponsors who want exactly those listeners.
Myth: Expensive gear is the key to a professional podcast.
Fact: Room sound, good microphone technique and clean editing matter more than price. Many shows start with a single USB mic.
:::

## Step 6: Launch and grow

:::steps "Launch plan"
1. Record three episodes | Launching with several gives new listeners a reason to subscribe.
2. Write a trailer | One to two minutes that tells the ideal listener why to follow.
3. Publish everywhere | Submit your feed to the main podcast apps and upload video to YouTube.
4. Cut clips | Turn each episode into three to five short clips for social media.
5. Build an email list | Offer show notes, resources or a free guide on your website.
6. Book guests with audiences | Ask each guest to share their episode.
:::

:::quote "Joanna Okedara-Kalu" "Founder & CEO, TechDella"
Downloads don't help much if you can't reach your listeners directly. From the first episode, I'd give people a reason to join your email list: show notes, a resource, a checklist from a guest. Sponsorships and your own products both depend on an audience you can contact. The apps own the feed; your list is the part of the business you actually own.
:::

## Podcast business FAQs

:::faq
### How much does it cost to start a podcast business?
A basic setup with a USB microphone, headphones, free editing software and low-cost hosting can start for under $200 or £200. Video, multiple hosts and room treatment raise costs, but rarely above a few thousand.
### How do podcasts make money?
Through sponsorships and ads, selling the host's own products or services, paid memberships, producing shows for other companies, and live events. Smaller shows can earn more from their own offers than from ads.
### Do I need a website for my podcast?
It's strongly recommended. A website gives each episode a searchable page, lets you collect emails, and gives sponsors and guests a professional place to learn about the show.
### Do I have to disclose sponsors on my podcast?
Yes. In the US, the FTC expects clear disclosure of material connections. In the UK, the ASA requires ads to be obviously identifiable. In Nigeria, ARCON requires approval of adverts, including sponsored digital content.
### Should I record video for my podcast?
If you can, yes. Many people now watch podcasts as well as listen, and video clips are useful for social media. A phone or webcam is enough to start.
:::
