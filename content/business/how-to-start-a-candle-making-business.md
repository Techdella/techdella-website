To start a candle-making business from home, develop a small range and burn-test it until it performs safely, work out the full cost of every candle, add the safety and hazard labels your market requires, register the business, and start selling through one online shop plus local markets. Candles are a fire risk and scented candles contain chemicals, so testing and labelling aren't optional extras; they're the job.

This guide covers how to start a candle-making business in the US, the UK and Nigeria, with the safety rules, a cost breakdown and pricing maths that holds up.

:::takeaways
- Burn-test every wax, wick, fragrance and container combination before you sell it.
- In Great Britain, scented candles fall under CLP hazard labelling; safety labels follow BS EN 15494.
- In the US, ASTM fire-safety and labelling standards are voluntary but widely expected by retailers.
- Price from your full unit cost, including packaging, fees and your time, then check your margin covers wholesale.
:::

The point isn't to scare you off. It's that the makers who last are the ones with tested products, clear warning labels and records to prove it. That's also what wholesale buyers check before they stock you.

## Step 1: Learn the craft and develop a small range

Start with one wax type and three to five scents. Soy, rapeseed (common in the UK), coconut blends and paraffin all behave differently. For each candle, record:

- Wax, wick size, fragrance load (percentage), dye and container.
- Burn test results: flame height, melt pool, sooting, container temperature and how it behaves at the end of its life.
- Cure time before testing.

Keep these records. They become your due-diligence file if anything goes wrong.

## Step 2: Follow the product rules in your market

| | United States | United Kingdom | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product safety | No mandatory federal performance standard for candles (lead-cored wicks are banned), but the [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Candles) points makers to ASTM F2417 (fire safety) and F2058 (fire-safety labelling). Candles containing a hazardous substance under the FHSA need cautionary labelling. | The General Product Safety Regulations 2005 apply. Scented candles, wax melts and diffusers fall under GB CLP hazard labelling, overseen by the HSE. Safety warnings follow BS EN 15494, with fire-safety and sooting standards alongside. | Locally manufactured products are covered by the [Standards Organisation of Nigeria](https://son.gov.ng/mancapservice/) and its MANCAP certification scheme against Nigerian Industrial Standards. Ask SON which standard applies to candles. |
| Business registration | LLC or sole proprietorship; free EIN from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number); sales tax permit in most states | Sole trader (Self Assessment once trading income tops £1,000) or £100 limited company ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)) | Business name or company with the [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.ng/); TIN |
| Home production | Check zoning and your home insurance | Tell your home insurer; check tenancy terms | Check estate or landlord rules on production at home |

:::callout warning "Fragrance oils change your label"
In Great Britain, many fragrance oils are classified as hazardous, so a scented candle may need a CLP label with pictograms, hazard statements and your UK address. Ask your fragrance supplier for the safety data sheet and a CLP calculation for your fragrance load, and check Trading Standards guidance.
:::

These are planning estimates for a small home setup, not quotes.

| Product liability insurance | $300–$1,000 / £150–£600 a year | Ask markets and shops what cover they require |
| Online shop, branding, photography | $200–$2,000 / £150–£1,500 | Good photos sell candles |
| Safety kit | $50–$150 / £40–£120 | Fire extinguisher, fire blanket, gloves |

In Nigeria, check that imported fragrance oils come with safety data sheets, budget for steady power or a gas-based melting setup, and store wax away from heat during hot months. Price imported supplies in naira on the day you buy.

## Step 4: Price from unit cost

**Worked example: one 200 g scented candle (replace with your numbers):**

- Wax: 180 g at $0.012 per gram = $2.16
- Fragrance at 8%: 16 g at $0.08 per gram = $1.28
- Wick and sticker: $0.30
- Jar and lid: $1.60
- Label, warning sticker and box: $0.90
- Labour: 6 minutes per candle at $20 an hour = $2.00
- **Unit cost: $8.24**

A common rule of thumb is to set a wholesale price around twice your cost ($16.50) and retail around twice wholesale ($33). If $33 feels high for your market, look at cheaper containers or larger batches before cutting price, because a retail price that leaves no wholesale margin locks you out of shops later. Selling only direct? Remember online marketplace and payment fees come off the top.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Natural wax candles don't need safety warnings.
Fact: Any candle can start a fire. Warning labels about burning within sight and away from flammables apply to every candle, whatever the wax.
Myth: A strong scent means a better candle.
Fact: Overloading fragrance can cause sooting and poor burning, and in Great Britain it can change your CLP hazard label. Test to find the load that performs safely.
:::

## Candle-making business FAQs

:::faq
### How much money do I need to start a candle-making business from home?
A small home setup with equipment, first materials, containers, labels, insurance and a simple online shop can start from a few hundred dollars or pounds. Our planning estimate is roughly $1,150–$5,850 in the US or £810–£4,320 in the UK, depending on how much stock you buy upfront.
### Is candle-making profitable?
It can be if you price from full unit cost, including labour and packaging, and keep enough margin for wholesale. Profit improves with bigger batches, repeat customers and seasonal ranges.
### Do I need a license to sell homemade candles?
Not usually a specific candle licence, but you must register the business, follow product safety rules and label correctly. In Great Britain, scented candles often need CLP hazard labels. In Nigeria, ask the Standards Organisation of Nigeria about certification.
### What labels does a candle need?
At minimum, fire-safety warnings: burn within sight, keep away from flammables, and keep out of reach of children and pets. In Great Britain, scented candles may also need CLP labels. US makers commonly follow ASTM F2058.
### Where can I sell my candles?
Start with one online shop and local markets, then approach gift shops with a wholesale price list. An email list helps sell new scents and seasonal launches.
:::
