To build a startup team without burning your budget, start from outcomes, not job titles. Write down what must happen in the next six months, such as "ship the MVP," "sign ten pilot customers," "keep the books clean," and decide which of those need someone every day. Cover those with founders or a few early hires, and use contractors, agencies or fractional specialists for everything else. Add full-time people only when the work is constant and revenue or funding can carry the cost.

This guide covers who to hire first, how to compare employees and contractors, what an employee actually costs in the US, UK and Nigeria, and how to scale without losing focus.

:::takeaways
- Hire for outcomes over the next six months, not for an org chart you might need in three years.
- The core team builds and sells. Almost everything else can start part-time or outsourced.
- An employee costs more than salary: taxes, pensions, insurance, equipment and management time.
- Most businesses stay small. Growing headcount is a choice, not a milestone.
:::

## Most businesses are small, and that's fine

It's easy to assume a "real" startup needs a big team. The data says otherwise. In the UK, three in four private sector businesses have no employees beyond the owners.

Small doesn't mean stuck. Many companies grow revenue a long way with a tight core team and good partners. The skill is knowing which roles must be in-house.

## Who should be on your first startup team?

Every early startup needs three capabilities covered, by someone:

| Capability | What it means early on | In-house or outsourced? |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Design, develop and maintain the product | Core: a technical founder or first engineer, often supported by a development partner |
| Sell | Talk to customers, close deals, learn what they need | Core: usually a founder |
| Run | Finance, legal, admin, payroll | Outsource to a bookkeeper, accountant and lawyer at first |
| Market | Positioning, website, search, content, campaigns | Agency or fractional lead until there's a full-time workload |
| Support | Onboarding and customer help | Founders first, then a hire when volume is steady |

If a founder can't cover build or sell, that's your first hire or co-founder search. Everything else can start part-time.

## How to build a startup team, step by step

:::steps
1. List outcomes for the next six months | "Launch MVP," "ten paying pilots," "monthly reporting in place." Not titles.
2. Map who owns each outcome | Write a name next to each, even if it's a founder for now.
3. Find the gaps that need daily work | Those are your candidates for early full-time hires.
4. Fill the rest flexibly | Contractors for defined projects, agencies for execution, fractional leaders for strategy.
5. Write clear scopes | Every contractor gets deliverables, deadlines and a budget in writing.
6. Hire generalists first | Early employees who can handle several jobs are worth more than narrow specialists.
7. Set ownership and rhythm | One owner per outcome, a weekly check-in and a shared task board.
8. Document as you go | Processes, logins and decisions in one place, so new people ramp up fast.
9. Hire when the work is constant | When a contractor is busy every week and growth pays for it, convert the role to full time.
:::

## What does an employee really cost?

Salary is only part of it. Employer obligations differ by country:

:::stats "Employer costs and duties to plan for"
10% + 8% | minimum Nigerian pension contributions: employer 10% and employee 8% of monthly emoluments | National Pension Commission FAQ, 2018 | https://www.pencom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/FAQ-CPS-reviewed.-17-Apr.-2018.pdf
3 staff | Nigeria's contributory pension rules are not mandatory for organizations with fewer than three employees | National Pension Commission FAQ, 2018 | https://www.pencom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/FAQ-CPS-reviewed.-17-Apr.-2018.pdf
$135,980 | US median annual wage for software developers, May 2025 | US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026 | https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm
:::

**United States.** Every new employee completes Form I-9 for work eligibility and a Form W-4 for tax withholding, and you report wages on Form W-2 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/hiring-employees)). Add payroll taxes, any benefits and state rules.

**United Kingdom.** Before your first payday, register as an employer and set up PAYE, check National Minimum Wage, set up a workplace pension and get Employers' Liability insurance ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/employing-staff)).

**Nigeria.** Register for PAYE with your state internal revenue service, and once you have three or more employees, register with a pension fund administrator and remit contributions ([PenCom](https://www.pencom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/FAQ-CPS-reviewed.-17-Apr.-2018.pdf)).

## Employee vs contractor: a worked example

Illustrative numbers for a marketing role. Use your own salary benchmarks and quotes.

**Full-time marketing manager**
- Salary: 60,000
- Employer taxes, pension and benefits at an assumed 20%: 12,000
- Equipment, software and training: 3,000
- **Total: 75,000 a year, or 6,250 a month**

**Fractional marketing lead plus an agency**
- Fractional lead, 2 days a month at 600 a day: 1,200 a month
- Agency execution retainer: 2,500 a month
- **Total: 3,700 a month, or 44,400 a year**

The flexible setup costs 75,000 − 44,400 = **30,600 less** a year, and you can scale it up or down monthly. The trade-off is less day-to-day availability and context. Once marketing needs someone every day, the full-time hire starts to make sense. ## How to hire your first employees well

When a role is ready to become full-time, a simple process avoids most early-hire regrets:

- **Write a scorecard, not a job description.** List the three outcomes the person must deliver in their first six months.
- **Use a paid work sample.** A short, realistic task shows more than any interview answer.
- **Check references properly.** Ask former managers what the person needed help with, not just what they did well.
- **Agree a 90-day plan.** Clear goals for the first three months, with a check-in every two weeks.
- **Offer equity carefully.** If you grant shares or options, use proper documents with vesting so ownership matches contribution.

## Build culture before you hire

Culture in a five-person startup is just how you work. Write it down early:

- **How decisions get made** and who has the final say on each area.
- **How you communicate:** which tools, response times, when to meet and when to write.
- **What "done" means:** quality bar, review steps, who signs off.
- **How you treat contractors:** as part of the team, with access to context and goals.

Before hiring for repetitive work, see if software can do it. Automated lead routing, email follow-ups, invoicing reminders and reporting can save hours each week. :::myth-fact
Myth: A bigger team means faster growth.
Fact: Every hire adds communication and management overhead. Small teams with clear ownership often move faster.
Myth: Contractors can't be trusted with important work.
Fact: With clear scopes, deadlines and access to context, contractors and agencies deliver core work for many startups. Poor briefs cause most failures.
Myth: You should hire specialists from day one.
Fact: Early on, needs change monthly. Generalists who can handle several jobs adapt better until the work is stable enough for specialists.
:::

:::checklist "Before your next hire"
- The outcome this person will own is written down
- The work is needed every week, not occasionally
- You've priced the full cost, not just salary
- Revenue or funding covers at least 12 months of the role
- A contractor or fractional option has been compared
- Onboarding docs, tools and access are ready
- Employer registrations for your country are in place
:::

:::faq
### How many people should a startup have at the start?
As few as can cover building and selling well, often two or three founders or early team members. Use contractors and partners for everything else until the work is constant enough to justify full-time roles.
### Should startups hire full-time employees first?
Usually only for core, everyday work such as building the product. For specialist or occasional work, contractors, agencies and fractional leaders are cheaper and more flexible at the start.
### What is the biggest startup hiring mistake?
Hiring ahead of need: adding people for an org chart you expect to have, before the work or revenue exists. It burns cash and adds management overhead before it adds output.
### How do I hire a startup team with no money?
Start with co-founders who share equity and risk, then use contractors paid per project once there's early revenue. Some founders also offer equity to early contributors, which needs proper legal documents and vesting.
### When should a startup convert a contractor into an employee?
When the role is needed every week, the person understands the business deeply, and you can afford the full cost for at least a year. Check your country's employment rules too, because some long-term contractor arrangements are treated as employment.
:::
