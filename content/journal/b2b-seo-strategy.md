A B2B SEO strategy is a plan for showing up when business buyers search for the problems you solve, and for turning those visits into sales conversations. The short version: find the questions buyers ask at each stage of a purchase, build the pages that answer them (decision-stage pages first), keep the site technically clean, earn credible mentions, and measure the result in pipeline rather than traffic.

That last point is where most B2B SEO goes wrong. A blog that pulls 40,000 visits a month from people who will never buy is a cost center. A dozen pages that rank for the searches your buyers make in the week before they shortlist vendors can pay for the whole program.

:::takeaways
- Start with the searches closest to revenue: comparisons, alternatives, pricing and "best X for Y" queries. Then work back to education.
- Build one topic cluster per offer, not one blog post per keyword.
- Most buyers research without you. If your pages aren't in that research, you aren't on the shortlist.
- Measure SEO by qualified leads and pipeline. Rankings and traffic are leading indicators, not results.
- Write for AI answers as well as blue links: clear, answer-first passages with sources.
:::

## Why B2B SEO matters more when buyers avoid sales reps

B2B buyers do most of their homework alone. They search, read, compare and ask AI assistants long before they fill in a form. By the time they talk to you, the shortlist is usually set.

Put those together and the job of B2B SEO becomes clear. It isn't to "get traffic." It's to make sure your company is part of the research that builds the shortlist.

Search results are also changing shape. When Google shows an AI summary, people click fewer traditional links:

That doesn't make SEO less useful. It changes what "winning" looks like. Informational clicks are thinner, so the value moves to two places: decision-stage queries (where people still click to compare vendors), and being the source the AI summary draws on. ## How B2B SEO differs from consumer SEO

The mechanics are the same. Google crawls, indexes and ranks pages in the same way. What changes is the buyer.

| | Consumer SEO | B2B SEO |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | One person, often quickly | A buying group, over weeks or months |
| Search volume | High; thousands of searches per term | Low; 20 to 500 searches a month is common for buyer terms |
| Value of one visit | Usually small | One visit can be one contract |
| What converts | Product pages, reviews, price | Comparisons, proof, pricing logic, integrations, security answers |
| Main KPI | Revenue per session | Qualified leads, pipeline, win rate |
| Content tone | Broad appeal | Specific to role, industry and use case |

The low-volume row trips people up. A keyword tool shows "30 searches a month" and the term gets skipped. In B2B, 30 searches a month from heads of finance comparing payment platforms is worth more than 3,000 searches from students.

## The B2B SEO strategy framework

This is the order we use when we build a B2B SEO strategy. Each step depends on the one before it.

:::steps "Seven steps, in order"
1. Pin down the offer and the buyer | Pick the one service or product you most want to sell this year, the industry you sell it to, and the roles in the buying group.
2. Map searches to buying stages | Do keyword research by stage: problem, solution, vendor comparison and decision. Talk to sales before you open a keyword tool.
3. Build decision pages first | Service pages, comparisons, alternatives, pricing logic and industry pages sit closest to revenue. They come before blog posts.
4. Add a topic cluster around each offer | A pillar page plus focused supporting articles, all linked to each other and to the offer page.
5. Fix the technical floor | Crawlable, indexable, fast pages with clean internal links. No orphan pages, no duplicate templates.
6. Earn authority | Links and mentions from places your buyers already trust: partners, industry publications, data people want to cite.
7. Measure pipeline | Track leads, qualified leads and opportunities from organic search, by page. Prune or rewrite what doesn't contribute.
:::

### 1. Pin down the offer and the buyer

SEO can't fix vague positioning. If your homepage says you "help businesses grow," there is no search query to rank for. Write down three things before any keyword research:

- **The offer.** One service or product, named the way buyers name it.
- **The industry or segment.** "Fintech companies in Nigeria and the US" is a strategy. "Businesses" is not.
- **The buying group.** Who searches, who signs, and who can veto. Forrester's 2026 buyer research found that a typical purchase involves 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers ([Forrester, 2026](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forrester-2026-the-state-of-business-buying/)). Each of them searches differently.

### 2. Map searches to buying stages

Good [keyword research](marketing:/glossary/keyword-research/) for B2B starts with people, not tools. Pull 20 recent sales call notes. List every question a prospect asked. Then check how those questions are phrased in search.

Group what you find into four stages:

| Stage | What the buyer is thinking | Example searches | Page type that fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | "Something is wrong. What is it?" | "why are our demo requests dropping" | Guide or diagnostic article |
| Solution | "What kinds of fixes exist?" | "outsourced SEO vs in-house SEO" | Comparison guide, framework article |
| Vendor | "Who does this well?" | "B2B SEO agency for fintech" | Service page, industry page, case study |
| Decision | "Is this one right for us?" | "[vendor] pricing", "[vendor] alternatives" | Pricing page, alternatives page, proof page |

Most B2B blogs are almost all Problem-stage content. That is the stage with the most AI summaries and the fewest buyers. Balance the plan so at least a third of your first 20 pages are Vendor or Decision pages.

### 3. Build decision pages first

These pages convert because the searcher already wants a solution. In practice that means:

- **One page per service**, written for the buyer's outcome, with proof and a clear next step.
- **Industry pages** that say something specific to that industry: its regulations, its channels, its buying cycle. Not a template with the industry name swapped in. Google's spam policies treat swapped-name pages as doorway pages.
- **Comparison and alternatives pages** that are fair. Name the trade-offs honestly; buyers can tell.
- **A pricing page**, even if it only explains how pricing works and what drives cost.

### 4. Build topic clusters around each offer

A [topic cluster](marketing:/glossary/topic-clusters/) is a pillar page that covers a subject broadly, plus supporting pages that each answer one narrower question, all linked together. Clusters help search engines understand what you're an authority on. They also give a reader a natural path from a question to your offer.

Rules we follow:

- One cluster per offer. Don't start the next cluster until the first one has its pillar page, at least six supporting pages and links into the offer page.
- Every supporting page links up to the pillar and across to two siblings.
- Every page in the cluster has one clear next step, usually the matching service page.

### 5. Fix the technical floor

[Technical SEO](marketing:/glossary/technical-seo/) isn't the strategy, but problems here cap everything else. The checks that matter most on B2B sites:

- **Indexing.** Important pages are indexable, in the XML sitemap and not blocked in robots.txt. Thank-you pages, internal documents and tag archives are noindexed.
- **Duplication.** One URL per page. Parameter variants (UTM tags, filters) point to the clean URL with a canonical tag.
- **Speed.** Pages pass Core Web Vitals on mobile. Heavy page builders and third-party scripts are the usual cause when they don't.
- **Internal links.** No orphan pages. Service pages get links from the blog, not just the menu.
- **Rendering.** Key text is in the HTML, not injected by JavaScript after load. This matters for AI crawlers too.

If you run WordPress, our [technical SEO service](marketing:/services/technical-seo/) covers these checks in detail.

### 6. Earn authority

Links still matter, but in B2B the best ones come from relevance, not volume. The sources worth pursuing:

- **Partners and integrations.** Listing pages, co-written guides, joint webinars.
- **Industry publications.** Contributed pieces with a real opinion or real data.
- **Original data.** A short survey of your customers, an analysis of your own anonymized numbers, a benchmark nobody else has.
- **Founders and experts.** Podcasts, panels and quotes in trade press.

Skip link schemes, paid guest-post networks and "link insertion" offers. They carry spam risk and rarely move B2B rankings.

### 7. Measure pipeline

Connect organic sessions to your CRM. At a minimum, record the landing page and source on every lead, then report by page: leads, qualified leads, opportunities and won revenue. Rankings and traffic tell you whether the engine is running. Pipeline tells you whether it's going anywhere.

## A worked example: what is one topic cluster worth?

Here is the arithmetic we use to decide whether a cluster is worth building. The inputs below are illustrative, not client data. Replace them with your own numbers.

Say a cluster targets 25 keywords with a combined 3,000 searches a month. You expect to rank on page one for about half of that demand within six months, at an average click-through rate of 5%:

- Visits: 3,000 × 50% × 5% = **75 visits a month**
- Leads at a 3% conversion rate on decision pages: 75 × 3% = **2.25 leads a month**, or **27 leads a year**
- Qualified leads at 40%: 27 × 40% = **10.8 a year**
- Deals at a 25% win rate: 10.8 × 25% = **2.7 deals a year**
- Revenue at a $15,000 average first-year contract: 2.7 × $15,000 = **$40,500 a year**

If the cluster costs $12,000 to build, it pays back in the first year on these assumptions. It also keeps working in year two at a much lower cost, because the pages already exist.

Now change one input. If the cluster targets problem-stage keywords and converts at 0.5% instead of 3%, the same traffic produces 4.5 leads a year and well under one deal. That's the case for building decision pages first.

## B2B SEO myths worth dropping

:::myth-fact
Myth: Longer content ranks better, so every article should be 3,000 words.
Fact: Google says it has no preferred word count ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Write as long as the question needs, and no longer.
Myth: Low-volume keywords aren't worth targeting.
Fact: In B2B, a term with 30 searches a month from the right buyers can outperform one with 3,000 from the wrong ones. Judge keywords by buyer fit and stage, then volume.
Myth: SEO always takes a year before anything happens.
Fact: Results depend on the site, the competition and the pages you start with. Decision pages on a healthy domain can move quickly; Paycape saw its first result in 50 days.
Myth: AI answers make SEO pointless.
Fact: AI features in Google draw on the same index, and Google says the same SEO fundamentals apply ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Strong SEO is the base for AI visibility.
:::

## Your first 90 days

:::checklist "B2B SEO strategy: the 90-day checklist"
- Weeks 1–2: choose one offer, one segment and the buying-group roles
- Weeks 1–2: pull questions from 20 sales calls and support tickets
- Weeks 2–3: run a technical audit; fix indexing, duplicates and orphan pages
- Weeks 2–4: map 40–60 keywords to the four buying stages
- Weeks 3–6: rewrite or build the offer page, one industry page and one comparison page
- Weeks 5–10: publish the pillar page and the first six supporting articles
- Weeks 6–12: pitch two partners and one industry publication for links or mentions
- Every week: log landing page and source on every lead in your CRM
- Day 90: report leads, qualified leads and pipeline by page; decide the next cluster
:::

## How to measure a B2B SEO strategy

Report on three layers, in this order of importance:

| Layer | Metrics | Where to find them | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business | Organic-sourced pipeline, won revenue, win rate | CRM | Monthly |
| Conversion | Leads and qualified leads by landing page | CRM plus analytics | Monthly |
| Visibility | Impressions, clicks, average position for priority pages; AI answer mentions | Google Search Console; a fixed prompt set | Weekly to monthly |

Google Search Console counts clicks and impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode inside the normal "Web" search type ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), so you won't see them separately there. Track AI answer visibility with a fixed set of prompts, as we explain in the [AI search guide](/how-to-get-cited-in-ai-search/).

If organic leads are healthy but pipeline isn't, the problem is usually after the click: weak offers, slow follow-up or forms that ask for too much. Our guide to [B2B lead generation](/b2b-lead-generation/) covers that side.

:::faq
### What is a B2B SEO strategy?
A B2B SEO strategy is a plan to rank for the searches business buyers make while they research a purchase, and to turn those visits into qualified leads. It covers which offer and buyers to target, keywords by buying stage, the pages to build, technical fixes, authority building and how results are measured in pipeline.
### How long does B2B SEO take to work?
It depends on your domain's history, your competitors and which pages you start with. Decision-stage pages on a healthy site can show movement within a couple of months; Paycape saw its first result in 50 days. Clusters of educational content usually take longer. Plan on a 6–12 month program and review progress every month.
### Should B2B companies focus on blog content or service pages?
Service, industry, comparison and pricing pages first, because they sit closest to revenue. Then build blog content in clusters that link back to those pages. A blog without strong decision pages tends to attract readers who never become buyers.
### How many keywords should a B2B SEO strategy target?
Start with 40 to 60 keywords for one offer, mapped across the four buying stages. That's enough for one cluster and its decision pages. Add the next offer's keywords once the first cluster is published and linked.
### Does AI search change B2B SEO?
Yes, in emphasis more than method. AI summaries reduce clicks on informational searches, so decision-stage pages matter more, and clear, sourced, answer-first writing helps you get cited in AI answers. Google says no special optimization is needed for its AI features beyond SEO fundamentals.
### How do I know if my B2B SEO is working?
Look at organic-sourced qualified leads and pipeline by landing page, monthly. Rising impressions and rankings are good early signs. If traffic grows but qualified leads don't, you're probably ranking for the wrong stage of search.
:::

## The short version

A B2B SEO strategy is a sales strategy that happens to run through search. Pick one offer, learn how buyers search for it, build the pages that win the shortlist, and measure the pipeline those pages create. Then do it again for the next offer.
