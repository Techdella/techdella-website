Q4 is the fourth quarter of the year: October 1 to December 31 if your fiscal year follows the calendar. A strong Q4 marketing strategy treats it as three different months, not one season. October is setup and early wins, November is peak demand (and peak ad prices), and December is closing deals and setting up January. Planning should start in August, because by October your competitors have already tested their offers.

:::takeaways
- Start Q4 planning in August. Test creative and offers in September, launch in early October.
- Plan October, November and December as three separate mini-strategies.
- Expect higher competition and ad costs in November. Budget from your targets and your real acquisition cost, not from last quarter's spend.
- Run four campaign types at once: urgency, value, relationship and pipeline building.
- Use December to build Q1 pipeline, not just to chase the last sale.
:::

## What does Q4 mean in business?

A quarter is a three-month period in a company's financial calendar. Four quarters of three months make a 12-month year. For a business whose fiscal year matches the calendar:

| Quarter | Months | Typical focus |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | January to March | Planning and fresh budgets |
| Q2 | April to June | Execution and growth |
| Q3 | July to September | Momentum and adjustment |
| Q4 | October to December | Finishing the year, peak seasonal demand |

Not every company uses the calendar year. Many businesses and governments run a different fiscal year, so their Q4 falls in a different season. If you sell to larger companies, ask when their fiscal year ends. That date, not December 31, is when their "spend it before we lose it" pressure peaks.

## Why Q4 matters more than other quarters

Q4 is different because buyer behavior changes, and so does the competition for their attention.

### Ecommerce and DTC: the holiday peak

For consumer brands, Q4 holds Black Friday, Cyber Monday and holiday gifting. The US Census Bureau's quarterly data shows how big the swing is.

:::chart "US retail e-commerce sales by quarter, 2025 (not seasonally adjusted, $ billions)" unit=$bn highlight=3
Q1 2025 | 275.7
Q2 2025 | 293.1
Q3 2025 | 299.7
Q4 2025 | 365.2
source: US Census Bureau, Quarterly Retail E-Commerce Sales, 4th Quarter 2025 (released March 2026) | https://www2.census.gov/retail/releases/historical/ecomm/25q4.pdf
:::

That's a 21.8% jump from Q3 to Q4, and Q4 alone was about 30% of 2025's $1,233.7 billion in US e-commerce sales ($365.2 billion ÷ $1,233.7 billion = 29.6%). More shoppers, but also more brands bidding for the same people.

### B2B: year-end budgets and planning

B2B buying in Q4 is driven by budget cycles. Teams with unspent budget may try to commit it before the fiscal year closes, and stalled deals can suddenly move. At the same time, this is when companies set next year's budgets and choose vendors. If you're not in those conversations in Q4, you start Q1 behind. December also gets quiet fast as holidays begin, so B2B deals that aren't moving by early December often slip to January.

### Every startup: the year-end scorecard

Investors, advisors and your own team judge the year by how you finish. A strong Q4 makes Q1 conversations easier, whether that's fundraising, hiring or renewals.

:::myth-fact
Myth: Q4 marketing starts in October.
Fact: October is launch. Planning, creative and testing need to happen in August and September, before auctions get crowded.
Myth: Everyone should spend more in Q4.
Fact: Spend more only where your acquisition cost still works at higher prices. If it doesn't, protect cash and push the budget into Q1.
Myth: Q4 is only for ecommerce.
Fact: B2B budget and planning cycles make Q4 just as important for service and SaaS companies, with a different playbook.
:::

## Your Q4 marketing strategy, month by month

### August and September: plan and test

- Run a Q4 planning session. Set goals by channel and agree on what "good" looks like each week.
- Build your offers: year-end contracts or annual plans for B2B, bundles and gift sets for DTC.
- Create and test ad creative and landing pages while costs are lower.
- Write the email sequences for October, November and December.
- Check your site speed, mobile experience and checkout or booking flow.
- Set up tracking. Tag every campaign link with UTM parameters (our free [UTM generator](marketing:/tools/free-utm-parameter-generator/) helps) so you can see which channel brings revenue.

### October: launch and early wins

**B2B:** review the pipeline and mark deals that can close this year. Launch year-end messaging ("go live before January"). Run account-based outreach to your top target accounts. Schedule webinars on next-year planning.

**DTC:** confirm inventory and shipping cutoff dates. Finalize Black Friday creative. Build holiday email sequences: teaser, early access, main sale, last chance. Publish gift guides and bundles.

**Everyone:** scale the ads that won in September testing, not new experiments.

### November: peak demand

**B2B:** push year-end contract offers. Retarget everyone who engaged in Q3. Run customer appreciation events.

**DTC:** run your Black Friday to Cyber Monday sequence over several days, not a single email. Increase email frequency to your engaged segments. Activate creator and partner campaigns.

**Everyone:** double down on what's working. This is not the month to test new channels. Watch cash flow, and staff support for the extra volume.

:::callout tip "Tell your ad platform the peak is coming"
If you run Google Ads with Smart Bidding, [seasonality adjustments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906) let you warn the algorithm about an expected change in conversion rate. Google recommends them for short events of 1 to 7 days, such as a Black Friday sale, not for the whole quarter.
:::

### December: close and set up Q1

**B2B:** personal outreach to every open opportunity. Executive calls on large deals. Start Q1 conversations with prospects who won't buy this year.

**DTC:** last-minute offers with clear shipping cutoffs, then "new year" messaging from December 26. Clear inventory that didn't move in November.

**Everyone:** thank customers and your team. Write down what worked while the data is fresh. Finalize Q1 plans.

## The four campaign types to run in Q4

Most startups only run urgency campaigns in Q4. You need all four running at the same time, each aimed at a different audience.

| Campaign type | Aimed at | Examples | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urgency | Warm leads ready to buy | Time-limited offers, "lock in this year's price", countdowns to a real deadline | Converting now |
| Value | Prospects still comparing | Bundles, annual plans with a bonus, free setup | Moving consideration to purchase |
| Relationship | Existing customers | VIP early access, appreciation events, personal thank-you notes | [Customer retention](marketing:/glossary/customer-retention/) and repeat sales |
| Pipeline | People who won't buy this year | Planning webinars, trend reports, Q1 preparation guides | Revenue in Q1 and Q2 |

Keep urgency honest. A countdown that resets, or "only 3 spots left" when it isn't true, costs you trust and can break consumer protection rules.

## How to set your Q4 marketing budget

Don't copy a percentage from someone else's plan. Work backwards from your target and your own numbers.

:::steps "A Q4 budget you can build on paper"
1. Set the target | How many new customers (or booked calls, or deals) do you need from Q4?
2. Find your real acquisition cost | Use your Q3 [customer acquisition cost](marketing:/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) by channel, from tracked data, not estimates.
3. Add a buffer for competition | Expect auctions to be more expensive in November. Pick a buffer you can afford and test it in September.
4. Check the payback | Make sure the higher cost still pays back within the time your cash allows.
5. Split by month | Weight spend toward the weeks your buyers actually buy, and keep a reserve for scaling winners.
:::

**Worked example (illustrative numbers, replace with yours):** you need 60 new customers in Q4. Your Q3 acquisition cost through paid ads was $150. At Q3 efficiency that's 60 × $150 = $9,000. If you plan for November auctions costing 20% more per customer, you'd need 60 × $180 = $10,800. If a customer is worth $400 in the first six months, $180 still pays back, so the plan holds. If a customer is worth $160, it doesn't, and you're better off pushing that spend to Q1.

Spending more without tracking more is how Q4 budgets disappear. Decide which numbers you'll check daily before the money goes out.

## Common Q4 mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Starting too late.** By October, competitors have tested their creative and booked partners. Plan in August, test in September.
2. **Treating Q4 as one block.** October, November and December need different goals and messages.
3. **Underestimating competition.** Ad costs, inbox competition and acquisition costs usually rise. Budget for it, or move spend to Q1.
4. **Neglecting customer support.** More customers mean more tickets. Set response-time expectations and prepare answers to common questions before November.
5. **Ignoring cash flow.** You pay for ads, stock and people before revenue clears. Model cash monthly and check payment terms.

## Q4 metrics to track

- Revenue or pipeline, week over week
- Acquisition cost by channel (does it hold as spend scales?)
- Conversion rate on key landing pages
- Average order value or deal size
- Email engagement relative to send frequency
- Site speed and checkout or booking abandonment under load
- Support response time and satisfaction
- Cash position, weekly
- For B2B: deal velocity and deals pushed to next year
- Retention of customers you won in Q4

## How to use Q4 to set up next year

The best Q4 plans finish by making Q1 easier. January is often slow: in the same Census data, US e-commerce sales in Q1 2025 were 20.3% lower than in Q4 2024 (not seasonally adjusted).

:::checklist "Q4 to Q1 bridge"
- Build the Q1 content and campaign calendar in November, while the insights are fresh
- Start partnership conversations in December that launch in January
- For B2B, push annual contracts over monthly to secure next year's revenue
- Survey Q4 customers in December about what would make them buy again
- Document what worked, what didn't, and what you'd change next Q4
- Plan next year in December, not in the second week of January
:::

:::faq
### What does Q4 mean?
Q4 is the fourth quarter of a company's fiscal year. For a business that follows the calendar year, Q4 runs from October 1 to December 31. Companies with a different fiscal year have a different Q4, so check your customers' fiscal calendars.
### Is a quarter 3 months or 4 months?
A quarter is always three months. Four quarters of three months make a 12-month year.
### When should I start planning my Q4 marketing strategy?
Start in August. Use September to test creative, offers and landing pages, and launch in early October. Starting in October means testing while ad auctions are already getting crowded.
### Should B2B companies keep marketing in December?
Yes, but shift the goal. Push open deals to close, then use the quiet weeks for relationship campaigns and Q1 pipeline building, such as planning content and conversations with prospects who will buy next year.
### How much should I increase my ad budget in Q4?
There's no universal percentage. Start from how many customers you need and your real acquisition cost, add a buffer for higher November prices, and only scale channels that still pay back at those prices.
