B2B lead generation is the system that turns the right companies into sales conversations. A working system does four things. It knows how many qualified leads the revenue target needs. It reaches buyers while they are still forming a shortlist. It makes the path from first visit to booked call obvious. And it judges every channel by the pipeline it creates, not by form fills.

Most B2B teams have the activity. What they lack is the system: a clear target, an agreed definition of a qualified lead, and channels chosen for where buyers actually are in their decision. This guide shows how to build each part.

:::takeaways
- Work back from revenue: deals needed → opportunities → qualified leads → total leads.
- Buyers decide a lot before they contact you. Lead generation has to reach them earlier than a sales call can.
- Choose channels by buying stage. Search and referrals capture demand; content, social and events create it.
- A lead isn't a lead until sales agrees it is. Write the definition down.
- Speed and relevance of follow-up decide how many leads become pipeline.
:::

Three implications for [lead generation](marketing:/glossary/lead-generation/):

1. **Be in the research.** If your company isn't in what buyers find on search, in AI answers, from peers and in their feeds, you're unlikely to be the pre-contact favorite.
2. **Serve the whole buying group.** The person who fills in your form is rarely the only decision-maker. Give them material they can forward: a one-page summary, a pricing explainer, a security answer.
3. **Relevance beats volume.** Spraying generic outreach doesn't just fail. It puts people off.

## Start with the number: work back from revenue

Before choosing channels, work out how many leads you need. Here's the calculation with illustrative inputs. Swap in your own.

- New revenue target for the year: **$600,000**
- Average first-year deal size: **$20,000**
- Deals needed: $600,000 ÷ $20,000 = **30 deals**
- Win rate from opportunity: **25%**, so opportunities needed: 30 ÷ 25% = **120**
- Qualified leads that become opportunities: **50%**, so qualified leads needed: 120 ÷ 50% = **240**
- Leads that become qualified: **30%**, so total leads needed: 240 ÷ 30% = **800 a year**, or about **67 a month**

Now you have a target you can plan against. It also shows where to look first. Raise the qualification rate from 30% to 40% and you need 600 leads instead of 800. Better targeting and better offers are often cheaper than more traffic.

Add cost to the model. If your blended [customer acquisition cost](marketing:/glossary/customer-acquisition-cost/) ceiling is $5,000 per deal, your total budget for these 30 deals is $150,000 across all channels, people and tools.

## Define a lead before you generate one

Marketing and sales arguing about lead quality is almost always a definition problem. Agree on three stages, in writing:

| Stage | Definition (example) | Who owns it | Exit rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | A contact at a company in your target segment who has shared their details | Marketing | Enriched and scored within one business day |
| Marketing-qualified lead (MQL) | Fits the ideal customer profile and has shown buying intent: demo request, pricing page visit, audit request | Marketing | Passed to sales with context within one hour |
| Sales-qualified lead (SQL) | Sales has confirmed need, timing and the person's role in the decision | Sales | Opportunity created, or returned to nurture with a reason |

The exact wording matters less than having one. Review it every quarter with sales, using real leads from the last 90 days.

## Choosing B2B lead generation channels

There's no best channel, only a best fit for your buyer, budget and timeline. Use this table to decide where to start:

| Channel | Best for | Time to first leads | Main cost | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SEO and content | Capturing buyers already researching the problem | Months; faster for decision pages | Time and expertise | Ranking for topics buyers never act on |
| Paid search | Capturing active demand right now | Days | Media spend per click | High cost per click in competitive categories |
| LinkedIn ads | Reaching specific roles and companies | Days to weeks | Media spend; creative | Cheap lead forms that sales can't use |
| Email nurture | Turning interest into conversations over time | Weeks | List building and content | Deliverability; sending without permission |
| Outbound (email, LinkedIn) | Starting conversations with named accounts | Weeks | People and data | Irrelevant messages that damage the brand |
| Partnerships and referrals | High-trust introductions | Months | Relationship time | Hard to scale on demand |
| Events and webinars | Depth with a buying group | Weeks to months | Production and promotion | Attendees who were only there to learn |

A practical rule: pair one **demand capture** channel (search, referrals) with one **demand creation** channel (content, LinkedIn, events). Capture gives you leads this quarter. Creation fills the pipeline for the quarters after.

## The path from visit to booked call

Most lead problems we see aren't traffic problems. Visitors arrive and can't work out what to do next. Fix the path before you buy more traffic.

### Offers that match the stage

Not every visitor is ready for a sales call. Give each stage a reason to raise their hand:

- **Ready to buy:** book a call, request a quote, start a trial.
- **Comparing:** a growth audit, a pricing explainer, a comparison sheet, a short assessment.
- **Learning:** a practical checklist, a template, a benchmark, a newsletter.

Lower-commitment offers bring in earlier-stage leads. That's fine, as long as they go into a nurture path and not straight to a salesperson.

### Landing pages that do one job

A [landing page](marketing:/glossary/landing-page/) should have one offer and one action. The pattern that works:

- A headline that names the outcome and who it's for.
- Three to five bullets on what they get.
- Proof: a named client, a real number, a verbatim quote.
- A short form. Ask only what sales needs for the first call; enrich the rest.
- What happens next, stated plainly: "We'll reply within one business day."

Then test it. [Conversion rate optimization](marketing:/glossary/conversion-rate-optimization/) on a page that gets real traffic is one of the cheapest ways to get more leads.

### Follow-up that's fast and specific

A form fill is a moment of intent. Reply quickly, reference what they asked for, and offer a specific next step. Route high-intent requests to a person, not a generic sequence. Everything else goes into a nurture track built for their stage.

### Nurture for leads that aren't ready

Most leads won't buy this quarter. That's normal in B2B, where buying groups are large and cycles are long. What separates a pipeline that lasts from one that dries up is what happens to those leads next.

- **Segment by stage and role.** A finance lead comparing vendors needs a different email from a marketer who downloaded a checklist.
- **Send useful things, not "just checking in."** A relevant guide, a short case story, an invitation to a small session, a pricing explainer they can forward.
- **Watch for intent signals.** Return visits to pricing, a second download, a reply. Route those to sales with context.
- **Recycle, don't discard.** SQLs that stall go back to nurture with a reason code, so sales can see why and when to try again.

## How to measure B2B lead generation

Report the same handful of numbers every month, by channel:

| Metric | What it tells you | Healthy direction |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | Volume of interest | Steady or rising, but never the headline |
| Lead-to-qualified rate | Targeting and offer fit | Rising |
| Cost per qualified lead | Channel efficiency | Falling or stable as volume grows |
| Opportunities and pipeline value | Real sales impact | Rising toward the target from your revenue math |
| Win rate and sales cycle by source | Lead quality after handoff | Win rate up, cycle stable or shorter |

If leads rise but qualified leads don't, fix targeting and offers before spending more.

## Lead generation myths

:::myth-fact
Myth: More leads means more pipeline.
Fact: Only qualified leads become pipeline. Doubling unqualified leads doubles the work for sales and can lower win rates. Track qualified leads and opportunities by channel.
Myth: Gated content is dead.
Fact: Gating works when the asset is worth an email address and the lead goes into a relevant nurture path. It fails when a thin PDF goes straight to a sales call.
Myth: Outbound alone can fill the pipeline.
Fact: Gartner found 73% of B2B buyers actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach. Outbound works best when it's targeted and when buyers can find proof about you elsewhere.
Myth: The lowest cost per lead is the best channel.
Fact: A $40 lead that never qualifies costs more than a $200 lead that becomes an opportunity. Compare cost per qualified lead and cost per opportunity.
:::

## Build your lead generation system in 30 days

:::steps "A 30-day setup"
1. Set the number | Work back from revenue to monthly qualified leads, as in the example above.
2. Write the definitions | Agree lead, MQL and SQL with sales, including handoff times.
3. Audit the path | Check every offer, landing page and form. Remove fields sales doesn't use. Add proof.
4. Pick two channels | One to capture demand, one to create it. Set a budget and a 90-day test window.
5. Wire up tracking | Record source, landing page and campaign on every lead in the CRM. Pass qualified stages back to ad platforms.
6. Build the follow-up | Fast routing for high-intent leads; a stage-matched nurture track for the rest.
7. Review weekly, decide monthly | Look at qualified leads and opportunities by channel. Shift budget toward what produces pipeline.
:::

:::faq
### What is B2B lead generation?
B2B lead generation is the process of attracting companies that fit your ideal customer profile and turning them into sales conversations. It covers the channels that reach buyers, the offers and landing pages that capture interest, the follow-up that qualifies it, and the measurement that shows which activity produces pipeline.
### What are the best B2B lead generation strategies?
The best strategy pairs one channel that captures existing demand, such as search or referrals, with one that creates future demand, such as content, LinkedIn or events. Back both with clear offers, fast follow-up and an agreed definition of a qualified lead. The right mix depends on your deal size, sales cycle and budget.
### How many leads do I need?
Work back from your revenue target. Divide it by your average deal size to get deals, then by your win rate to get opportunities, then by your lead-to-opportunity rates. For example, $600,000 at $20,000 per deal with a 25% win rate needs 120 opportunities.
### What's the difference between an MQL and an SQL?
A marketing-qualified lead fits your target profile and has shown buying intent, such as requesting a demo. A sales-qualified lead has been confirmed by sales as having a real need, timing and a role in the decision. Write both definitions down and review them with sales every quarter.
### How long does B2B lead generation take to work?
Paid search and LinkedIn can produce leads within days, but tuning them for quality takes a few months. SEO and content usually take several months to build momentum. Plan a 90-day test per channel and judge it by qualified leads and opportunities, not by week-one results.
### Should B2B companies use gated content?
Yes, when the asset is useful enough to earn an email address and the lead goes into a nurture path matched to its stage. Keep top-of-funnel education ungated so buyers and AI search tools can find it, and gate deeper tools, templates and assessments.
:::

## The short version

B2B lead generation works when it's a system: a revenue-based target, agreed definitions, channels matched to buying stages, a clear path from visit to call, and pipeline as the scorecard. Fix the path before you buy more traffic, and give each channel a fair 90-day test. If you want help finding the leaks, start with a [growth audit](marketing:/growth-audit/).
