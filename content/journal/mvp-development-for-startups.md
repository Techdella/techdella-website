MVP development for startups is the work of turning a validated idea into the smallest product real users can rely on, then using what they do to decide what to build next. A focused MVP usually takes three to four weeks of discovery and prototyping, followed by roughly two to three months of build, testing and launch. Cost follows scope and team. As a reference point, Techdella Studios' MVP Discovery & Prototype starts from {{price:mvp_discovery}} and an MVP Build starts from {{price:mvp_build}}.

This guide covers the realistic timeline, what drives cost up or down, how the main team options compare, and the process we follow from discovery to iteration.

:::takeaways
- Plan **two phases**: discovery and prototype first, then the build. Skipping discovery is the most common cause of expensive rebuilds.
- Cost is roughly **people × weeks**. Cut scope and you cut both.
- Integrations, payments, compliance and multiple user roles are the biggest cost multipliers.
- Budget for **after launch** too: fixes, analytics and the first round of changes usually come within weeks.
:::

## What MVP development includes

A real MVP build is more than code. Expect these pieces, in roughly this order:

- **Discovery:** the problem, the first users, the riskiest assumption and what success looks like.
- **Scope:** a short list of must-have features and a written "later" list.
- **UX and UI design:** user flows, wireframes and the screens for the core path.
- **Architecture and setup:** data model, hosting, authentication and environments.
- **Build:** the core workflow, an admin view, integrations and analytics.
- **QA:** testing the core flows on the devices your users actually use.
- **Launch and feedback loop:** analytics, onboarding, a way to collect feedback and a post-launch plan.

If you're still deciding *what* to build, start with [how to develop an MVP](/how-to-develop-an-mvp-for-startup/), which covers assumptions, MVP types and cutting scope.

## MVP development timeline: how long does it take?

The timeline below is how a typical coded web MVP runs at Techdella Studios: a discovery and prototype phase, then a build phase. Your project can be shorter if the scope is small, or longer with complex integrations or compliance work.

| Phase | Typical length | What you get at the end |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery & scope | Week 1 | Problem statement, first users, riskiest assumption, must-have list, success metric |
| UX flows & prototype | Weeks 2–4 | Clickable prototype of the core path, tested with target users |
| Architecture & setup | First 1–2 weeks of build | Data model, environments, authentication, deployment pipeline |
| Core build | Most of the build phase | Working core workflow, admin view, integrations, analytics |
| QA & fixes | Final 2 weeks of build | Tested core flows, performance checks, launch checklist |
| Launch & learn | Ongoing | Live MVP, analytics dashboard, feedback loop, next-version plan |

Discovery & Prototype runs 3–4 weeks. The MVP Build runs 8–12 weeks after discovery. End to end, most focused MVPs land between about 11 and 16 weeks.

### What makes MVP timelines slip

- **Scope added mid-build.** Every "small" addition means design, code, testing and a moved launch date.
- **Slow decisions.** A week waiting for feedback on screens is a week added to the end.
- **Unknown integrations.** Payment providers, identity checks and third-party APIs have their own approval processes and quirks.
- **No content ready.** Copy, legal pages and onboarding text are often left to the last week, then delay launch.

## MVP development cost: how much should you budget?

There's no honest single number, because cost depends on what you build and who builds it. What you can do is understand the arithmetic.

**Cost ≈ number of people × weeks × weekly rate.** Scope drives weeks; complexity drives how many specialists you need.

A worked example with illustrative numbers: a team of 3 (designer, two developers) for 10 weeks is 30 person-weeks. At an illustrative blended rate of $1,000 per person-week, that's $30,000. Cut the scope so the same team needs 6 weeks, and it becomes 18 person-weeks, or $18,000. Remove one integration that needed a specialist for 2 weeks, and you save another 2 person-weeks. Rates vary widely by country and seniority, so plug in your own; the point is that **scope is the lever you control.**

### What drives MVP cost up

- **Features and user roles.** Each role (customer, provider, admin) multiplies screens, permissions and tests.
- **Payments and money movement.** Wallets, payouts, refunds and reconciliation take far longer than a checkout button.
- **Compliance.** Fintech, health and identity products carry legal and security work that can't be skipped.
- **Integrations.** Each third-party API adds setup, error handling and testing.
- **Native mobile apps.** Two app stores, device testing and review cycles cost more than a mobile web app.
- **Custom design.** Complex animation, charts and bespoke components take longer than a clean, simple system.

## Who should build your MVP? Team options compared

| Option | Best when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| No-code or AI builders | You need a demand or usability test fast and cheaply | Limits on scale, security and data ownership; see [what vibe coding can and can't do](/what-is-vibe-coding/) |
| Freelancers | You have a clear spec and time to manage them | You become the project manager; gaps between design, build and QA |
| Agency or studio | You want strategy, design, build and QA in one team with a fixed scope | Pick one that cuts scope with you, not one that sells more features |
| In-house team | The product is your core technology and you have funding | Hiring takes months and is expensive before product-market fit |
| Technical co-founder | You can find one who shares the risk | Takes time to find; equity is the price |

Many founders mix these: a studio for discovery and the first build, then hire in-house once the product is proving itself.

:::myth-fact
Myth: The cheapest quote is the cheapest MVP.
Fact: An MVP that has to be rebuilt costs you twice, plus the months lost. Compare quotes on scope, testing and who owns the code, not just the headline price.
Myth: You should build every feature competitors have so users take you seriously.
Fact: Early users switch for one thing done well. Copying a full competitor feature list multiplies cost and delays the evidence you need.
:::

## The MVP development process step by step

:::steps "How Techdella Studios runs an MVP"
1. Validate the problem | Talk to target users about their current workaround and what it costs them. Ask about past behavior, not opinions.
2. Define one core user and outcome | Pick a single user segment and the one result they come for, such as booking a service or sending an invoice.
3. Prioritize features | Sort into must-have, should-have and later. Treat anything not needed for the core outcome as later.
4. Prototype and test | Build a clickable prototype of the core path and watch target users try it before any code.
5. Build in short sprints | Build only the approved scope, demo progress weekly, and log new ideas for the next version.
6. Launch, measure and improve | Ship to a small group with analytics, track activation, retention and drop-off points, then plan the next version from evidence.
:::

## Are you ready to start MVP development?

:::checklist "Ready-to-build check"
- You've spoken to at least 10 people in your target segment about the problem
- You can describe the first user in one sentence
- You know the riskiest assumption the MVP must test
- You can name the one outcome users come for
- You have a written must-have list and a "later" list
- You have budget for the build and for changes after launch
- You have a plan to reach the first 20–50 users
:::

If several of these are missing, spend the next few weeks on validation before you spend on development.

### How much does MVP development cost for a startup?
It depends on scope and team: cost is roughly people × weeks × rate. At Techdella Studios, MVP Discovery & Prototype starts from {{price:mvp_discovery}} and an MVP Build starts from {{price:mvp_build}}. Payments, compliance, integrations and native apps push costs up; a tight scope brings them down.
### How long does it take to develop an MVP?
A focused coded MVP typically needs 3–4 weeks of discovery and prototyping, then 8–12 weeks of build, testing and launch. Simple demand tests such as a landing page can run in days; regulated or integration-heavy products take longer.
### What should be included in an MVP?
Only what the core user needs to get the core result: usually sign-up, the main workflow, a basic admin view, analytics and a way to collect feedback. Everything else goes on a written "later" list.
### Is it better to hire freelancers or an agency for an MVP?
Freelancers can cost less if you have a clear spec and time to coordinate them. A studio or agency costs more per week but brings strategy, design, build and QA together under one scope. Choose based on how much management time you have.
### What is the difference between a prototype and an MVP?
A prototype shows how the product might work, usually without real data. An MVP is used by real people to get a real result. At Studios the prototype comes first, in discovery, so the MVP build is based on tested flows.
