A hashtag generator turns a short description of your post into a set of relevant hashtags. Type the topic, pick LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok or X, and this tool returns a suggested set sized for that platform, plus every tag it built from your words and up to 20 related tags. It's free, it runs in your browser, and it asks for your email once to show results.

It's made for founders and small B2B teams who post regularly and want tags that describe the post, not a list of the most popular words on the platform.

## How the hashtag generator works

The tool doesn't call an AI model or scrape a platform. It follows a fixed, predictable process, so you can see why each tag appears.

**1. It builds tags from your words.** The topic is lowercased, punctuation is removed, and filler words (`and`, `for`, `the`, `of`, `to`, `in`, `on`, `with`, `my`, `our`, `your`, `a`, `an`) are dropped. From the words left, it makes:

- one tag from the first three words joined in CamelCase,
- one tag for each word longer than two letters,
- one tag from the last two words, if there's more than one word,
- one "tips" tag from the first two words.

**2. It finds related tags.** It compares your words with 23 themed lists: marketing, SEO, AI, AI search, ads, email, social, leads, startup, SaaS, fintech, ecommerce, design, tech, Africa, business, health, food, travel, fashion, education, gaming and real estate. Each list has trigger words. A list matches when one of your words equals a trigger, or when a trigger longer than three letters is the start of your word (or the other way round). Tags from lists with more matching words rank higher, and up to 20 related tags are kept.

**3. It sizes the set for the platform.** The suggested set takes tags from your own words first, up to half the set, then fills the rest with the top related tags.

| Platform | Suggested set | Tool's guidance |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 5 | A few specific tags at the end of the post |
| Instagram | 5 | The most specific tags, within Instagram's five-hashtag cap |
| TikTok | 6 | One or two broad tags plus specific ones about the video |
| X | 3 | One to three tags keep the post readable |

These set sizes are our editorial defaults, not platform rules. The one platform limit we know has changed is Instagram's: [Social Media Today reported](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/) on 18 December 2025 that Instagram's @creators account announced it would "gradually update the number of hashtags that you can include in a caption for a reel or post to five." The tool's Instagram set follows that cap: five tags, the most specific first.

If the Related list says there are no related tags, none of your words matched a theme. Add one broader word, like "marketing", "startup" or "fintech", and run it again.

## A worked example

Topic: **fintech product launch in Lagos**. Platform: **LinkedIn**.

**Step 1: your words.** The tool drops `in`, leaving `fintech`, `product`, `launch`, `lagos`. It builds seven tags:

`#FintechProductLaunch` `#Fintech` `#Product` `#Launch` `#Lagos` `#LaunchLagos` `#FintechProductTips`

**Step 2: related tags.** Four themes match, one word each: `fintech` matches the fintech list, `launch` the startup list, `product` the SaaS list and `lagos` the Africa list. Each list's first tag scores 10, the second 9, and so on. Tags already made from your words are removed. The top of the Related list reads `#Startup`, `#SaaS`, `#AfricaTech`, `#StartupLife`, `#B2BSaaS`, `#Payments`, `#NigeriaTech`.

**Step 3: the set.** LinkedIn gets five tags. Half of five, rounded up, is three, so the first three of your own tags go in: `#FintechProductLaunch`, `#Fintech`, `#Product`. The remaining two slots go to the top related tags: `#Startup` and `#SaaS`.

Suggested set: `#FintechProductLaunch #Fintech #Product #Startup #SaaS`

**Step 4: edit.** This is where your judgment matters. `#Product` is too broad to mean anything. `#SaaS` is there only because the word "product" triggers the SaaS list; if the launch is a payments app, it doesn't fit. The place is missing, even though Lagos is the point. A better LinkedIn set:

`#Fintech #FintechProductLaunch #LagosStartups #NigeriaTech #Payments`

That's one tag for the topic, one for the event, two for the place and audience, and one for the category. Every one of them came from the tool's lists; the editing took a minute.

## Hashtag best practices by platform

:::checklist "Before you post"
- Use tags that describe this post, not tags that are merely popular.
- Mix one or two broad tags with specific ones about the topic, audience or place.
- Put tags at the end of the post, not in the middle of sentences.
- Write multi-word tags in CamelCase (`#LagosStartups`). They're easier to read, for people and for screen readers.
- Keep one branded or series tag and reuse it, so people can follow the series.
- Check the platform's current limit, especially on Instagram.
:::

**LinkedIn.** Posts are read in the feed, so a block of ten tags looks like spam to the people you want to reach. Three to five tags at the end work well. If you post a weekly series, give it its own tag and use it every week.

**Instagram.** Trim to five. With fewer slots, every tag needs to describe the post precisely, so drop anything generic first. Don't try to get around the cap by moving extra tags into a comment; the point of the change is fewer, more relevant tags.

**TikTok.** One or two broad tags plus a few that describe what happens in the video. Your on-screen text and spoken words describe the video too, so the tags don't have to do all the work.

**X.** One to three tags. Posts are short, and tags eat into the space for the message.

## Common hashtag mistakes

:::myth-fact
Myth: More hashtags mean more reach.
Fact: A few precise tags work better. Instagram is cutting captions to five tags, and long lists read as spam to the people scrolling past.
Myth: The most popular hashtags are the best ones to use.
Fact: A broad tag puts your post among millions of others. Specific tags reach fewer people who are more likely to care about the post.
Myth: A hashtag generator's output is ready to paste.
Fact: Matching on words can't judge meaning. Always remove tags that don't describe the post before you copy the set.
:::

Two more we see often:

- **Tagging every post with the same block.** Platforms and people both notice. Rotate tags to match each post's actual topic.
- **Using a tag you haven't checked.** Before you adopt a new branded or series tag, search it on the platform. If it already belongs to an unrelated community, pick another.
