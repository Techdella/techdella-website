The AI Business Name Generator turns a short brief into up to 10 distinct name ideas, each with a style label and a one-line reason it fits. Tell it your industry, what the business does, words you like, a tone and words to avoid. It is free; the first time you use it, we ask for your email to show the results.

**The names are AI-generated.** The model can't check trademark registers, company registries or domains, so any name it suggests may already belong to someone. Use the list to start your shortlist, then check every name before you choose.

It is for founders naming a new company, product or side project who want more angles than a blank page gives them.

## How the AI business name generator works

You fill in two required fields and two optional ones:

| Field | Required | Limit | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Industry | Yes | 120 characters | Be specific: "cross-border payments", not "finance" |
| What the business does, plus words you like | Yes | 300 characters | Who you serve, the outcome, and 2 to 4 words you like |
| Tone | No | Pick one | Any, modern, playful, serious, technical or premium |
| Words to avoid | No | 160 characters | Competitor names, overused words in your category |

Our server sends your brief to an AI model with fixed instructions. The model is asked for exactly 10 distinct, pronounceable names of 1 to 3 words and at most 24 characters, with no domain extension, and a mix of styles. It is told not to suggest the names of well-known companies or obvious trademarks. For each name it gives:

- **The name.**
- **A style badge**: invented, compound, descriptive, metaphor or acronym.
- **Why it fits**: one sentence of up to 20 words.

Our server removes duplicates and anything malformed, so you may see fewer than 10. A note under the results reminds you that the names are AI-generated and need checking. You can copy the whole list with one click. Each visitor can run the AI tools 10 times a day.

## How to read your results

There is no score. Use the style badge to see what kind of name each one is, and what that means for you.

| Style | Example shape | Strength | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invented | A new word | Distinctive, often easier to protect | Needs explaining; spelling can be hard |
| Compound | Two words joined | Memorable, can hint at what you do | Popular pairs are often taken |
| Descriptive | Says what you do | Clear on day one | Hard to protect; can box you in |
| Metaphor | Borrows an image | Room for a story | The link may not be obvious to buyers |
| Acronym | Initials | Short | Forgettable without a known full name |

## A worked example

Brief: industry **fintech**, business **cross-border payments for freelancers**, words liked **flow, bridge, pay**, tone **modern**.

You'll get a mix of styles: some compounds built from your words, some invented words, a descriptive option or two, and metaphors. Your list will differ each time you run it.

Say you shortlist three. Score each one from 0 to 2 on five tests, for a maximum of 10:

| Test | Name A | Name B | Name C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spelled correctly by 5 people after hearing it | 2 (5 of 5) | 1 (3 of 5) | 2 (4 of 5) |
| No live trademark conflict in your markets | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Usable .com or main local domain | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Social handles free on the 2 networks you use | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| Works in every market's main language | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| **Total** | **9** | **8** | **8** |

For the spelling test we gave 2 points for 4 or 5 correct spellings, 1 for 3, and 0 for 2 or fewer. Name C scores well everywhere except trademarks, and a conflict there rules it out whatever the total. Name A leads at 9 out of 10, with a domain that needs a modifier. That is a typical outcome: the best-sounding name isn't always the one you can use.

## How to check a name before you use it

:::steps "From shortlist to safe choice"
1. Search the open web | Search the name with your industry and country. If a similar business shows up, move on.
2. Search the trademark registers | Use the official register in each market, such as [USPTO Trademark Search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) in the US, or the [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://branddb.wipo.int/) to search many national registers at once.
3. Check your company registry | Make sure you can register the company name where you will incorporate.
4. Check domains and handles | The .com or your main local domain, plus the social networks you will actually use.
5. Say it and spell it | Read it aloud to five people and ask them to spell it back. Check what it means in each market's main language.
6. Get legal advice | Before you spend on a logo, signage or ads, ask a trademark professional to confirm the name is clear to use.
:::

## Best practices

- **Brief the tool like a person.** "Payments for freelancers who bill clients abroad; words: flow, bridge" gives the model something to work with. "Fintech app" doesn't.
- **Run it more than once.** Change the tone or the words you like between runs, so each list explores a different direction.
- **Don't fall for the first name.** Shortlist, sleep on it, then test.
- **Think beyond the first product.** A name that only fits your first feature can be hard to stretch later.
- **Plan for how people will find you.** A distinctive name is easier to search and helps [brand awareness](/glossary/brand-awareness/) build faster than a generic phrase that matches thousands of pages.

## Common mistakes

:::myth-fact
Myth: If the AI suggested it, the name must be original and free to use.
Fact: The model can't search trademark registers or the web from this tool. It may suggest a name someone already uses or owns. Only an official register search, plus legal advice, tells you a name is clear.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: Owning the domain means you own the name.
Fact: A domain registration and a trademark are different things. You can own a domain and still be asked to stop using a name that infringes someone's trademark. Check both.
:::

:::myth-fact
Myth: The best business names describe exactly what you do.
Fact: Descriptive names are clear but often hard to register as trademarks and easy to confuse with competitors. Many strong brands use invented or metaphor names and explain what they do in the tagline.
:::

Other mistakes we see often:

- **Checking only one market.** If you sell in the US, UK and Nigeria, search all three.
- **Choosing a name that only works in one language.** Say it in each market before you choose.
- **Skipping the spelling test.** If people can't spell it after hearing it, they can't search for it.

## What the tool can't do

It can't check trademarks, company registries, domains or social handles. It doesn't know your competitors unless you name them as words to avoid. It can't tell you whether a name carries an unwanted meaning in every language. Use it for range and speed, then do the checks that protect you.
