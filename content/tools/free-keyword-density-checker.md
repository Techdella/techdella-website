## What keyword density tells you, and what it doesn't

Keyword density is how often a word appears compared with the total word count. It used to be treated as a ranking lever. It isn't one now. Search engines look at whether a page answers the query well, not at a percentage.

The check is still useful in two ways: it catches stuffing (the same phrase over and over), and it shows gaps (the topic you meant to cover isn't in your top phrases).

## A worked example

A 900-word page targeting "b2b seo services" uses the exact phrase 14 times. That's very high for a phrase of that length, and it shows in the copy: several sentences exist only to fit the keyword in. We'd cut it to the title, the H1, the intro and a few natural mentions, then add related terms like "technical SEO", "content strategy" and "link building" where they fit.

## Where to put your keyword

- The title tag and H1.
- The first two or three sentences.
- One or two subheadings, if it reads naturally.
- Image alt text only when the image actually shows it.
